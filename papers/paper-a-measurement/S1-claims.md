# Paper A, section 1 -- What this paper claims, and what it does not

**Status.** Draft, 2026-08-20. Drafted after section 3, per the committed drafting order. Nothing here is a result; the claims are the objects the rest of the paper defends, and each is stated with the strongest published objection attached, because a claim whose opposition is not engaged where the claim is made is not being tested.

---

## 1.1 Four claims

**C1. Techno-industrial capability should be reported as a small vector with explicit posterior uncertainty, not as a scalar composite.** The strongest objection is not that composites are defensible in principle but that they have receipts: scalar measures have demonstrated out-of-sample predictive validity on conflict outcomes -- Beckley's two-variable product predicts 78 percent of wars over 1816--2010 against CINC's 70 ([Beckley](https://scispace.com/pdf/the-power-of-nations-measuring-what-matters-441eqyutp6.pdf)) -- and a vector has none. Declining to aggregate declines to answer the question the field asks. The paper's answer is confined to section 3 and is deliberately narrow: the aggregation step destroys the specific information this research programme is about, namely which domain is moving in which direction. It is not a claim that the vector predicts anything better.

**C2. The scalar-versus-vector choice is empirically consequential, not stylistic.** The sign of relative US--PRC movement is dimension-dependent within the same years: over 2015--2025 the deployment dimensions and the frontier dimensions move in opposite directions, so any scalar reports the sign of its own weights. The objection: if component series are highly correlated, aggregation is nearly lossless and the whole dispute is academic. The objection is correct as a conditional, and whether the antecedent holds is an empirical question about the estimated posterior -- it belongs to Paper B and is not settled here. What this paper shows is only that in the observed decade the correlation was not high enough for the choice to be free.

**C3. Definitional non-comparability between two states' published statistics should enter as measurement uncertainty, not be absorbed into a ratio.** The concrete instance is the dispatchable-capacity rule (section 4.4), which refuses to emit a cross-state ratio because US capacity factors and PRC utilisation hours are computed from differently scoped statistics. The objection is the serious one: this inflates intervals to the point of vacuity, and an interval wide enough to contain every hypothesis has no content. The paper's answer is to report the interval and let the reader judge whether it is vacuous, rather than to narrow it by a comparability assumption neither statistical office will underwrite.

**C4. A long-horizon claim can be pre-registered even though its data are observational.** The objections to pre-analysis plans in observational settings are extensive and three of them apply to this project specifically (section 5.2 gives them at full strength). The load-bearing distinction is Monogan's own classification: registration of analyses over existing data cannot exclude prior glimpsing, but a forecast whose outcome does not yet exist is immune to the glimpsing objection by construction ([Monogan](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/ps-political-science-and-politics/article/research-preregistration-in-political-science-the-case-counterarguments-and-a-response-to-critiques/E80124ED16BA47D0EA09F03D72B89EC7)). The 2026 baseline does not escape the objection; the 2030--2050 conditions do.

## 1.2 What the paper does not claim

Stated here so that no reader has to reconstruct it from silences.

- It does **not** claim the vector predicts conflict, transition, or anything else better than a scalar index. It makes no predictive comparison, and section 5.6 reproduces the strongest available evidence that expert forecasting in social science underperforms naive benchmarks -- evidence against the enterprise this programme is engaged in, placed in the methods section rather than a closing limitations paragraph.
- It does **not** claim pre-registration improves accuracy. The claim is auditability: registration "does not favor prediction over postdiction; its purpose is to make clear which is which" ([Nosek et al., PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1708274114)).
- It does **not** claim the measurement model is identified in every parameter. Section 6 names the parameters it is not identified in, per quantity, following the disciplinary norm set by the latent-measurement literature it joins.
- It makes **no claim about which state is stronger**. The paper's position is that the question is not well posed as asked, and section 9 poses -- without answering -- the question of whether that refusal makes the framework unusable for the field that asked.

## 1.3 The paper's value if the thesis is wrong

The programme's substantive thesis (Paper B's object) may be wrong, and the falsification apparatus is designed to detect that. This paper is written so that its value survives that outcome: the measurement architecture, the identification statements, the pre-registration template for future-dated conditions, and the published record of the apparatus's own failures (sections 5.6 and 7) are each usable by a reader who rejects every substantive proposition attached to them. A methods paper that is only worth reading if its authors' hypothesis survives is not a methods paper.
