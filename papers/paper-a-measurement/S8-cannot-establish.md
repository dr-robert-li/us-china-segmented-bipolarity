# Paper A, section 8 -- What this paper cannot establish

**Status.** Draft, 2026-08-20.

---

Stated here so that it need not be discovered by a reader, and so that no later citation of this paper can honestly attribute to it a claim from this list.

**It cannot show the vector predicts anything better than a scalar index**, because it makes no predictive comparison. The best predictive record in the power-measurement literature belongs to a two-variable scalar (section 2.3), and nothing in this paper disturbs that. Whether a predictive comparison should be constructed, and against what benchmark, is section 9's question, not this paper's finding.

**It cannot rule out that the four elements are correlated tightly enough that aggregation would lose little.** Claim C2 shows the correlation was imperfect over 2015--2025 -- the sign of relative movement was dimension-dependent -- but "imperfect over one decade" is not "low enough to matter over the estimation window". That is an empirical question about the estimated posterior, and it belongs to Paper B. If the posterior returns tightly correlated elements, C1 survives as a reporting principle but loses most of its practical force, and the honest report will say so.

**It cannot verify that no author glimpsed the historical data before the 2026 baseline was registered.** The objection is unanswerable in principle for the baseline year: the registration uses published historical series, and no procedural device can demonstrate the absence of prior acquaintance with published numbers. Only the future-dated conditions escape the objection, by construction. The calibration exercises in section 5.5 inherit the same limitation and disclose it pair by pair: one dry-run pair was registered blind; the others were characterisation exercises run after reconnaissance, and are labelled as the weaker evidence they are.

**It cannot claim pre-registration improved this project's accuracy**, and on the available evidence should not. The claim defended in section 5 is auditability -- which statements were predictions -- and section 5.6's forecasting-tournament evidence is a standing reason to expect the predictions themselves to be no better than naive benchmarks. The apparatus makes failure detectable; it does not make failure less likely.

**It cannot prevent the companion paper from being prior-dominated**, and does not claim otherwise. Section 6.3's contingency has a stated consequence: if the mechanism parameter is barely learned, the long-horizon regime posterior inherits the priors, and the honest description of Paper B becomes a prior sensitivity analysis whose empirical core is confined to the anchored, well-identified quantities. The contraction gate makes that outcome detectable and reportable; nothing can make it avoidable.
