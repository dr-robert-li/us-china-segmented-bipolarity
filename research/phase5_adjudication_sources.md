**Evidentiary conventions used throughout.** Every value carries a markdown link to the page it was taken from. Three tiers are distinguished explicitly:

- **Officially announced** — stated by a PRC state organ (Xinhua, gov.cn, Ministry of National Defense, NPC) or a US official body (CBO, Federal Reserve, OFR, Treasury, CRS).

## A3. Seated count of full Politburo members as of 1 January 2026

Arithmetic, on the A1 baseline and the A2 dates, counting only removals whose official announcement fell **on or before 1 January 2026**:

```
Full Politburo members elected 24 Oct 2022                      = 24
Less: He Weidong, expelled 17 Oct 2025 (confirmed 23 Oct 2025)  = -1
Less: Zhang Youxia (investigation announced 24 Jan 2026)        =  0   [after the cut-off]
Less: Ma Xingrui (investigation 3 Apr 2026; expelled 14 Jul 2026) = 0   [after the cut-off]
Plus: replacements elected between Congresses                    = +0
                                                                 ----
Seated full Politburo members as at 1 Jan 2026                   = 23
```

**Were any replacements elected? No.** The Party does not add Politburo members between Congresses, and the Fourth Plenum communique bears this out directly. The plenum filled Central Committee vacancies and altered the CMC, but made no Politburo addition: "Zhang Shengmin was added to the Central Military Commission as Vice Chairman," and eleven alternate members of the Central Committee were promoted to full membership (Yu Huiwen, Ma Hancheng, Wang Jian, Wang Xi, Wang Yonghong, Wang Tingkai, Wang Xinwei, Wei Tao, Deng Yiwu, Deng Xiuming, Lu Hong) ([gov.cn, 23 Oct 2025](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202510/23/content_WS68fa3825c6d00ca5f9a06fbe.html)). Zhang Shengmin was promoted **within the CMC only**; the page records no Politburo elevation. CRS likewise records only alternate-to-full Central Committee promotions: "the Central Committee has lost 14 members (12 expelled, 1 resigned, 1 died) and promoted 14 alternate members to fill its vacancies," while for the Politburo it records losses and no additions ([CRS IF12505, data as of 27 Apr 2026](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/HTML/IF12505.html)).

**Independent corroboration of the 23 figure.** Reuters, reporting the CPPCC opening on 4 March 2026, refers to "the 23-member Politburo" ([Reuters, 4 Mar 2026](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/two-senior-communist-party-officials-absent-top-chinese-political-event-2026-03-04/)). This matches `24 - 1 = 23` and implies that as of March 2026 Zhang Youxia was still nominally counted.

**Later state of the count (outside the 1 January 2026 question, recorded for completeness).** After Ma Xingrui's expulsion, `24 - 2 = 22` on the official record (He Weidong and Ma Xingrui expelled; Zhang Youxia under investigation but not officially expelled). The NYT instead reports 21, treating Zhang as expelled ([NYT, 14 July 2026](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/07/14/world/asia/china-expels-politburo-official-sex-corruption.html)). The discrepancy is entirely attributable to Zhang's contested status; it is flagged rather than resolved.

## A5. Scholarly and expert assessment, 2023–2026: instability versus consolidating discipline

### A5.1 The instability / succession-crisis case, at full strength

**Matthew Johnson, "Late Stalinism in Beijing," *China Brief* Vol. 26 Issue 3, Jamestown Foundation, 26 January 2026.** Standing: Jamestown Foundation is a Washington research institution; *China Brief* is its peer-reviewed-adjacent analytical publication with named authorship and editorial review, not a news outlet.

> "General Secretary Xi Jinping appears to have prevailed in the latest round of military purges in the Central Military Commission (CMC), but only by further dismantling the institutional safeguards that once stabilized elite politics—deepening, rather than resolving, long-term regime fragility."

> "This consolidation does not alter near-term timelines on Taiwan, but it accelerates political drift in the People's Republic of China toward a late Stalinist disequilibrium in which Xi's personal control is maximized at the cost of orderly succession management, professional and expertise-based authority—exemplified by figures like Zhang Youxia—and tolerance for dissenting or corrective views within the leadership."

> "On the one hand, there was no visible indication that Xi's personal authority had meaningfully eroded. On the other, history shows that the personalization of power he has engineered carries an unavoidable cost: systems governed more by diktat than by institutions are inherently unstable."

> "These persistent signs of instability within the system mean that a transition toward fragmentation and elite realignment cannot be ruled out."

> "There are no independent institutions of appeal. There are no durable procedures for succession. There is only the leader and the system he holds together."

> "Xi has solved the problem of control. But he has also ensured that when the center fails, whether through succession crisis or elite fracture, it is primed to fail catastrophically."

All quotations from [Jamestown, 26 Jan 2026](https://jamestown.org/late-stalinism-in-beijing/). The article situates itself against an earlier piece, "Terminal Authority: Assessing the CCP's Emerging Crisis of Political Succession," *China Brief*, 26 July 2025.

**"Why Xi Jinping Is Purging China's Top Leaders," *Journal of Democracy* online exclusive, 25 March 2026.** Standing: the *Journal of Democracy* is a peer-reviewed journal published by Johns Hopkins University Press for the National Endowment for Democracy; online exclusives are editor-commissioned rather than peer-reviewed. **Authorship is uncertain** — see Gaps and failures.

> "By systematically purging all capable and prestigious potential heirs, Xi is engineering a monumental succession crisis."

> "His eventual departure will leave a profound power vacuum, triggering a fierce struggle for survival among those who remain."

> "Yet, while Xi's power remains unassailable, his prestige is inexorably drawing closer to a 'Lin Biao moment.'"

## A7. Xi Jinping succession arrangements and the 2018 abolition of presidential term limits

### B2(ii) Emergency Federal Reserve intervention in the Treasury market aimed at market functioning

**Finding: no emergency intervention occurred. Everything in the record is either routine standing-facility usage, a scheduled balance-sheet policy change, or reserve-management purchases within the FOMC's pre-announced framework.** The four categories the brief asks to be kept apart are kept apart below.

**1. Emergency intervention: none found.** No Section 13(3) facility, no unscheduled large-scale Treasury purchase programme for market-functioning purposes, and no emergency FOMC action on the Treasury market was located for January 2024 – August 2026. The NY Fed's own review of April 2025 explicitly contrasts it with March 2020: "it was nowhere close to what happened in March 2020" ([Roberto Perli, NY Fed, 9 May 2025](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/speeches/2025/per250509)).

**2. Routine open-market operations and the end of runoff — a scheduled policy change, not an emergency.** At its 28–29 October 2025 meeting the FOMC decided to end balance-sheet runoff effective 1 December 2025. Official wording: "Consistent with its Plans and money market conditions suggesting that reserves were approaching ample levels, the Committee judged it appropriate to conclude balance sheet runoff as of December 1, 2025" ([Federal Reserve Board, policy normalization Q&A](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/policy-normalization-qa.htm)). The Board's normalization page records the cumulative scale: "Since balance sheet reduction began in June 2022, the Federal Reserve's total securities holdings declined by more than $2.2 trillion, with redemptions of about $1.6 trillion in Treasury securities and $600 billion in agency mortgage-backed securities (MBS)" ([Federal Reserve Board](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/policy-normalization.htm)).

The stated rationale is money-market tightness, not Treasury-market dysfunction. The October FOMC minutes: "Rates on Treasury repurchase agreements (repo) moved notably higher relative to the interest rate on reserve balances (IORB). Investors attributed this movement to a decline in available liquidity amid ongoing balance sheet runoff and continued large Treasury debt issuance" ([FOMC minutes, 28–29 Oct 2025](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcminutes20251029.htm)). Powell: "Our long-stated plan has been to stop balance sheet runoff when reserves are somewhat above the level we judge consistent with ample reserve conditions … Signs have clearly emerged that we have reached that standard in money markets" ([Reuters, 29 Oct 2025](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/fed-end-balance-sheet-reduction-december-1-2025-10-29/)).

**3. Reserve management purchases — framework-based, begun December 2025.** At the December 2025 meeting the Committee judged reserves had reached ample and directed purchases: "when it judged that reserves declined to the ample range in December, it instructed the Desk to begin reserve management purchases (RMPs) to maintain reserves within that range" ([Roberto Perli, NY Fed, 26 Mar 2026](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/speeches/2026/per260326)). The December directive: "Increase the System Open Market Account holdings of securities through purchases of Treasury bills and, if needed, other Treasury securities with remaining maturities of 3 years or less to maintain an ample level of reserves" ([FOMC minutes, 9–10 Dec 2025](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcminutes20251210.htm)). The initial size was about $40 billion of Treasury bills in the first month ([St. Louis Fed, Feb 2026](https://www.stlouisfed.org/publications/page-one-economics/2026/feb/fed-balance-sheet-and-ample-reserves)). Same minutes: "Conditions in U.S. short-term funding markets remained orderly but were generally tighter over the intermeeting period," with average reserve balances "around $2.9 trillion" ([FOMC minutes, 9–10 Dec 2025](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcminutes20251210.htm)).

### B2(iii) Missed federal debt payment — did any occur?

**Finding: no missed federal debt payment occurred. Stated plainly: the United States made every scheduled payment on its debt, in full and on time, throughout January 2024 – August 2026.** No source located reports otherwise, and the debt-limit and shutdown episodes below are reported separately precisely because they are not missed payments.

**Debt-limit episode, January–July 2025 (not a missed payment).** Official record: "Following a suspension period established by the Fiscal Responsibility Act of 2023 (P.L. 118-5), the statutory debt limit was reinstated on January 2, 2025, at $36.1 trillion, the precise level of federal debt subject to limit outstanding on that date. On July 4, 2025, the statutory debt limit was raised by $5.0 trillion, to $41.1 trillion, through enactment of P.L. 119-21. Between January 2025 and that enactment, Treasury implemented 'extraordinary measures' to prevent a debt limit from binding" ([CRS IN10837](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IN10837)). CRS elsewhere states the purpose explicitly: extraordinary measures were used "to prevent the debt limit from binding and allow the government to make its payments in full and on time" ([CRS R47574, 5 Dec 2025](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R47574)).

Extraordinary measures used, with headroom added, January–July 2025 ([CRS IN10837](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IN10837)):

```
Suspension of G Fund reinvestment (FERS)                  $298 billion
Debt Issuance Suspension Period declaration               $145 billion one-time + $8.8 billion/month
Suspension of invested balance, Exchange Stabilization Fund $20 billion
Exchanging securities for Federal Financing Bank obligations $0.3 billion
Suspension of State and Local Government Series           $0 (prevents ~$10 billion/month increase)
```

Extraordinary measures were "projected to be exhausted in August or September of that year" ([CRS R47574](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R47574)); the Bipartisan Policy Center's X-date range was "between August 15 and October 3" ([BPC](https://bipartisanpolicy.org/press-release/uncertainty-x-date-debt-limit-june-2025/)). Treasury Secretary Bessent told Congress on 9 May 2025 that absent action Treasury "probably wouldn't be able to pay its bills in full and on time beginning in August" ([Brookings Hutchins Center, 7 Jul 2025](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-hutchins-center-explains-the-debt-limit/)). The limit was raised before the X-date arrived; the contingency did not occur.

**Government shutdowns (not missed debt payments).** The FY2026 lapse ran from 00:01 ET on 1 October 2025 to the signing of a funding package on 12 November 2025 — **43 days**, the longest full government shutdown in US history, ended by a House vote of 222–209 ([Reuters, 12 Nov 2025](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/us-house-vote-deal-end-longest-government-shutdown-history-2025-11-12/); [NPR, 13 Nov 2025](https://www.npr.org/2025/11/13/nx-s1-5606921/longest-government-shutdown-in-u-s-history-ends-after-43-days)). Funding for most agencies was extended to 30 January 2026 with three full-year appropriations bills enacted ([NADO, 14 Nov 2025](https://www.nado.org/longest-government-shutdown-ends/)). A **partial** shutdown of the Department of Homeland Security is recorded as beginning 14 February 2026 and lasting 76 days ([Wikipedia, government shutdowns in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_shutdowns_in_the_United_States)) — tertiary source only; treated as unverified and flagged in Gaps and failures. Debt service is not appropriated spending and was unaffected in either case.

#### The cash-futures basis trade — the central contested question, resolved against the unwind hypothesis

**The basis trade did not unwind.** This is the strongest single finding for the "no impairment" side and it comes from two independent official sources.

NY Fed: "**No Evidence of Unwind of the Basis Trade** … As a result, according to Desk staff's estimates, the basis remained relatively stable." The same speech gives the March 2020 comparator — the basis then "jumped ~100bp" — and concludes "it was nowhere close to what happened in March 2020" ([NY Fed (Perli), 9 May 2025](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/speeches/2025/per250509)).

OFR, with the mechanical test: "a material unwind of this trade did not occur in April 2025, and the causes of amplified volatility in yields are not very clear." … "Despite speculation that the increases were related to a basis trade unwind, repo borrowing against the Treasuries usually delivered at futures contract expiration did not decline, which is inconsistent with a material unwind." … "hedge fund repo borrowing with Treasuries as collateral was at least $1.4 trillion with no large declines in April or May. … there was a rise in the use of CTD 10-year Treasuries as repo collateral. This is the opposite of what would happen if the basis trade unwound" ([OFR 2025 Annual Report](https://www.financialresearch.gov/annual-reports/files/OFR-AR-2025.pdf)).

**What did unwind was the swap-spread trade, in measured size, and it deleveraged orderly.** OFR: "market intelligence suggests that the cash-swap basis trade became popular in Q1 2025 as hedge funds anticipated policy actions that would increase demand for Treasuries. When the auction for three-year Treasuries had weaker demand than expected on April 8, some funds found that the swap spread trade had become too risky and exited." … "Still, funding conditions in U.S. dollar repo remained strong. This is consistent with an orderly deleveraging. … Unwinding of some swap spread trades may have contributed to volatility in April but did not cause market dysfunction" ([OFR 2025 Annual Report](https://www.financialresearch.gov/annual-reports/files/OFR-AR-2025.pdf)).

Federal Reserve staff later quantified it: "about $60 billion (20 percent) of swap spread positions unwound in April and a further $40 billion unwound in May. Through September 2025, we estimate that hedge funds' positions in swap spread arbitrage returned to pre-stress levels of about $305 billion" ([FEDS Notes, 22 Jun 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/decomposing-hedge-funds-u-s-treasury-exposures-20260622.html)). The same note records that "The April 2025 tariff announcements triggered steep declines in swap spreads amid sharply increased Treasury yields and deteriorating Treasury market liquidity" and that "Market participants noted that strained conditions in Treasury markets were likely exacerbated by hedge funds rapidly unwinding their swap spread trades."

FSOC's assessment is consistent: "unlike March 2020, spreads between off-the-run and on-the-run Treasuries remained steady in April, and there was limited stress in the cash-futures basis trade" ([FSOC 2025 Annual Report](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/261/FSOC2025AnnualReport.pdf)).

A Dallas Fed study of the trade's fragility adds the structural point: "large increases in the cost of funding would be required to trigger deleveraging of positions funded overnight, with term-financing risk unhedged" ([Dallas Fed, 15 Jul 2025](https://www.dallasfed.org/research/economics/2025/0715)).

#### Late-2025 and 2026 funding and market pressure

**Repo/funding pressure, September–December 2025.** "Repo rates have traded sustainably above the Effective Federal Funds Rate (EFFR) since September alongside further rises in the TGA and declines in reserves. This has been apparent in the Tri-Party General Collateral Rate (TGCR) … and even more so in the broader Secured Overnight Financing Rate (SOFR)" ([NY Fed (Perli), 12 Nov 2025](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/speeches/2025/per251112)). FOMC characterisation: "Conditions in U.S. short-term funding markets remained orderly but were generally tighter over the intermeeting period. In secured markets, liquidity conditions were tighter, on average, amid robust Treasury issuance, declining reserve balances in recent months, and month-end pressures" ([FOMC minutes, 9–10 Dec 2025](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcminutes20251210.htm)). Reserve balances stood at $2,955,581 million on the week to 24 December 2025 ([Federal Reserve H.4.1, 29 Dec 2025](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h41/20251229/)).

**March–April 2026 deterioration and recovery.** Fed FSR May 2026: "Since the November report, geopolitical risk associated with developments in the Middle East led to periods of heightened interest rate volatility, especially for shorter-tenor Treasury rates. Treasury market liquidity initially deteriorated in line with this heightened volatility, demonstrating the vulnerability of market liquidity during periods of acute stress. In subsequent weeks, liquidity recovered, and, on net, market depth … was little changed since the previous report." One measure remained weak: "a market depth measure for the most liquid on-the-run two-year Treasury note remained near the first quartile of its historical distribution," and a heading reads "Market depth for the most liquid 2-year on-the-run Treasury note stayed at historically low levels" ([Fed FSR, May 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/financial-stability-report-20260508.pdf)). Measured depth as of 23 April 2026: "the 30-year depth was approximately 25 million, the 10-year around 150 million, and the 5-year about 100" million ([Fed FSR May 2026 accessible tables](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-may-financial-stability-report-accessibility-tables.htm)).

**August 2026 long-end repricing.** As of 18 August 2026: the 30-year Treasury yield traded above 5%, "its highest since 2007"; the 10-year was "around 4.71%"; and the NY Fed's 10-year term premium estimate was "around 80 basis points," "close to its highest level in 12 years," with federal debt "nearly $40 trillion" ([Reuters, 18 Aug 2026](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/selling-grips-bond-markets-us-japan-inflation-fiscal-worries-take-hold-2026-08-18/)). This is a level and term-premium event; no accompanying functioning impairment was sourced.

**Dealer capacity, 2026.** "Measures of dealer intermediation activity in Treasury markets increased further due to growth in secured lending, particularly repurchase agreement (repo) lending to hedge fund clients." … "Dealers' value-at-risk (VaR) remained below internal risk limits, suggesting dealers' intermediation capacity remains adequate for market functioning in normal times. That said, VaR can rise rapidly when volatility increases, so dealers' willingness and ability to intermediate can be tested if VaR approaches those limits during periods of market stress" ([Fed FSR, May 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/financial-stability-report-20260508.pdf)).

**Hedge fund leverage, 2026 — the live vulnerability.** "Leverage for hedge funds remained stable at record-high levels over the period for which comprehensive data have been collected, as a decrease in the cash-futures basis trade was largely offset by other relative value trades, such as swap spread trades." … "High leverage can lead to spillovers if the fund suddenly loses access to funding" ([Fed FSR, May 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/financial-stability-report-20260508.pdf)).

### Federal Reserve — Financial Stability Report

**November 2025 (published 7 November 2025).** The adverse statement and the recovery statement, both:

> "Amid the April volatility, Treasury market liquidity hit historically low levels. Since then, various measures of Treasury market liquidity, including two different measures of market depth in the most liquid on-the-run segment, indicated that liquidity increased back to or above previous levels across all maturities."

## B5. The crisis case and the no-crisis case, both at full strength

### B5.1 The strongest published case that the US has entered or is close to a fiscal/debt crisis

**Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget, "CBO's February 2026 Budget and Economic Outlook," February 2026.** Standing: CRFB is a non-partisan Washington fiscal-policy organisation; Fortune describes it as "non-partisan" ([Fortune, 30 May 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/05/30/national-debt-treasury-bond-rates-fiscal-crisis/)). This is the most explicit crisis-risk language located from an institutional source.

> "CBO's latest baseline shows an unsustainable fiscal outlook, with debt approaching record levels, deficits remaining elevated at more than twice a reasonable target, and interest costs exploding."

> "High and rising debt levels present significant risks and threats to the economy and the nation as a whole. This includes slower economic growth, higher interest rates, increased inflationary pressure, heightened national security risks, reduced fiscal space, growing debt service payments, and greater risk of a fiscal crisis."

> "Later in the decade, under CBO's baseline, the average interest rate on all federal debt will exceed nominal economic growth (R>G), which could represent the start of a debt spiral."

> "The actual fiscal outlook could be far worse." … "If the Supreme Court rules with lower courts that a large share of the President's tariffs are illegal and policymakers extend various expiring or expired provisions, deficits could reach $3.8 trillion in 2036 as opposed to $3.1 trillion, and debt could grow to 131% of GDP by 2036 as opposed to 120%." … "In this case, a debt spiral would be far more likely and the risk of a fiscal crisis would grow."

> "Interest costs will explode." … "Under CBO's baseline, debt will surpass the 106% of GDP record set after World War II by FY 2030 – just four years from now – and will continue to grow to 120% of GDP by 2036." … "Over the long run, CBO projects debt will continue to grow as a share of the economy, reaching 144% of GDP after 20 years and 175% of GDP by 2056."

All from [CRFB, February 2026](https://www.crfb.org/papers/cbos-february-2026-budget-and-economic-outlook). The underlying CBO figures are in [CRFB's summary PDF](https://www.crfb.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/CRFB_CBO_February_2026_Budget_And_Economic_Outlook_3.pdf): nominal interest costs "more than double from $970 billion in 2025 to $2.1 trillion by 2036," rising "from a record 3.2% of GDP in 2025 to 4.6% by 2036."

**Market-side crisis argument, named practitioners, 18 August 2026.** Standing: sell-side and asset-management strategists, not academics; recorded at that standing.

### B5.3 The shape of the disagreement

The two sides are not in conflict about facts and barely overlap in claim. The no-crisis case is about **market functioning** and is supported by measured liquidity, repo and positioning data from the institutions that hold the data. The crisis case is about **the fiscal trajectory and the level of rates** and is supported by CBO's own baseline. Nothing in the no-crisis evidence contradicts the crisis case's projections; nothing in the crisis case identifies a functioning failure. On the four specific criteria in B2, the period yields: no failed auction, no emergency Fed intervention, no missed payment, and an acute-but-reversed rather than sustained impairment — while simultaneously yielding a rating downgrade, a 5%-plus 30-year yield, a 12-year-high term premium, and an official baseline showing record debt.
