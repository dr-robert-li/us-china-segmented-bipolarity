# F4 adapter specification

**Condition.** AI raises rather than lowers within-occupation wage dispersion.

**Threshold.** p90/p10 ratio rises in high-AI-exposure occupations across 10 countries.

**Deadline.** 2032.

**Type.** D -- methodology-sensitive.

**Bears on.** The AI wealth-pump mechanism, a conditional sub-claim. Does **not** bear directly on P1, P2 or P3.

**Depends on.** `pipeline/adjudication.md` for gap-year handling.

**Correlated with.** F6 at **-0.25**, the only negative entry in the dependence matrix. If AI raises within-occupation dispersion, a durable reversal of graduate underemployment becomes less likely rather than more.

---

## The structural defect: this condition's source does not yet exist

F4 names as its data source a **successor** to a study published in 2024. No such successor is guaranteed to appear.

This is a defect in the pre-registration, not an implementation gap, and it must be recorded as such rather than engineered around. Three consequences follow.

First, if no qualifying successor study is published by 2032, F4 is **permanently indeterminate**. It cannot trigger and cannot be cleared.

Second, a condition that cannot resolve contributes nothing to the falsification architecture. F4 would then be decorative, and the papers must say so. The pre-registered set would in practice be seven conditions, not eight.

Third, the temptation to substitute a more readily available study must be resisted. Source substitution without a committed methodological justification is a listed prohibition, and it would be especially damaging here because the choice of exposure measure largely determines the result.

### The reframing this condition monitors

F4 exists to monitor a transition condition. The baseline finding it tests against is that AI exposure has shown **no** effect on between-occupation wage inequality, and has been associated with **lower** dispersion *within* occupations -- with wage inequality declining most in the most-exposed occupations, and a one-standard-deviation increase in exposure associated with roughly **1.5 percentage points lower** growth in the p90/p10 ratio.

The wealth-pump mechanism is therefore a forward-looking conditional hypothesis, not an observed fact. F4 asks whether the observed compression pattern reverses. That framing is what makes the condition worth keeping even in weakened form.

## Fallback construction, and its cost

One alternative to waiting for a successor study exists: construct the measure independently from national wage microdata using the same occupational exposure index, replicating the original methodology.

**Not committed.** It is recorded as an option with its costs stated, because adopting it silently would be worse than leaving the condition unresolved.

The costs are substantial. It requires harmonised wage microdata across at least ten countries with consistent occupational coding. It reproduces every methodological choice of the original study, each of which is contestable. And a self-constructed measure evaluated by the same analyst whose thesis it bears on has an obvious independence problem that an external publication does not.

If the fallback is ever adopted, it must be committed by diff, with the replication code published in this repository, **before** any evaluation year in which it would be load-bearing.

## Coding rules, fixed in advance

These are the Type D rules that were blocking this adapter. All are committed here.

### Exposure measure

The occupational AI-exposure index used must be the same family as the baseline study's, and its **vintage must be named** in the series descriptor. Exposure indices have been revised, and a verdict computed on a different vintage than the baseline is not a comparison.

Where a successor study uses a different exposure measure, that is a **methodology break** under `DATA-INTEGRITY.md`, and the two are not spliced. The verdict is then computed on the successor's own baseline, not against the 2024 figures.

### Country sample

Ten countries, per the threshold. The committed selection rule is the **ten most-populous countries present in both** the baseline study's sample and the successor's, ranked by working-age population at the successor's reference date.

A fixed rule is required because a discretionary sample choice from a larger set could produce either verdict. Ranking by population is arbitrary but pre-committed and checkable; ranking by effect size would not be.

If fewer than ten qualifying countries appear in both samples, the condition is `indeterminate`. It is not evaluated on eight.

### Dispersion statistic

p90/p10 of the wage distribution, as the threshold states. Not the Gini coefficient, not the variance of logs, not p90/p50. Alternatives are reported as sensitivity only.

### "High-exposure" boundary

Occupations in the **top quartile** of the exposure index within each country. Committed here rather than left to the successor study's own cut, so that the verdict does not depend on an editorial choice made elsewhere.

### Direction and majority rule

The clause is `met` if the p90/p10 ratio **rises** in high-exposure occupations in **at least six of the ten** countries, on a change measured over the successor study's full observation window.

Six of ten is committed as a simple majority plus one. A bare five-of-ten split yields `indeterminate` rather than a verdict.

### Magnitude floor

A rise of less than **0.5 percent** in the p90/p10 ratio over the observation window counts as no change, not as a rise. Without a floor, measurement noise in either direction could resolve the condition.

## Sensitivity reporting

1. Committed rules as above
2. Alternative dispersion statistics -- p90/p50, p50/p10, variance of logs
3. Top-decile rather than top-quartile exposure boundary
4. Seven-of-ten and five-of-ten majority rules
5. Magnitude floors at 0 and 1.0 percent
6. Gender- and age-disaggregated results, since the baseline compression finding did **not** extend to reducing wage gaps by gender or age, and that limitation should not disappear at the successor stage

Setting 6 is included because the original finding's stated limitation is the part most likely to be dropped in summary reporting.

## Tests

1. **Missing source.** With no successor study available, the verdict is `indeterminate` and the log states that the condition cannot currently resolve.
2. **Vintage mismatch.** An exposure index of a different vintage than declared raises `structural_break` and blocks splicing.
3. **Sample shortfall.** Fewer than ten qualifying countries yields `indeterminate`, not evaluation on a smaller set.
4. **Majority boundary.** A five-of-ten split yields `indeterminate`.
5. **Magnitude floor.** A 0.3 percent rise counts as no change.
6. **Statistic substitution.** A Gini-based input supplied to the verdict fails validation.
7. **Fallback gate.** A self-constructed measure cannot feed the verdict unless a committed diff adopting it exists in history prior to the evaluation year.

Test 7 is the one that prevents this adapter from quietly becoming its own data source.
