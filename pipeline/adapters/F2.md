# F2 adapter specification

**Condition.** US federal discretionary fiscal capacity recovers.

**Threshold.** Non-interest discretionary outlays exceed 6.0 percent of GDP without a debt crisis.

**Deadline.** 2036.

**Type.** B/D hybrid. Reclassified from D; see below.

**Bears on.** Pillar A (constraints on American self-correction), and P1 secondarily. Does **not** bear on P2 or P3.

**Depends on.** `pipeline/adjudication.md`, including a narrow documented exception to its symmetric-bar rule.

**Correlated with.** F6 at 0.55, the strongest dependency in the matrix. Both would be produced substantially by the same underlying state: a sustained, broad-based expansion with restored fiscal and administrative capacity. They are close to two measurements of one condition, and `n_eff` for the pair is 1.29.

---

## Reclassification note

`falsifiers/PRE-REGISTRATION.md` classifies F2 as Type D, methodology-sensitive but quantitative. That misses something: the clause **"without a debt crisis"** is qualitative and cannot be computed. F2 is therefore compound, and is reclassified to B/D.

The reclassification adds an adjudication obligation that did not previously exist. Under the directional test recorded in `pipeline/adapters/F8.md`, adding a required determination is a tightening, and the change is permissible on that basis.

That this was missed at pre-registration is worth recording plainly. The original classification pass read the numeric threshold and stopped.

## Clause 1 -- Discretionary outlays above 6.0 percent of GDP

### The "non-interest" qualifier is redundant

Under standard federal budget classification, net interest is **not** discretionary spending. It is a separate category, driven by debt stock and rates rather than by annual appropriation. The qualifier "non-interest" therefore excludes something that was never inside the category being measured.

**Committed interpretation.** The clause is read as **total discretionary outlays**, defense plus nondefense, as a percentage of GDP. The redundancy of the qualifier is recorded rather than treated as implying some narrower construction, because no narrower construction is identifiable from the wording.

This interpretation is committed here and may not be revisited after any evaluation. A reader who thinks the qualifier was meant to carve out something specific can see from this file that the question was considered and how it was resolved.

### Committed series

CBO baseline discretionary outlays as a share of GDP, defense and nondefense recorded separately and summed. Budget authority is **not** admissible as a substitute for outlays; the two diverge materially, particularly where emergency-designated funding is involved, and the condition specifies outlays.

### Outturn versus projection

Same rule as F3. The threshold is tested on **outturn only**. Projections are ingested, tracked, and structurally incapable of triggering.

This matters more for F2 than for F3, because CBO projections extend across the full evaluation window and are revised annually. A baseline revision that lifted projected discretionary outlays above 6.0 percent would otherwise fire a falsifier without any appropriation having occurred.

### Current position

Discretionary outlays stand at approximately **5.9 percent of GDP in 2026**, described in the source as the lowest share recorded since 1962, the earliest year for which comparable data exist. The projected path **declines** to roughly 4.8 percent by 2036, with defense falling from about 2.8 to 2.4 percent and nondefense from about 3.1 to 2.4 percent.

Two observations follow, and they pull in opposite directions.

The condition is currently within roughly **0.1 percentage points** of its threshold. That is closer than any other condition in the set except F8. A single year of elevated emergency appropriation could carry outturn above 6.0 percent.

But the projected trajectory moves **away** from the threshold throughout the window. On the current baseline, F2 becomes progressively harder to satisfy over time rather than easier.

The honest summary is that F2 is near-triggered on level and far from triggered on trend. Both must be reported; reporting only the trend would understate how little would have to change.

## Clause 2 -- Without a debt crisis

### The negative-clause problem

This clause is stated as an **absence**. Under the symmetric-bar rule in `pipeline/adjudication.md`, a determination of `met` requires affirmative evidence -- but affirmative evidence of a non-event is not generally obtainable.

Applying the rule literally would make Clause 2 permanently `indeterminate`, which would make F2 permanently unfalsifiable. That is not a defensible reading of a pre-registered condition.

### Committed exception

Clause 2 is `met` by **default**, and becomes `not_met` when crisis indicators fire. This is an explicit, narrow exception to the symmetric-bar rule, justified solely because the clause is stated in the negative.

The exception is bounded in three ways. It applies only to F2's Clause 2. It does not extend to any other condition. And the adverse-interest requirement still applies in full: the adjudicator must write the strongest available case that a debt crisis **did** occur before recording `met`.

Without that last constraint the exception would be an unmonitored escape hatch, since `met` on Clause 2 is the state that permits F2 to trigger and therefore damages Pillar A.

Note the direction carefully. Here the default favours **triggering** the falsifier, not protecting the thesis. An analyst wanting to protect Pillar A would want to find a debt crisis. The exception is therefore not self-serving, which is part of why it is acceptable.

### Crisis indicators

Any of the following, occurring within the evaluation year, moves Clause 2 to `not_met`:

| Indicator | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Failed or materially undersubscribed Treasury auction | Any, at benchmark maturities |
| Emergency central-bank intervention in the Treasury market | Purchases or facilities explicitly aimed at market functioning rather than at monetary policy |
| Missed payment on federal debt obligations | Any, including technical default arising from a borrowing-limit episode |
| Disorderly real-yield movement | Sustained, alongside documented market-functioning impairment |
| Sovereign rating downgrade **plus** market dysfunction | Both required; a downgrade alone is not sufficient |

A rating action alone does not qualify. Downgrades have occurred without market dysfunction, and treating them as crises would make the clause fire on commentary rather than on events.

## Compound assembly

Per `pipeline/adjudication.md`. A quantitative `not_met` short-circuits: while outturn discretionary outlays remain below 6.0 percent, F2 is `not_triggered` and no Clause 2 adjudication is required.

Given that outturn sits near the threshold, Clause 2 must nonetheless be adjudicated **every year** rather than only on crossing. The rubric-exercise logic used in F3 does not suffice here, because the crossing could occur with a single year's notice.

Contemporaneity applies: both clauses in the same year.

## Sensitivity reporting

Type D obligations:

1. Committed interpretation -- total discretionary outlays
2. Nondefense discretionary only, as a narrower alternative reading
3. Budget authority instead of outlays, reported to show the size of the gap and **not** as a candidate verdict
4. Fiscal-year versus calendar-year alignment where the GDP denominator differs in basis

## Tests

1. **Projection cannot trigger.** A projected value of 6.2 percent yields `not_met`.
2. **Budget authority rejection.** A budget-authority figure supplied to Clause 1 fails validation.
3. **Component sum.** Defense plus nondefense reconciles to the total within tolerance, or flags.
4. **Negative-clause default.** With no crisis indicators, Clause 2 returns `met`, with an `adverse_case` present.
5. **Adverse case required.** A Clause 2 record with empty `adverse_case` fails validation even where the determination is the default.
6. **Rating action alone.** A downgrade without documented market dysfunction does not move Clause 2 to `not_met`.
7. **Annual adjudication.** Clause 2 adjudication is required every year regardless of Clause 1's value.
8. **Emergency spike.** A single year of emergency-driven outlays above 6.0 percent triggers, and the log records that the trend remained downward.

Test 8 encodes the level-versus-trend tension rather than resolving it.
