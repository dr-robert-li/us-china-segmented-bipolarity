# F6 adapter specification

**Condition.** US elite overproduction reverses.

**Threshold.** Graduate underemployment falls below 30 percent for five consecutive years.

**Deadline.** 2035.

**Type.** D -- methodology-sensitive.

**Bears on.** Pillar A only. Does **not** bear on P1, P2 or P3.

**Depends on.** `pipeline/adjudication.md` for sustained-condition semantics.

**Correlated with.** F2 at **0.55**, the strongest positive dependency in the matrix, and F4 at **-0.25**, the only negative one. Two crossings of F2 and F6 together yield `n_eff` of 1.29 and are close to a single observation, not two refutations.

---

## Current position and what it implies

The committed series reports a recent-graduate underemployment rate of approximately **42 percent**, with the recent-graduate unemployment rate at about **5.6 percent**, as of the second quarter of 2026. The source describes labour market conditions for recent graduates as remaining challenging, with underemployment having **edged up**.

Against a threshold of below 30 percent, F6 sits roughly **12 percentage points** away, in the wrong direction, and has recently moved further away.

The five-year persistence requirement compounds this. With a 2035 deadline, the **latest possible qualifying window is 2031 through 2035**. A run beginning in 2032 cannot trigger F6 regardless of what follows. For F6 to trigger, underemployment would have to fall by more than twelve points and remain below thirty percent for half a decade, beginning within roughly five years.

This is recorded not to dismiss the condition but to characterise it honestly: F6 is a **demanding** falsifier. A reader is entitled to know that a condition pre-registered as capable of overturning Pillar A requires a very large structural change on a short fuse. That is a legitimate criticism of the threshold's calibration, and it belongs in the papers rather than in a footnote.

## Coding rules, fixed in advance

These are the Type D rules that were blocking this adapter.

### Committed series and its construction

The committed measure is the underemployment rate for **recent college graduates**, constructed from national household survey microdata combined with an occupational requirements database, where underemployment means working in a job that does not typically require a bachelor's degree.

This construction is committed because it is the one the threshold was set against. Alternative constructions -- self-reported skill mismatch, wage-based definitions, or credential-to-occupation crosswalks from other databases -- produce materially different levels and are reported as sensitivity only.

### Cohort boundary

**Recent graduates** means those aged 22 to 27 with a bachelor's degree or higher who are not enrolled in further study.

The committed source distinguishes recent graduates from all graduates, the latter spanning ages 22 to 65. Using the all-graduates series would understate underemployment substantially and would not measure the cohort the elite-overproduction argument concerns. The distinction is committed here so it cannot be swapped later.

### Frequency and smoothing

The published series is seasonally adjusted and smoothed with a three-month moving average. Annual qualification uses the **calendar-year mean of the monthly smoothed series**.

Committed because the alternatives -- a single month, a year-end value, or an unsmoothed average -- are each defensible and each would produce different marginal verdicts. A single-month rule would be the worst choice, since it would let one observation resolve a year.

### Qualification rule

A year qualifies if the calendar-year mean is **strictly below 30.0 percent**. Exactly 30.0 percent does not qualify. Committed to remove any ambiguity at the boundary.

### Precision

The series is published to one decimal place. Qualification is evaluated at that precision, without inferring further digits.

## Sustained-condition semantics

Per `pipeline/adjudication.md`:

- Recomputed from scratch every evaluation. No stateful counter.
- Gap years are `indeterminate`; they neither advance nor reset.
- A year at or above 30.0 percent **resets** the run to zero.
- Runs shorten on revision. The underlying survey microdata is revised and reweighted, so this will occur.
- Latest qualifying window is 2031 through 2035.

From 2032 onward, if no run is in progress, the annual log must state that F6 has become unfalsifiable within its own horizon. That statement is itself information about the pre-registration's design and must not be omitted because it is unflattering.

---

## Structural break risk

The series depends on both a household survey and an occupational requirements database. Either can be revised in ways that shift the level without any change in the labour market.

Specific risks:

- **Occupational classification revision.** A change in which occupations are coded as requiring a degree moves the rate directly.
- **Survey reweighting.** Population control revisions shift levels.
- **Requirements-database update.** Reclassification of degree requirements for large occupations has an outsized effect.

Any of these is a registered break under `DATA-INTEGRITY.md`. A series is not spliced across a break without a committed bridging rule, and a run in progress at a break is **suspended**, not continued, until the bridging rule is committed.

This is a live rather than theoretical risk. A reclassification that moved the rate below 30 percent without any change in graduate outcomes would otherwise start a qualifying run.

## Sensitivity reporting

Type D obligations:

1. Committed construction and cohort as above
2. All-graduates cohort, ages 22 to 65, as the alternative reading
3. Unsmoothed annual mean
4. Year-end value rather than annual mean
5. Alternative underemployment definitions, where published
6. Verdict computed on pre-revision and post-revision series where a break has occurred

## Tests

1. **Cohort separation.** An all-graduates figure supplied to the verdict fails validation.
2. **Boundary.** Exactly 30.0 percent does not qualify.
3. **Annual mean.** A single month below 30 percent within a year averaging above it does not qualify the year.
4. **Reset.** One non-qualifying year resets the run to zero.
5. **Gap suspension.** A gap year neither advances nor resets.
6. **Recompute shortening.** A revision removing an early year's qualification shortens the recomputed run.
7. **Break suspension.** A registered break suspends a run in progress and blocks splicing absent a committed bridging rule.
8. **Deadline arithmetic.** A run beginning in 2032 cannot produce `triggered`.
9. **Unfalsifiability notice.** From 2032, with no run in progress, the log emits the required statement.

Test 7 protects the condition from being satisfied by a reclassification. Test 9 protects the reader from an unfalsifiable condition being reported as merely not-yet-triggered.

## Amendment 1 -- 2026-08-20 -- dry-run result: discrimination untestable; threshold outside observed range

**Appended, not substituted. No rule, threshold, cohort, or construction changes.** F6's live verdict is `not_triggered` at roughly 12 points before and after; nothing here can bear on it. Recalibration remains prohibited by the pre-registration; this amendment is disclosure.

### Dry-run result

The exercise at `../../falsifiers/adjudications/dry-run/F6-2001-2010.md` applied the committed coding rules to the full published span of the committed series (January 1990 -- June 2026, snapshotted). Result: **the discrimination test is untestable.** The committed series has never produced a qualifying year -- zero of thirty-six -- and no source establishes that the condition F6 tests (a reversal of elite overproduction) has occurred within the span, so no expected-positive episode exists. This is a third outcome category, distinct from F1's pass and F5-B's failure: no event was missed, and nothing was validated at the threshold.
