# F5 adapter specification

**Condition.** PRC succession fails destructively.

**Threshold.** Extra-constitutional leadership transfer, a purge exceeding 15 percent of the Politburo, or military intervention in succession.

**Deadline.** 2040.

**Type.** C -- expert-adjudicated. The only pure Type C condition in the set.

**Bears on.** P1 only. **Consistent with P3.** A crossing shifts posterior mass toward the peaking-power reading rather than refuting segmented bipolarity.

**Depends on.** `pipeline/adjudication.md` in full. F5 has no automatable component except the purge arithmetic.

**Correlated with.** F3 at 0.25 and F7 at 0.20. Both run through elite conflict and industrial-policy continuity respectively; both are set low because the direction of causation is genuinely ambiguous.

---

## Why this condition is the weakest in the set, and why it is kept

F5 has no series. Its named source is contemporaneous scholarly consensus, which is not a dataset and cannot be queried. It is the condition most exposed to the criticism that adjudication is doing the work that measurement should do.

It is retained for one reason: the alternative is worse. A structural analysis of the PRC that contains no condition capable of registering elite-political discontinuity would be asserting that such discontinuity is either impossible or irrelevant, and neither is defensible. A weak but honest condition beats a silent gap.

What the adapter can do is constrain the discretion. The three sub-clauses below are specified so that each has named, checkable referents, and one of them is arithmetic.

---

## Disjunctive structure

F5 is satisfied if **any one** of three sub-clauses is met. This differs from F3 and F7, which are conjunctive.

The disjunction is inherited from the pre-registered wording and is not narrowed here. Each sub-clause is adjudicated separately and recorded separately, so that a reader can see which limb fired.

---

## Sub-clause A -- Extra-constitutional leadership transfer

### What counts

A transfer of the paramount leadership position, or of General Secretary of the Party, effected by a process that departs from the procedures set out in Party and state constitutional documents in force at the time.

The reference point is the documents **in force at the time of the transfer**, not at pre-registration. This matters: constitutional amendment is itself a constitutional process, so a transfer conducted under amended rules is not extra-constitutional merely because the rules changed.

### What does not count

- Amendment of term limits or succession procedures, however consequential
- Transfer following amended procedures
- Death or incapacity handled under existing provisions
- Retirement outside conventional age norms, absent procedural departure

The distinction between *irregular* and *extra-constitutional* is the whole content of this sub-clause. A leader remaining in office beyond prior convention, by amending the rule that established the convention, is not what this clause tests.

---

## Sub-clause B -- Purge exceeding 15 percent of the Politburo

### The arithmetic

The only computable element in F5.

```
threshold_members = ceil(0.15 * seated_politburo_count)
```

The seated count is **recorded from primary Party sources at the start of each evaluation year**, not hardcoded. Politburo size has varied across Party congresses, and a hardcoded number would silently misstate the threshold after any change.

For illustration only: a Politburo seated at 24 full members yields a threshold of 4, since 15 percent of 24 is 3.6, rounded up. The actual count governing any evaluation is whatever is recorded that year, from primary sources.

Rounding is **up**, committed here. Rounding down would let a threshold be cleared by fractional arithmetic.

### What counts as a purge removal

The hard problem is separating **anti-corruption enforcement** from **succession conflict**. Routine discipline enforcement against senior cadres is a standing feature of the system and is not evidence of destructive succession. Counting it as such would make F5 fire on ordinary politics.

A removal counts toward the purge total only where the adjudication finds, on the evidence, that it is more plausibly explained by leadership-succession conflict than by enforcement of standing discipline. Indicators the adjudication must address explicitly:

| Indicator | Weight toward `met` |
|---|---|
| Temporal clustering | Multiple removals within a short window rather than sequentially over years |
| Factional coherence | Removed members share a factional or patronage association |
| Procedural irregularity | Removal announced without the usual investigative sequence |
| Proximity to a succession event | Removals cluster around a congress, plenum, or leadership transition |
| Absence of stated cause | No corruption or discipline finding published |
| Military or security portfolio concentration | Removals concentrated in security-apparatus roles |

None is individually sufficient. A removal accompanied by a published discipline finding, arriving in an ordinary investigative sequence, and not clustered, counts toward **nothing**.

### Window

Removals are counted within a rolling **24-month** window. Committed here because a purge is a bounded episode; removals spread over a decade are attrition, not a purge, and an unbounded window would eventually accumulate a threshold crossing from ordinary turnover.

## Sub-clause C -- Military intervention in succession

### What counts

Deployment or threatened deployment of military or armed-police force to determine, block, or secure a leadership transition. Includes military units acting to secure the succession of a particular claimant.

### What does not count

- Routine security arrangements around Party meetings
- Military representation in Party bodies, which is structural rather than interventionary
- Personnel changes in the military command, which fall under Sub-clause B if they qualify at all
- Exercises or deployments directed externally

This sub-clause is the least likely to be satisfied ambiguously and the most likely to be reported unreliably in real time. The evidence standards bind hardest here: single-outlet or anonymously-sourced reporting is insufficient alone for `met`.

---

## Adjudication requirements

Beyond the general framework:

- Each sub-clause is adjudicated and recorded **separately**, even where one is clearly not met.
- The adverse-interest requirement applies **per sub-clause**. The strongest case that each limb is met must be written before its determination.
- Contrary sources must be cited for each limb where any exist.
- Official PRC statements are evidence of a position, never of a fact. This binds unusually hard for F5, where official characterisation of an event is often the only immediately available account.
- Where scholarly assessment is genuinely divided, the determination records the division and returns `indeterminate` rather than adopting the majority view. "Contemporaneous scholarly consensus" means consensus; its absence is not resolved by counting.

---

## Verdict

```
triggered      if any sub-clause is met
indeterminate  if no sub-clause is met but any is indeterminate
not_triggered  if all three are not_met
```

F5 is **not** a sustained condition. A single qualifying event satisfies it permanently; a subsequent return to procedural regularity does not clear it.

| `politburo_threshold` | Computed member threshold | count | derived | **Yes** |

All three are sourced from primary Party and state publications. No third-party tabulation feeds the verdict, though such tabulations are ingested as cross-checks.

## Tests

1. **Threshold recomputation.** A change in seated count recomputes the threshold; no hardcoded value exists in code.
2. **Rounding.** A count of 24 yields a threshold of 4, not 3.
3. **Window bound.** Removals spread over 36 months do not accumulate to a crossing.
4. **Enforcement exclusion.** A removal with a published discipline finding, in ordinary sequence, unclustered, contributes zero.
5. **Per-sub-clause adverse case.** A record missing `adverse_case` for any adjudicated limb fails validation.
6. **Consensus division.** Divided scholarly assessment returns `indeterminate`, not a majority verdict.
7. **Permanence.** A satisfied sub-clause cannot be cleared by later events.
8. **Constitutional amendment.** A transfer under amended procedures does not satisfy Sub-clause A.

Test 4 is what stops F5 becoming a corruption-enforcement detector. Test 8 is what stops it becoming a norm-erosion detector. Both would be interesting conditions; neither is this one.

## Amendment 2026-08-19 -- Sub-clause B window and denominator anchoring

**Appended, not substituted.** Everything above stands as originally committed. This amendment resolves two ambiguities that the original text left open and that were discovered while authoring `falsifiers/adjudications/2026/F5-B.md`.

**Stated first, because it is the only thing that makes this amendment admissible:** Sub-clause B is currently `not_met` at a distance of one to two members, and neither resolution below changes that determination. `ceil(0.15 * 24) = 4` and `ceil(0.15 * 23) = 4`. **This is the last moment at which these definitions can be fixed without the fix being indistinguishable from fitting the rule to a known outcome.** After one further Politburo expulsion, either resolution becomes outcome-determining, and any change made then -- however well reasoned -- would be uncontaminated only by assertion. The amendment is made now for that reason and for no other.

### Defect 1 -- the denominator was endogenous to the numerator

The original text specifies that the seated count is "recorded from primary Party sources at the start of each evaluation year". It does not say whether removals already counted toward the purge total are deducted from the denominator.

Read one way, they are, and the clause becomes a ratchet: each removal shrinks the base, which lowers the member threshold, which makes the next removal more likely to cross it. Three removals against 24 do not reach the threshold of 4; the same three against a base of 20 do reach a threshold of 3. **The clause could therefore have been crossed by arithmetic rather than by evidence.**

**Resolution, committed.** The denominator is the count of full Politburo members **seated at the start of the counting window, before deduction of any removal counted within that window**. Where the window opens after a Party Congress with no intervening removals, this equals the count as elected.

For the 20th Politburo the denominator is **24**.

Rationale: a purge is measured as a share of the body it was inflicted on, not as a share of what survived it. The alternative reading measures each removal against a base the earlier removals already reduced, which double-counts them.

### Defect 2 -- the anchoring date of a removal was unspecified

Four candidate dates exist for any single removal, and they can fall in different years: announcement of investigation, internal decision by the deciding organ, first official public announcement of expulsion or removal, and confirmation by the Central Committee in plenary session. The original text names none.

Two live cases show the spread. He Weidong: expelled 17 October 2025 by announcement, confirmed 23 October 2025 at the Fourth Plenum. Ma Xingrui: decided by the Politburo 30 June 2026, announced 14 July 2026.

**Resolution, committed.** A removal is anchored to the date of the **first official public announcement that the member has been expelled or removed from the Politburo**.

Rationale, stated with its cost:

- **Announcement of investigation is rejected.** An investigation is not a removal. Anchoring to it would count members who remain seated, and would let the clause fire on a stage of process rather than on an outcome. This is the resolution that makes Sub-clause B **harder** to satisfy, and it is adopted for that reason being incidental rather than the reason.
- **Internal decision date is rejected** despite being the substantively correct moment, because it is knowable only when the Party chooses to disclose it, and it is not disclosed uniformly. A rule that depends on discretionary disclosure is not auditable.
- **Plenary confirmation is rejected** because it clusters removals artificially into plenum dates, which would inflate the temporal-clustering indicator that the adapter itself uses as evidence of purge rather than enforcement. Anchoring to confirmation would let the Party's meeting calendar manufacture the appearance of clustering.
- **First official public announcement is adopted** because it is dated, public, primary, uniform across cases, and independent of the analyst's choice.

Where no official announcement exists, the member is **not counted**, regardless of secondary reporting to the contrary. This is applied against interest in 2026: the record conflicts on one member's status, one major outlet counts him as expelled, and he is not counted.

### Window endpoint

The rolling 24-month window committed above is anchored **backward from the end of the evaluation year**, so the 2026 window runs 1 January 2025 through 31 December 2026 inclusive, on announcement dates. This is stated because "rolling 24-month window" without an anchor admits a choice of endpoint, and a choice of endpoint is a choice of which removals are inside.

### Effect on the 2026 determination

None. Under both defects resolved: denominator 24, threshold 4, window 1 Jan 2025 -- 31 Dec 2026, counted removals 2 (He Weidong, announced 17 Oct 2025; Ma Xingrui, announced 14 Jul 2026). Determination `not_met`, distance one to two members.

### Test additions

9. **Denominator non-endogeneity.** Counting a removal must not change the threshold used for that same window.
10. **Removal anchoring.** A removal whose investigation was announced inside the window but whose expulsion was announced outside it does not count in that window.
11. **No unannounced removals.** A member reported as removed by secondary sources, with no official announcement, contributes zero.

## Amendment 3 -- 2026-08-19 -- registered false-negative mode; candidate replacement anchoring, prospective only

**No rule in this adapter changes with effect on evaluation year 2026.** This amendment registers a demonstrated defect and a candidate replacement whose earliest possible effect is evaluation year 2027.

### The demonstrated defect

Amendment 1 committed the anchoring of a removal to the date of the **first official public announcement that the member has been expelled or removed from the Politburo**, with four reasons: the date is dated, public, primary, uniform across cases, and independent of the analyst's choice.

The dry run at `../../falsifiers/adjudications/dry-run/1976.md` exercised Sub-clause B against the succession following the death of Mao Zedong. Result: **`not_met`** at 1 counted removal against a threshold of 4, on a denominator of 21.

The negative control at `../../falsifiers/adjudications/dry-run/2012.md` also returns `not_met`. **The clause does not discriminate between the two episodes**, which was named in advance as the most serious of the three failure conditions.

The mechanism is specific and is a property of this adapter rather than of the threshold:

> Under an extra-constitutional removal, the deciding authority has an interest in delaying or withholding the formal announcement, because the announcement is the act that makes the removal reviewable. Under an ordinary disciplinary removal, prompt announcement is the norm. The announcement date is therefore **anti-correlated** with the irregularity Sub-clause B exists to detect.

Concretely: the Gang of Four were arrested 6 October 1976 and expelled at the Third Plenum of 16--21 July 1977, outside a window ending 31 December 1976. Four of the five most consequential removals in the episode were invisible to the rule.

The four reasons given in Amendment 1 remain sound as **auditability** criteria. They were the wrong criteria to optimise against, because an anchoring rule selected for auditability was permitted to determine what the clause could observe. That is recorded as an error of design priority, not of reasoning within the priority chosen.

### Registered flag

```yaml
adapter_flags:
  - flag: known_false_negative_mode
    clause: B
    registered: 2026-08-19
    basis: falsifiers/adjudications/dry-run/1976.md
    statement: >
      The committed removal-anchoring rule fails to register removals effected by
      arrest or de facto dismissal where formal expulsion is announced outside the
      evaluation window. Sub-clause B returns not_met on the archetypal historical
      case of extra-constitutional removal. A not_met determination on Sub-clause B
      is therefore not evidence that no purge-scale event occurred.
    directionality: one-directional
    directionality_note: >
      The negative control returns not_met under every anchoring considered. The
      rule can be made to miss a purge; it has not been shown capable of inventing
      one. False-positive risk is low; false-negative risk is demonstrated.
    earliest_resolution: evaluation_year 2027
```

Any consumer of this adapter reporting a Sub-clause B `not_met` must carry this flag with it. The dashboard and the annual log entry both do so from this date.

### Candidate replacement anchoring -- registered, not in force

```yaml
candidate_rule:
  id: F5-B-ANCHOR-2
  status: registered_not_in_force
  registered: 2026-08-19
  earliest_effective: evaluation_year 2027
  supersedes: F5-B-ANCHOR-1 (first official public announcement of expulsion)
  definition: >
    A removal is anchored to the earliest date on which the member is established
    by two or more independent non-PRC-state sources to have effectively lost the
    office, whether by detention, arrest, published dismissal, or formal expulsion,
    whichever occurs first.
  exclusions:
    - Death in office, by any cause, counts toward nothing.
    - Retirement at or after a scheduled congress counts toward nothing.
    - Announcement of investigation alone is insufficient; effective loss of the
      office must be established.
  costs_accepted:
    - Loses uniformity: the establishing evidence differs in kind across cases.
    - Loses analyst-independence: judging "effective loss of office" from reporting
      is a judgement, and the exclusion rule cannot fully constrain it.
    - Depends on non-official reporting, which the framework's evidence hierarchy
      ranks below primary documents.
  why_accepted_anyway: >
    The rule it replaces is demonstrably insensitive to the event class the clause
    describes. A less auditable rule that can observe the phenomenon is preferable
    to a more auditable rule that cannot.
  required_before_taking_effect:
    - Re-run both dry runs under F5-B-ANCHOR-2 and publish both results, including
      the negative control, before the 2027 annual review opens.
    - If the negative control returns met under the new anchoring, the candidate is
      rejected and this registration is closed unadopted.
```

### Why the candidate is not adopted now

Under `F5-B-ANCHOR-2` the live 2026 numerator moves from 2 to 3 against a threshold of 4, because a member reported detained in January 2026 would count. The distance to trigger narrows from two members to one.

**Adopting a rule now that moves a live determination toward the project's own predicted direction is prohibited.** The pre-registration's bar on changing a rule as its threshold is approached is direction-neutral. A change that helps the thesis is not more acceptable than one that hurts it; if anything the reverse, since the incentive to make it is stronger and the reasoning supporting it will feel more compelling to the person making it.

The change is substantively correct and procedurally unavailable. Both halves of that sentence are recorded, and the second governs.

### Effect on the 2026 determination

**None.** Denominator 24, threshold 4, window 1 January 2025 -- 31 December 2026, counted removals 2 on announcement dates. Sub-clause B `not_met`, now carrying the `known_false_negative_mode` flag.

### Test additions

12. **False-negative flag propagation.** A Sub-clause B `not_met` result must carry the `known_false_negative_mode` flag through to any consumer; a consumer that drops it fails.
13. **Candidate rule is inert.** `F5-B-ANCHOR-2` must not affect any computation for `evaluation_year <= 2026`.
14. **Death exclusion.** Seats vacated by death contribute zero to the numerator under both anchorings.

## Amendment 4 -- 2026-08-20 -- death-vacancy rate reported alongside every Sub-clause B verdict

**Appended, not substituted. Inert for the 2026 determination:** this amendment adds a published quantity and touches neither the numerator, the denominator, the threshold, nor the anchoring. The 2026 value of the new quantity is zero, and the Sub-clause B determination is `not_met` at 2 counted removals against a threshold of 4 before and after.

### Why the quantity is reported

The 1976 dry run showed the exclusion of deaths from the purge numerator is load-bearing at the same order of magnitude as the threshold itself: **five of twenty-one seats fell vacant by death inside the 1976 window -- 24 percent of the body, above the 15 percent threshold on mortality alone.** A rubric counting vacated seats rather than removals would return `met` on 1976 for reasons having nothing to do with a purge. The exclusion is correct -- death in office is not a purge -- but its magnitude is invisible in a bare Sub-clause B verdict, and a definitional choice of threshold-order magnitude should not be invisible.

### The rule, committed

Every published Sub-clause B verdict, in any evaluation year, reports alongside it:

```
death_vacancy_rate = deaths_of_full_members_in_window / denominator
```

using the same counting window and the same denominator as the verdict. The rate feeds **nothing**. It is a published context quantity only; no threshold attaches to it and none may be attached without a fresh pre-registered condition.

### Values for the episodes on file

| Episode | Deaths in window | Denominator | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1976 dry run | 5 (Dong Biwu, Kang Sheng, Zhou Enlai, Zhu De, Mao Zedong) | 21 | 23.8% |
| 2012 dry run | 0 | 25 | 0.0% |
| 2026 determination | 0 | 24 | 0.0% |

The 2026 value rests on the record in `../../falsifiers/adjudications/2026/F5-B.md`, which establishes that the 20th Politburo's losses in the window were "by expulsion, not by retirement or death". The 2012 value rests on the dry run's finding that no member of the 17th Politburo died in office inside its window.

### Series addition

| series_id | Concept | Unit | Basis | Feeds verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `politburo_death_vacancy_rate` | Deaths of full members in window over denominator | ratio | derived | **No** -- reported context only |

### Test addition

15. **Death-vacancy rate is inert.** Changing the death count must not change the Sub-clause B verdict; omitting the rate from a published verdict fails the reporting requirement.
