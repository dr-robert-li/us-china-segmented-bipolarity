# F5 adapter specification

**Condition.** PRC succession fails destructively.

**Threshold.** Extra-constitutional leadership transfer, a purge exceeding 15 percent of the Politburo, or military intervention in succession.

**Deadline.** 2040.

**Type.** C -- expert-adjudicated. The only pure Type C condition in the set.

**Bears on.** P1 only. **Consistent with P3.** A crossing shifts posterior mass toward the peaking-power reading rather than refuting segmented bipolarity.

**Depends on.** `pipeline/adjudication.md` in full. F5 has no automatable component except the purge arithmetic.

**Correlated with.** F3 at 0.25 and F7 at 0.20. Both run through elite conflict and industrial-policy continuity respectively; both are set low because the direction of causation is genuinely ambiguous.

---

## Why this condition is the weakest in the set, and why it is kept

F5 has no series. Its named source is contemporaneous scholarly consensus, which is not a dataset and cannot be queried. It is the condition most exposed to the criticism that adjudication is doing the work that measurement should do.

It is retained for one reason: the alternative is worse. A structural analysis of the PRC that contains no condition capable of registering elite-political discontinuity would be asserting that such discontinuity is either impossible or irrelevant, and neither is defensible. A weak but honest condition beats a silent gap.

What the adapter can do is constrain the discretion. The three sub-clauses below are specified so that each has named, checkable referents, and one of them is arithmetic.

---

## Disjunctive structure

F5 is satisfied if **any one** of three sub-clauses is met. This differs from F3 and F7, which are conjunctive.

The disjunction is inherited from the pre-registered wording and is not narrowed here. Each sub-clause is adjudicated separately and recorded separately, so that a reader can see which limb fired.

---

## Sub-clause A -- Extra-constitutional leadership transfer

### What counts

A transfer of the paramount leadership position, or of General Secretary of the Party, effected by a process that departs from the procedures set out in Party and state constitutional documents in force at the time.

The reference point is the documents **in force at the time of the transfer**, not at pre-registration. This matters: constitutional amendment is itself a constitutional process, so a transfer conducted under amended rules is not extra-constitutional merely because the rules changed.

### What does not count

- Amendment of term limits or succession procedures, however consequential
- Transfer following amended procedures
- Death or incapacity handled under existing provisions
- Retirement outside conventional age norms, absent procedural departure

The distinction between *irregular* and *extra-constitutional* is the whole content of this sub-clause. A leader remaining in office beyond prior convention, by amending the rule that established the convention, is not what this clause tests.

---

## Sub-clause B -- Purge exceeding 15 percent of the Politburo

### The arithmetic

The only computable element in F5.

```
threshold_members = ceil(0.15 * seated_politburo_count)
```

The seated count is **recorded from primary Party sources at the start of each evaluation year**, not hardcoded. Politburo size has varied across Party congresses, and a hardcoded number would silently misstate the threshold after any change.

For illustration only: a Politburo seated at 24 full members yields a threshold of 4, since 15 percent of 24 is 3.6, rounded up. The actual count governing any evaluation is whatever is recorded that year, from primary sources.

Rounding is **up**, committed here. Rounding down would let a threshold be cleared by fractional arithmetic.

### What counts as a purge removal

The hard problem is separating **anti-corruption enforcement** from **succession conflict**. Routine discipline enforcement against senior cadres is a standing feature of the system and is not evidence of destructive succession. Counting it as such would make F5 fire on ordinary politics.

A removal counts toward the purge total only where the adjudication finds, on the evidence, that it is more plausibly explained by leadership-succession conflict than by enforcement of standing discipline. Indicators the adjudication must address explicitly:

| Indicator | Weight toward `met` |
|---|---|
| Temporal clustering | Multiple removals within a short window rather than sequentially over years |
| Factional coherence | Removed members share a factional or patronage association |
| Procedural irregularity | Removal announced without the usual investigative sequence |
| Proximity to a succession event | Removals cluster around a congress, plenum, or leadership transition |
| Absence of stated cause | No corruption or discipline finding published |
| Military or security portfolio concentration | Removals concentrated in security-apparatus roles |

None is individually sufficient. A removal accompanied by a published discipline finding, arriving in an ordinary investigative sequence, and not clustered, counts toward **nothing**.

### Window

Removals are counted within a rolling **24-month** window. Committed here because a purge is a bounded episode; removals spread over a decade are attrition, not a purge, and an unbounded window would eventually accumulate a threshold crossing from ordinary turnover.

## Sub-clause C -- Military intervention in succession

### What counts

Deployment or threatened deployment of military or armed-police force to determine, block, or secure a leadership transition. Includes military units acting to secure the succession of a particular claimant.

### What does not count

- Routine security arrangements around Party meetings
- Military representation in Party bodies, which is structural rather than interventionary
- Personnel changes in the military command, which fall under Sub-clause B if they qualify at all
- Exercises or deployments directed externally

This sub-clause is the least likely to be satisfied ambiguously and the most likely to be reported unreliably in real time. The evidence standards bind hardest here: single-outlet or anonymously-sourced reporting is insufficient alone for `met`.

---

## Adjudication requirements

Beyond the general framework:

- Each sub-clause is adjudicated and recorded **separately**, even where one is clearly not met.
- The adverse-interest requirement applies **per sub-clause**. The strongest case that each limb is met must be written before its determination.
- Contrary sources must be cited for each limb where any exist.
- Official PRC statements are evidence of a position, never of a fact. This binds unusually hard for F5, where official characterisation of an event is often the only immediately available account.
- Where scholarly assessment is genuinely divided, the determination records the division and returns `indeterminate` rather than adopting the majority view. "Contemporaneous scholarly consensus" means consensus; its absence is not resolved by counting.

---

## Verdict

```
triggered      if any sub-clause is met
indeterminate  if no sub-clause is met but any is indeterminate
not_triggered  if all three are not_met
```

F5 is **not** a sustained condition. A single qualifying event satisfies it permanently; a subsequent return to procedural regularity does not clear it.

## Series required

| series_id | Concept | Unit | Basis | Feeds verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `politburo_seated_count` | Full Politburo members seated | count | reported | **Yes**, threshold denominator |
| `politburo_removals` | Removals with date, stated cause, portfolio | records | reported | **Yes**, Sub-clause B |
| `politburo_threshold` | Computed member threshold | count | derived | **Yes** |

All three are sourced from primary Party and state publications. No third-party tabulation feeds the verdict, though such tabulations are ingested as cross-checks.

## Tests

1. **Threshold recomputation.** A change in seated count recomputes the threshold; no hardcoded value exists in code.
2. **Rounding.** A count of 24 yields a threshold of 4, not 3.
3. **Window bound.** Removals spread over 36 months do not accumulate to a crossing.
4. **Enforcement exclusion.** A removal with a published discipline finding, in ordinary sequence, unclustered, contributes zero.
5. **Per-sub-clause adverse case.** A record missing `adverse_case` for any adjudicated limb fails validation.
6. **Consensus division.** Divided scholarly assessment returns `indeterminate`, not a majority verdict.
7. **Permanence.** A satisfied sub-clause cannot be cleared by later events.
8. **Constitutional amendment.** A transfer under amended procedures does not satisfy Sub-clause A.

Test 4 is what stops F5 becoming a corruption-enforcement detector. Test 8 is what stops it becoming a norm-erosion detector. Both would be interesting conditions; neither is this one.
