# F7 adapter specification

**Condition.** PRC robotics fails to offset labour contraction.

**Threshold.** Manufacturing TFP growth turns negative for five consecutive years despite continued IFR-recorded robot-density growth.

**Deadline.** 2040.

**Type.** A/D hybrid. Reclassified from B; see below.

**Bears on.** P1 and **P2**. F7 attacks the mechanism as well as the direction: if automation fails to offset labour contraction, the claim that the AI-robotics transition rewards China's physical-capacity advantages is directly undermined.

**Depends on.** `pipeline/adjudication.md` for sustained-condition semantics. Does **not** depend on it for any qualitative determination.

**Correlated with.** F3 at 0.45. Both common-cause and causal: disorderly deleveraging compresses industrial capital expenditure, which impairs the very offset mechanism this condition tests.

---

## Reclassification note

`falsifiers/PRE-REGISTRATION.md` classifies F7 as Type B, compound with an adjudicated qualitative clause. That is **wrong**, and the correction is mandated rather than discretionary.

Both clauses are computable. Manufacturing TFP growth is a number. Robot-density growth is a number. `pipeline/adjudication.md` prohibits adjudicating a clause that can be computed: *if a clause can be computed, it is computed*. Retaining F7 as Type B would introduce analyst discretion where none is needed, which is the opposite of what the framework is for.

F7 is therefore reclassified as A/D: computable arithmetic, but methodology-sensitive in its TFP series choice and its density decomposition, so Type D sensitivity obligations attach.

The change removes a human judgement rather than adding one. Under the directional test recorded in `pipeline/adapters/F8.md`, that is a tightening.

---

## The denominator trap

**This is the substantive problem with F7 and the reason the adapter needs a specification decision rather than just an implementation.**

Robot density is conventionally robots per 10,000 manufacturing employees. It is a ratio, and it rises when either the numerator grows **or the denominator shrinks**.

China faces a contracting manufacturing workforce. Density growth could therefore be fully satisfied by employment decline with **zero** new robot installations. Read literally, the clause "despite continued robot-density growth" would then be met by the very labour contraction the condition is asking whether robotics can offset -- which inverts the condition's meaning entirely.

### Specification decision

Density growth is **decomposed** into its numerator and denominator contributions:

```
d ln(D) = d ln(stock) - d ln(employment)
```

A year satisfies the robotics clause only if:

1. `d ln(D) > 0` -- density grew, as the pre-registered wording requires, **and**
2. `d ln(stock) > 0` -- the operational stock grew, so the growth is not wholly a denominator artifact

This is recorded as an **interpretation rule**, not a threshold change. The pre-registered threshold is untouched; condition 2 prevents the wording from being satisfied in a manner contrary to its evident purpose. The decomposition is published for every year so a reader can see both components and disagree with the interpretation if they wish.

Both the strict-wording verdict, using condition 1 alone, and the interpreted verdict are reported. Where they diverge, that divergence is the finding.

## The TFP series mismatch

The pre-registered source for TFP is a total-economy productivity database. The condition, however, specifies **manufacturing** TFP. These are not the same series, and the named source does not publish sector-level TFP for the PRC at the required granularity.

This is the binding definitional gap in the condition, structurally similar to F8's boundary problem.

**Committed rule.** Until a defensible manufacturing-specific series is identified:

- Total-economy TFP growth is used as a **declared proxy**, flagged on every observation.
- The proxy status is stated wherever an F7 figure is published.
- No `triggered` verdict may be emitted on proxy data alone. The strongest available verdict on the proxy is `indeterminate`.

The asymmetry is deliberate. A proxy is adequate to show that a condition has **not** been met; it is not adequate to declare a pre-registered falsifier satisfied. Triggering a falsifier on a series that measures something adjacent to what the condition names would be indefensible, and it would be indefensible in the direction that damages the thesis, which is exactly when the temptation to accept weak evidence is lowest and the obligation to refuse it is highest.

Candidate manufacturing-specific alternatives are to be evaluated and, if adopted, committed by diff with reasoning before any evaluation year in which they would be load-bearing.

## Sustained-condition semantics

Per `pipeline/adjudication.md`, applied to a conjunctive per-year clause.

### Per-year qualification

A year qualifies only if **both** hold within that **same** year:

- manufacturing TFP growth `< 0`
- the robotics clause is satisfied per the decomposition rule above

A year of negative TFP growth in which robot stock also contracted does **not** qualify. The condition tests failure of automation to offset, not industrial weakness generally. Conflating the two would make F7 a proxy for recession.

### Run handling

- Recomputed from scratch at every evaluation. No stateful counter.
- Gap years are `indeterminate`: they neither advance nor reset the run.
- A year in which either sub-condition clearly fails **resets** the run to zero.
- Runs can shorten on revision. Both named sources revise, so this will happen.

### Deadline arithmetic

Five consecutive qualifying years with a 2040 deadline means the **latest possible qualifying window is 2036 through 2040**. A run beginning in 2037 cannot trigger F7 regardless of what follows.

This is a consequence of the pre-registration, not a defect. It is stated explicitly so that the annual log can report, from 2037 onward, that F7 has become unfalsifiable within its own horizon -- which is itself information about the design of the condition.

## Series required

| series_id | Concept | Unit | Basis | Feeds verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `tfp_growth_manufacturing` | Manufacturing TFP growth | % p.a. | reported | **Yes**, when available |
| `tfp_growth_total_economy` | Total-economy TFP growth | % p.a. | reported | Proxy only; cannot trigger |
| `robot_stock_operational` | Operational industrial robot stock | units | reported | **Yes** |
| `robot_installations_annual` | Annual installations | units | reported | Context |
| `mfg_employment` | Manufacturing employment | persons | reported | **Yes**, as density denominator |
| `robot_density` | Robots per 10,000 manufacturing employees | ratio | derived | **Yes** |

The manufacturing employment series is subject to its own definitional instability and PRC reporting caveats, and carries the falsification-band treatment where it is GDP-linked or survey-derived. Registered breaks apply.

## Mandatory sensitivity reporting

Type D obligations. Every verdict is reported at:

1. Committed interpretation -- density growth **and** positive stock growth
2. Strict wording -- density growth alone
3. Manufacturing TFP where available, versus total-economy proxy
4. Alternative robot-density denominators, where the employment series has competing constructions

Where settings disagree, the verdict is `indeterminate` and the divergence is published.

---

## Tests

1. **Denominator trap.** A year with zero installations, contracting employment, and rising density must **fail** the committed interpretation and pass the strict wording, with both reported.
2. **Conjunctive contemporaneity.** Negative TFP in year t and qualifying robotics in year t+1 must not produce a qualifying year.
3. **Reset.** A single clearly non-qualifying year resets the run to zero.
4. **Gap suspension.** A gap year neither advances nor resets, and yields `indeterminate` where load-bearing.
5. **Recompute shortening.** A revision removing an early year's qualification shortens the recomputed run.
6. **Proxy ceiling.** A five-year qualifying run computed on total-economy TFP must emit `indeterminate`, never `triggered`.
7. **Deadline arithmetic.** A run beginning in 2037 cannot produce `triggered`.
8. **Structural break.** A break in the employment series is flagged and no bridging occurs without a committed rule.

Test 1 is the reason this adapter exists in its current form. Test 6 is the reason it cannot yet return its strongest verdict.

## Open items

- Identify a defensible manufacturing-specific TFP series for the PRC, or accept that F7 can only ever return `not_triggered` or `indeterminate`. The second outcome should be stated openly in the papers if it holds, because a falsifier that cannot fire is a weakened falsifier and the reader is entitled to know.
- Determine the manufacturing-employment series and its definitional boundary, including the treatment of migrant and informal employment.
- Establish the operational-stock methodology, including assumed retirement and service life, since stock is a modelled rather than counted quantity.
- Decide whether robot-density growth should be evaluated on a multi-year smoothed basis, given the volatility of annual installations. Provisional position: no smoothing, because the five-year persistence requirement already supplies it.
