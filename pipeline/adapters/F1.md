# F1 adapter specification

**Condition.** US generation-capacity additions converge on China's.

**Threshold.** US annual additions reach 40 percent of PRC annual additions, three-year rolling average.

**Deadline.** 2035.

**Type.** A -- direct quantitative. Fully automatable.

**Bears on.** P1 (conditional divergence) and P3 (segmented bipolarity). Maps to Pillar D, which is load-bearing, so a crossing here engages the pillar-concentration gate in `falsifiers/dependence.md`.

---

## Why this adapter is harder than it looks

F1 is a ratio of two national series. That makes it vulnerable to a failure mode that pure single-country conditions are not: the numerator and denominator can be measured on different definitional boundaries, producing a ratio that is arithmetically correct and substantively meaningless.

Three specific hazards, each of which must be closed by pre-commitment rather than discovered later.

### Hazard 1 -- Utility-scale versus grid-connected

US capacity-addition reporting conventionally covers **utility-scale** generators above a size threshold, excluding small-scale distributed solar. PRC reporting covers **grid-connected** capacity on a different basis. Comparing the two directly understates US additions by the whole of behind-the-meter solar, which is not a rounding error.

### Hazard 2 -- Gross additions versus net of retirements

Addition figures are frequently gross. Differencing consecutive year-end installed-capacity totals yields additions **net** of retirements. The US retires materially more thermal capacity than it did historically; China's fleet is younger. Mixing a gross numerator with a net denominator biases the ratio, and the bias direction is not constant across years.

### Hazard 3 -- Subset releases read as totals

PRC headline releases frequently report additions for a **subset** rather than the total fleet -- renewables additions, or wind-and-solar additions. Published 2024 figures include renewable additions of roughly 373 GW; published 2025 figures include wind and solar additions exceeding 430 GW. Neither is total net additions, and treating either as such would materially distort the denominator.

## Source design

### Primary: Ember yearly electricity data

Ember publishes harmonised yearly generation, capacity, emissions and demand data across more than 200 geographies via an open API, with coverage of 215 countries and 2025 yearly data available for the large majority of global demand. It is the primary source for **both** countries.

The reason is specific to F1's ratio structure: a single harmonised methodology applied to both numerator and denominator closes Hazard 1 by construction. Using each country's own national statistical agency for its own half of the ratio would reintroduce the mismatch the ratio is most vulnerable to.

### Cross-checks: EIA and NEA

EIA (for the US) and the National Energy Administration together with the National Bureau of Statistics (for the PRC) are ingested as **independent cross-checks**, not as inputs to the ratio. Their role is to raise `source_disagreement` when the harmonised series drifts from the national accounts beyond tolerance.

This inverts the intuitive design, in which national sources would be authoritative for their own country. The inversion is deliberate: for a ratio, internal consistency across the numerator and denominator matters more than fidelity to either national convention.

### Tolerance

Disagreement between Ember and the corresponding national source exceeding **5 percent** of the national figure, for total installed capacity in any year, raises `source_disagreement` and blocks automatic verdict emission for that year pending adjudication.

## Derivation

### Step 1 -- Net additions by differencing

For each country and each year t, from total installed capacity C:

```
A_t = C_t - C_{t-1}
```

Both values from the same source and, where possible, the same vintage. Records carry `basis: differenced` and `net_or_gross: net`.

Differencing is preferred over reported addition figures because it is symmetric across the two countries and because it yields net additions on both sides by construction, closing Hazard 2.

### Step 2 -- The rolling ratio

Two defensible constructions exist and they do not agree. The choice is **pre-committed here** rather than selected later.

**Committed: ratio of three-year sums.**

```
R_t = (A_US,t + A_US,t-1 + A_US,t-2) / (A_PRC,t + A_PRC,t-1 + A_PRC,t-2)
```

**Reported as sensitivity: mean of three annual ratios.**

```
R'_t = mean( A_US,t/A_PRC,t , A_US,t-1/A_PRC,t-1 , A_US,t-2/A_PRC,t-2 )
```

The ratio of sums is committed because it is robust to a single anomalous PRC year. The mean of ratios places large weight on any year with a small denominator, which over a decade of volatile build rates is a live risk rather than a theoretical one. Both are computed; the committed construction governs the verdict and the alternative is reported alongside it.

### Step 3 -- Verdict

```
triggered  if  R_t >= 0.40  for any evaluation year t <= 2035
```

A single year reaching the threshold triggers. The three-year averaging is already the smoothing mechanism; adding a persistence requirement on top would be double-counting and is not part of the pre-registered condition.

## Nameplate is permitted here, and only here

`DATA-INTEGRITY.md` requires energy inputs to be converted to dispatchable-adjusted output before entering any production function. **F1 is exempt**, because F1 is a test of build-rate convergence, not of effective energy availability, and the threshold was pre-registered against capacity additions.

The exemption is bounded and must not leak. The dispatchable conversion remains mandatory for every series feeding `Y_throughput`. F1's output is a falsifier verdict and is not an input to the capability vector.

This distinction should be stated explicitly wherever F1 figures appear in prose, because a reader who knows the dispatchable rule will otherwise reasonably suspect it has been forgotten.

---

## Known vintage discrepancy

PRC end-2024 total installed capacity is reported at approximately 3.35 billion kW. Year-on-year growth for that figure appears as **14.6 percent** in the contemporaneous national release and as **14.7 percent** in the source material underlying this programme's prospectus.

The discrepancy is small and almost certainly a rounding or base-revision artifact. It is recorded here anyway, for two reasons: it is the first concrete instance of the vintage-conflict machinery being needed, and an unexplained 0.1 percentage point that appears without comment in a published figure is exactly the kind of detail that costs credibility when a reader finds it independently.

Resolution rule: the differenced series computed from year-end totals governs. Published growth-rate percentages are treated as cross-checks, not as inputs.

## Tests

Required before the adapter is considered complete.

1. **Unit conversion.** PRC figures published in 100-million-kW units convert to GW correctly, including at scale boundaries.
2. **Differencing across a vintage boundary.** Where `C_t` and `C_{t-1}` come from different vintages, the flag is raised and the derivation records both vintages.
3. **Subset trap.** A renewables-only or wind-and-solar-only additions figure, if supplied as total additions, must fail validation rather than silently produce a low denominator.
4. **Tolerance breach.** A synthetic 6 percent Ember-versus-national divergence blocks verdict emission and raises `source_disagreement`.
5. **Idempotency.** Two consecutive runs against unchanged upstream data produce zero new observation records.
6. **Determinism.** Identical raw inputs and rule versions produce byte-identical derived output.
7. **Construction disagreement.** Where the committed ratio-of-sums and the sensitivity mean-of-ratios straddle 0.40, the verdict is `indeterminate` and both values are published.

Test 7 is the one that matters most. It is the case in which the pre-commitment made in Step 2 determines the verdict, and the honest response is to report that the verdict depends on a construction choice rather than to rely on the committed choice silently.

## Amendment 1 -- 2026-08-20 -- dry-run result registered; tolerance-basis defect; two open questions

**Appended, not substituted. No rule, threshold, window, source, or construction changes.** F1's live position is roughly 30 percentage points from its threshold and no pipeline verdict has yet been emitted from the primary series, so nothing this amendment registers can bear on a live determination. That distance is why the dry run was run now: the fix window on this adapter is open, and it closes as the ratio approaches 0.40.

### Dry-run result

The paired exercise at `../../falsifiers/adjudications/dry-run/F1-2003-2024.md` scored the committed derivation against evaluation year 2003 (expected-positive, the US gas-boom window) and evaluation year 2024 (negative control). **All three pre-named failure conditions did not occur.** The rubric returns `triggered` at R = 2.35 on the positive episode and `not_triggered` at R = 0.094 on the control, under both the committed ratio-of-sums and the sensitivity construction, and both verdicts survive substitution of the numerator source. F1's counting rules pass the discrimination test that F5 Sub-clause B failed.

The exercise cannot establish threshold-region sharpness: no historical evaluation year lands near 0.40 -- the record jumps from 0.67 to 0.27 between the 2005 and 2006 evaluations -- so adapter test 7 remains synthetically exercised only. This limitation is the dry run's, not the adapter's.

### Registered defect -- the source-tolerance rule has no committed comparison basis

```yaml
adapter_flags:
  - flag: tolerance_basis_unspecified
    rule: 5 percent Ember-versus-national divergence blocks automatic verdict emission
    registered: 2026-08-20
    basis: falsifiers/adjudications/dry-run/F1-2003-2024.md
    statement: >
      The tolerance rule does not fix the basis of the national comparison figure.
      Against EIA total net summer capacity -- the basis EIA headlines -- the 5
      percent tolerance is breached in 2000-2002 and in 2022-2024 (2025 sits at
      4.2 percent, inside it). A 2026 evaluation's three-year window contains the
      breaching years, so the rule as written blocks automatic verdict emission
      for the live window. The divergence is definitional (net summer versus
      installed), drifts with fleet composition, and flips sign as solar
      penetration grows.
    directionality: one-directional
    directionality_note: >
      This mode cannot flip a verdict. It can only convert an automatic verdict
      into an adjudicated one. False-alarm risk is demonstrated; verdict-error
      risk through this channel is nil.
```

**Required before first ingest, registered now while inert:** the cross-check must be committed as a basis-matched pair -- Ember installed capacity against an EIA installed/nameplate-basis total, and the PRC equivalent -- with the chosen EIA series and PRC series named in `definitional_boundary` at first fetch. The 5 percent figure itself is **not** revisited; only the series it compares. Selecting the comparison series is a decision statable entirely without reference to any verdict, since every candidate pairing leaves both dry-run verdicts and the live ~30-point distance unchanged.

### Open questions registered, not resolved

1. **Negative net additions.** US net additions are negative in 2015 on both sources. The committed construction absorbs a negative year inside a positive three-year sum, but the semantics of a negative three-year numerator sum are unstated, and the sensitivity mean-of-ratios admits negative annual ratios undamped. To be resolved before first ingest; no historical evaluation year is affected.
2. **`cap_total_installed` does not exist as a published Ember row.** Totals must be derived by summing the nine fuel-level rows; the summation rule must be committed at first ingest, and the sum checked against Ember's Clean-plus-Fossil aggregates.

### Test additions

8. **Tolerance basis mismatch is detected.** A cross-check comparison whose `definitional_boundary` does not match the primary series' basis must fail validation rather than silently raise or suppress `source_disagreement`.
9. **Negative-sum handling.** Once the semantics are committed under open question 1, a synthetic window with a negative three-year numerator must produce the committed behaviour and publish both constructions.

## Amendment 2 -- 2026-08-20 -- pre-ingest decisions committed: basis-matched cross-check pair, negative-sum semantics, fuel-row summation rule

**Appended, not substituted. No threshold, window, deadline, or ratio-construction changes.** All three selections were made by author decision of 2026-08-20 in structured Q&A, candidates and grounds recorded below. Each is provably inert for every existing determination: the two dry-run verdicts (R = 2.35 and R = 0.094) and the live ~30-percentage-point distance to threshold are unchanged under every candidate considered, so no selection here can be reverse-engineered from a preferred outcome. This closes the three items Amendment 1 registered as required before first ingest.

### Decision 1 -- the cross-check pair is committed on a nameplate/installed basis, both countries

**US: EIA nameplate-basis annual total** (Electric Power Annual, existing-capacity table, nameplate column; the exact table identifier and vintage are confirmed and snapshotted at first fetch, and recorded in `definitional_boundary` as `basis: nameplate`). Ground: Ember's series is installed capacity; a nameplate comparator matches it by definition, so the 5 percent rule measures data disagreement rather than the definitional drift Amendment 1 demonstrated (net summer diverges systematically, with sign flipping as solar penetration grows).

**PRC: NEA/NBS year-end grid-connected installed capacity** -- the national year-end series already carried in this adapter's vintage-discrepancy note (the ~3.35 billion kW figure for end-2024), recorded in `definitional_boundary` as `basis: grid_connected_installed`. Ground: same nameplate-family basis, and the only official national total. Proximity noted while inert: the Ember-versus-NEA divergence is 4.7 percent for end-2024, inside the 5 percent tolerance but near it. A future breach converts an automatic verdict into an adjudicated one and cannot flip a verdict (Amendment 1's directionality note carries over unchanged).

**Candidates considered and declined.** US: EIA-860 generator-level nameplate sum (exact, but heavy ingest for what is only a cross-check; remains available as an adjudication input); retaining net summer with a committed derate adjustment (the adjustment would drift with fleet composition -- this reintroduces the defect the decision exists to close); dropping the US cross-check (weakens the source-disagreement design). PRC: CEC annual statistics (secondary industry body, near-identical to NEA in practice; remains available as an adjudication input); an NEA-primary/CEC-tie-break rule (extra machinery without a demonstrated need).

**The 5 percent figure itself is not revisited**, per Amendment 1. The `tolerance_basis_unspecified` flag remains open until first fetch records both series in `definitional_boundary`; the decision it was waiting on is made here.

### Decision 2 -- negative-sum semantics

- **Negative three-year numerator sum**: the verdict is `not_triggered` -- negative additions satisfy no "reach 40 percent" threshold and no defensible `triggered` reading exists -- and a mandatory `negative_numerator` flag is raised, so that "US additions were negative" is published as a finding distinct from "US additions were X percent of China's". The negative ratio value is published as computed, not floored.
- **Sensitivity construction (mean of ratios)**: negative annual ratios are clamped to zero for the reported sensitivity value; the undamped mean is published alongside it. This bounds the single-year leverage Amendment 1 identified without hiding it.
- **Negative or zero three-year denominator sum**: the ratio is undefined or sign-inverted; the verdict is `indeterminate` pending adjudication. No PRC year in the observed record is within an order of magnitude of this case.

Candidates declined: flooring the numerator at zero (loses the sign information in the published ratio itself); `indeterminate` on any negative numerator sum (over-cautious -- the substantive reading of negative US additions against this threshold is unambiguous).

### Decision 3 -- `cap_total_installed` summation rule

`cap_total_installed` is derived per country-year as the sum of the nine fuel-level capacity rows under a **pinned whitelist**, filter `Category = Capacity`, `Subcategory = Fuel`:

```
Bioenergy, Coal, Gas, Hydro, Nuclear, Other Fossil, Other Renewables, Solar, Wind
```

The sum is validated against Ember's `Aggregate fuel` rows: `Clean + Fossil` must match the whitelist sum within rounding. An unrecognised fuel variable appearing in a future vintage, a whitelist member absent, or an aggregate mismatch **fails validation** rather than silently changing the definitional basis between vintages. Verified on the committed snapshot (sha256 259e1095...) across all 52 country-years in scope, matching within rounding throughout.

Candidates declined: using the Clean-plus-Fossil aggregate rows directly as the total (commits the measurand to derived rows Ember could redefine; they serve better as the check); an open sum without a whitelist (a new fuel category would silently change the basis between vintages).

### Test additions

10. **Whitelist violation.** A capacity fuel row whose `Variable` is outside the pinned nine-name whitelist, or a missing whitelist member, fails validation.
11. **Aggregate mismatch.** A whitelist sum that disagrees with the Clean-plus-Fossil aggregate beyond rounding fails validation.
12. **Negative denominator.** A synthetic window with a non-positive three-year denominator sum returns `indeterminate` and publishes both constructions. (Test 9's negative-numerator case is now specified by Decision 2 and must produce `not_triggered` plus the `negative_numerator` flag.)

### Remaining before first ingest

Only confirmations, no decisions: the exact EIA table identifier for the nameplate series (snapshot at first fetch), Ember's PRC basis confirmation against the NEA series (adapter open item, unchanged), and the recording of both cross-check series in `definitional_boundary`.

### Two semantics corrections, found by the committed tests, both conservative

1. **Whitelist-absence semantics (Decision 3 correction).** Amendment 2's letter said a whitelist member absent fails validation. Ember omits rows for fuels a country has none of (first observed: CHN 2022 has no Other Renewables row), and the 52-country-year verification that grounded Decision 3 summed present rows. Corrected to match the verified procedure: an absent member is an implicit zero **only while the Clean-plus-Fossil aggregate check passes**; an unknown row, or an absent member with a failing aggregate check, still fails. The values Decision 3 verified are unchanged.
2. **Subset-trap floor no longer fires on negative additions** (test 9 collision). A subset of additions is non-negative by construction, so a negative differenced value cannot be a subset read -- it is a genuine retirements-exceed-additions year (US 2015, -2.2 GW) and routes to Decision 2's `negative_numerator` semantics. The floor now applies to values in `[0, floor)` only. Without this correction the committed negative-sum semantics were unreachable: the trap blocked every negative year before Decision 2 could classify it.
