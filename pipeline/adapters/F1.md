# F1 adapter specification

**Condition.** US generation-capacity additions converge on China's.

**Threshold.** US annual additions reach 40 percent of PRC annual additions, three-year rolling average.

**Deadline.** 2035.

**Type.** A -- direct quantitative. Fully automatable.

**Bears on.** P1 (conditional divergence) and P3 (segmented bipolarity). Maps to Pillar D, which is load-bearing, so a crossing here engages the pillar-concentration gate in `falsifiers/dependence.md`.

---

## Why this adapter is harder than it looks

F1 is a ratio of two national series. That makes it vulnerable to a failure mode that pure single-country conditions are not: the numerator and denominator can be measured on different definitional boundaries, producing a ratio that is arithmetically correct and substantively meaningless.

Three specific hazards, each of which must be closed by pre-commitment rather than discovered later.

### Hazard 1 -- Utility-scale versus grid-connected

US capacity-addition reporting conventionally covers **utility-scale** generators above a size threshold, excluding small-scale distributed solar. PRC reporting covers **grid-connected** capacity on a different basis. Comparing the two directly understates US additions by the whole of behind-the-meter solar, which is not a rounding error.

### Hazard 2 -- Gross additions versus net of retirements

Addition figures are frequently gross. Differencing consecutive year-end installed-capacity totals yields additions **net** of retirements. The US retires materially more thermal capacity than it did historically; China's fleet is younger. Mixing a gross numerator with a net denominator biases the ratio, and the bias direction is not constant across years.

### Hazard 3 -- Subset releases read as totals

PRC headline releases frequently report additions for a **subset** rather than the total fleet -- renewables additions, or wind-and-solar additions. Published 2024 figures include renewable additions of roughly 373 GW; published 2025 figures include wind and solar additions exceeding 430 GW. Neither is total net additions, and treating either as such would materially distort the denominator.

## Source design

### Primary: Ember yearly electricity data

Ember publishes harmonised yearly generation, capacity, emissions and demand data across more than 200 geographies via an open API, with coverage of 215 countries and 2025 yearly data available for the large majority of global demand. It is the primary source for **both** countries.

The reason is specific to F1's ratio structure: a single harmonised methodology applied to both numerator and denominator closes Hazard 1 by construction. Using each country's own national statistical agency for its own half of the ratio would reintroduce the mismatch the ratio is most vulnerable to.

### Cross-checks: EIA and NEA

EIA (for the US) and the National Energy Administration together with the National Bureau of Statistics (for the PRC) are ingested as **independent cross-checks**, not as inputs to the ratio. Their role is to raise `source_disagreement` when the harmonised series drifts from the national accounts beyond tolerance.

This inverts the intuitive design, in which national sources would be authoritative for their own country. The inversion is deliberate: for a ratio, internal consistency across the numerator and denominator matters more than fidelity to either national convention.

### Tolerance

Disagreement between Ember and the corresponding national source exceeding **5 percent** of the national figure, for total installed capacity in any year, raises `source_disagreement` and blocks automatic verdict emission for that year pending adjudication.

## Derivation

### Step 1 -- Net additions by differencing

For each country and each year t, from total installed capacity C:

```
A_t = C_t - C_{t-1}
```

Both values from the same source and, where possible, the same vintage. Records carry `basis: differenced` and `net_or_gross: net`.

Differencing is preferred over reported addition figures because it is symmetric across the two countries and because it yields net additions on both sides by construction, closing Hazard 2.

### Step 2 -- The rolling ratio

Two defensible constructions exist and they do not agree. The choice is **pre-committed here** rather than selected later.

**Committed: ratio of three-year sums.**

```
R_t = (A_US,t + A_US,t-1 + A_US,t-2) / (A_PRC,t + A_PRC,t-1 + A_PRC,t-2)
```

**Reported as sensitivity: mean of three annual ratios.**

```
R'_t = mean( A_US,t/A_PRC,t , A_US,t-1/A_PRC,t-1 , A_US,t-2/A_PRC,t-2 )
```

The ratio of sums is committed because it is robust to a single anomalous PRC year. The mean of ratios places large weight on any year with a small denominator, which over a decade of volatile build rates is a live risk rather than a theoretical one. Both are computed; the committed construction governs the verdict and the alternative is reported alongside it.

### Step 3 -- Verdict

```
triggered  if  R_t >= 0.40  for any evaluation year t <= 2035
```

A single year reaching the threshold triggers. The three-year averaging is already the smoothing mechanism; adding a persistence requirement on top would be double-counting and is not part of the pre-registered condition.

## Nameplate is permitted here, and only here

`DATA-INTEGRITY.md` requires energy inputs to be converted to dispatchable-adjusted output before entering any production function. **F1 is exempt**, because F1 is a test of build-rate convergence, not of effective energy availability, and the threshold was pre-registered against capacity additions.

The exemption is bounded and must not leak. The dispatchable conversion remains mandatory for every series feeding `Y_throughput`. F1's output is a falsifier verdict and is not an input to the capability vector.

This distinction should be stated explicitly wherever F1 figures appear in prose, because a reader who knows the dispatchable rule will otherwise reasonably suspect it has been forgotten.

## Known vintage discrepancy

PRC end-2024 total installed capacity is reported at approximately 3.35 billion kW. Year-on-year growth for that figure appears as **14.6 percent** in the contemporaneous national release and as **14.7 percent** in the source material underlying this programme's prospectus.

The discrepancy is small and almost certainly a rounding or base-revision artifact. It is recorded here anyway, for two reasons: it is the first concrete instance of the vintage-conflict machinery being needed, and an unexplained 0.1 percentage point that appears without comment in a published figure is exactly the kind of detail that costs credibility when a reader finds it independently.

Resolution rule: the differenced series computed from year-end totals governs. Published growth-rate percentages are treated as cross-checks, not as inputs.

## Illustrative baseline

From secondary figures carried in the prospectus, pending re-derivation from primary series at first ingest:

| Year | US additions (GW) | PRC additions (GW) | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 | 48.6 | ~429 | ~11.3% |
| 2025 | 53 | ~540 | ~9.8% |

Against a 40 percent threshold, F1 is currently far from triggering, and the ratio moved **away** from the threshold between 2024 and 2025 despite 2025 being the largest single-year US capacity addition since 2002.

These figures are illustrative only. They span two years rather than three, so no rolling average is defined, and they are drawn from secondary reporting rather than from the primary series. The first pipeline run supersedes them. They are recorded so that the first run has something to be checked against.

## Tests

Required before the adapter is considered complete.

1. **Unit conversion.** PRC figures published in 100-million-kW units convert to GW correctly, including at scale boundaries.
2. **Differencing across a vintage boundary.** Where `C_t` and `C_{t-1}` come from different vintages, the flag is raised and the derivation records both vintages.
3. **Subset trap.** A renewables-only or wind-and-solar-only additions figure, if supplied as total additions, must fail validation rather than silently produce a low denominator.
4. **Tolerance breach.** A synthetic 6 percent Ember-versus-national divergence blocks verdict emission and raises `source_disagreement`.
5. **Idempotency.** Two consecutive runs against unchanged upstream data produce zero new observation records.
6. **Determinism.** Identical raw inputs and rule versions produce byte-identical derived output.
7. **Construction disagreement.** Where the committed ratio-of-sums and the sensitivity mean-of-ratios straddle 0.40, the verdict is `indeterminate` and both values are published.

Test 7 is the one that matters most. It is the case in which the pre-commitment made in Step 2 determines the verdict, and the honest response is to report that the verdict depends on a construction choice rather than to rely on the committed choice silently.
