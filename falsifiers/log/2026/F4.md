# F4 -- 2026

**Condition.** AI raises rather than lowers within-occupation wage dispersion.
**Threshold.** p90/p10 rises in high-AI-exposure occupations across 10 countries, by 2032.
**Verdict.** `indeterminate`

## Why indeterminate

The condition names as its source a **successor** to a 2024 study. No qualifying successor has been identified as of this entry.

Per the framework, `indeterminate` must name its own resolution condition. It is: publication of a study applying a comparable occupational AI-exposure measure to wage-dispersion outcomes across at least ten countries overlapping the baseline sample.

Whether that will occur is outside this project's control. If it does not occur by 2032, F4 is permanently indeterminate and the operative falsifier set is seven conditions, not eight.

## Baseline against which the reversal would be measured

Recorded so the comparison is fixed rather than reconstructed later:

- No effect of AI exposure on **between**-occupation wage inequality over the studied period
- Wage inequality **declined** most in the most-exposed occupations
- A one-standard-deviation increase in occupational exposure associated with approximately **1.5 percentage points lower** growth in the p90/p10 ratio
- The compression finding did **not** extend to reducing wage gaps by gender or age

The last point is retained deliberately, because it is the limitation most likely to be dropped when the finding is summarised.

## Fallback status

Self-construction from national wage microdata remains an uncommitted option. No commitment has been made and no replication code exists. Under the fallback gate, a self-constructed measure cannot feed a verdict absent a committed diff predating the evaluation year.

## Negative-check record

This entry constitutes the 2026 negative check. Successor-study checks are logged annually including when they find nothing, so that the absence of a source is documented over time rather than inferred from silence.
