# F2 -- 2026

**Condition.** US federal discretionary fiscal capacity recovers.
**Threshold.** Non-interest discretionary outlays exceed 6.0% of GDP without a debt crisis, by 2036.
**Verdict.** `not_triggered`

## Clause 1 -- quantitative

**Determination.** `not_met`

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Discretionary outlays, 2026 | ~5.9% of GDP |
| Defense component | ~2.8% |
| Nondefense component | ~3.1% |
| Projected 2036 | ~4.8% |
| Threshold | 6.0% |

The 2026 figure is described in the source as the lowest discretionary share since 1962.

**The outturn-only rule binds here immediately.** The 2026 value is a current-year estimate within a projection publication, not elapsed outturn. Under the committed rule it is **structurally incapable** of satisfying Clause 1 regardless of its value. It is recorded for monitoring only.

This is the rule's first live application, and it is worth noting that it operates against the falsifier rather than for it: a 6.1% estimate would still yield `not_met`.

**Distance.** Approximately 0.1 percentage points on the estimate. F2 is the second-nearest condition to its threshold in the set.

**Direction.** The projected path declines throughout the window, so on the current baseline F2 becomes harder to satisfy over time. Near on level, far on trend.

## Clause 2 -- qualitative

**Determination.** `met` (default, per the committed negative-clause exception)

No crisis indicator identified for 2026: no failed benchmark auction, no emergency central-bank Treasury-market intervention, no missed federal debt payment, no documented sustained market-functioning impairment.

**Adverse case.** Required by the framework and **not yet authored**. This entry is therefore incomplete against its own specification. Recorded as a deficiency to be closed at the 2027 review rather than backfilled silently.

## Flags

`preliminary`, `basis_mixed`

## Next review

2027. Clause 2 requires annual adjudication with a written adverse case regardless of Clause 1, because the crossing could occur with one year's notice.

## Amendment 1 -- 2026-08-19 -- Clause 2 upgraded from default to adjudicated determination

**Appended. Nothing above is edited.**

### What changed

Clause 2 was recorded as `met` **by default**, under the committed negative-clause exception, with the required adverse case explicitly not authored and the entry flagged as incomplete against its own specification.

That deficiency is now closed. A formal adjudication has been authored: `falsifiers/adjudications/2026/F2-C2.md`.

**Determination.** `met` -- no debt crisis in the evaluation window, on affirmative evidence against each of the four pre-registered indicators, with the adverse case written first and published in full.

**The determination did not change. Its standing did.** A default carries no evidentiary weight; this one does.

### Direction of the finding

Clause 2 is a negative qualifier. `met` means no crisis, which makes F2 **easier** to trigger. Establishing it on evidence therefore strengthens the falsifier rather than the thesis, and the record should be read that way.

### Combined verdict, unchanged

Clause 1 is `not_met` on the outturn-only rule, and a quantitative `not_met` short-circuits the conjunction. **F2 remains `not_triggered`.**

### The four indicators, each checked affirmatively

| Indicator | Finding | Weakest sourced reading |
|---|---|---|
| Failed benchmark auction | None | 20-year bond, 21 May 2025: bid-to-cover 2.46, dealer takedown 16.91% ([TreasuryDirect](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/instit/annceresult/press/preanre/2025/R_20250521_2.pdf)) |
| Emergency Fed intervention | None | Runoff ended 1 Dec 2025 **as pre-announced**; SRF usage $74.6bn at 31 Dec 2025 is a standing facility used as designed ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/banks-tap-record-liquidity-new-york-feds-standing-repo-facility-2025-12-31/)) |
| Missed federal debt payment | None | Debt limit binding ~6 months of 2025, raised 4 Jul 2025 before extraordinary measures were exhausted ([CRS IN10837](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IN10837)) |
| Sustained functioning impairment | Acute, reversed | April 2025 depth to ~one-quarter of recent levels, recovered by late summer 2025 and trending higher through Feb 2026 ([Liberty Street Economics](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/04/treasury-market-liquidity-since-april-2025/)) |

The finding that carries most of the fourth row: **the cash-futures basis trade did not unwind.** OFR, holding the repo data, records that "a material unwind of this trade did not occur in April 2025", with hedge fund Treasury-collateral repo borrowing "at least $1.4 trillion with no large declines in April or May", and borrowing against cheapest-to-deliver issues **rising** ([OFR 2025 Annual Report](https://www.financialresearch.gov/annual-reports/files/OFR-AR-2025.pdf)). What unwound was the swap-spread trade, roughly $60bn in April and $40bn in May ([Federal Reserve FEDS Notes](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/decomposing-hedge-funds-u-s-treasury-exposures-20260622.html)).

### The adverse case, summarised

Published in full in the adjudication. Its core: Moody's downgraded the United States Aaa to Aa1 on 16 May 2025, completing the loss of triple-A across all three agencies ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/markets/us/moodys-downgrades-us-aa1-rating-2025-05-16/)); CBO's own baseline has debt reaching 120% of GDP by 2036, past the 1946 record of 106%, with interest costs more than doubling to $2.1tn ([CBO](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/62105)); the 30-year yield passed 5% in August 2026, highest since 2007, with the term premium near a twelve-year high ([Reuters, 18 Aug 2026](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/selling-grips-bond-markets-us-japan-inflation-fiscal-worries-take-hold-2026-08-18/)); and the BIS describes the marginal buyer of government bonds as a leveraged, repo-funded intermediary operating around dealer balance-sheet constraints ([BIS](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt2512_foreword.htm)).

**The two cases barely overlap in claim.** The no-crisis case is about market functioning and is supported by measured data held by the institutions that produce it. The crisis case is about the fiscal trajectory and the level of rates and is supported by CBO's baseline. Nothing in the first contradicts the second's projections; nothing in the second identifies a functioning failure.

### A pre-registration limitation, recorded and not repaired

The four indicators operationalise "debt crisis" as a **payments and plumbing** failure. On that definition the qualifier returns `met` in a world of sharply rising debt-service costs and record debt, provided the plumbing holds -- which is the world the evaluation window describes. A qualifier written about sustainability rather than functioning would score the same evidence differently.

That choice was made in advance. It is not amended now. A reader who thinks it was the wrong choice is disagreeing with the pre-registration, which is the disagreement the pre-registration is there to make possible.
