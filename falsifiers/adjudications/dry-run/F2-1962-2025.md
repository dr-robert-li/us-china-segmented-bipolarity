# F2 dry run -- Clause 1 against the full outturn record, 1962-2025

**Nothing in this file is a finding about the present**, and per the committed flag-only scope for F2 (fix window shut at ~0.1pp), nothing here changes any rule or any live determination. Findings are carried as flags and a shadow value.

**Outcome, stated at the head: the mirror image of F6, with a live edge F6 does not have.** Clause 1's qualifying state -- discretionary outlays exceeding 6.0 percent of GDP -- has obtained in **63 of the 64 outturn years in the record**; the single exception, 1999, sits exactly at the boundary (6.0, which does not exceed 6.0). The threshold lies at the very bottom of the observed range. And unlike F6, this cuts live: the latest **elapsed** outturn (FY2025, 6.2 percent on the cross-check basis) exceeds the threshold while Clause 2 is already adjudicated `met` for 2026. Under one defensible reading of the committed rules, F2 is **triggered now**. The binding determination is untouched; the reading is registered as a prospective candidate and its shadow value published under the standing rule.

## The adverse case, written first

**Against the exercise's usefulness.** The finding could be dismissed as a wording quibble: everyone understands "recovers" to mean *returns above 6.0 after the projected decline materialises*, and the projected path (5.9 in 2026 falling to 5.0 by 2031) shows the decline coming. On that intent-reading, the exercise attacks a strawman. **Response:** pre-registration binds to the registered words, not the intended trajectory -- this project's whole architecture depends on that -- and the registered words test a level, not a recovery-after-decline path. If intent is allowed to narrow a clause after registration, F5-B's anchoring could have been "intended" too. The tension between the wording and the evident intent is itself the finding, and it is recorded rather than resolved, because resolving it now in either direction would move a live clause.

**Against the shadow value.** The OMB basis is not the committed series; CBO's figure for FY2025 could differ. Conceded: the shadow is labelled cross-check-basis, and its confirmation on the committed series is the registered resolution condition. The gap between OMB and CBO on this category is historically about a tenth of a point, and the FY2025 margin over threshold is two tenths.

## The arithmetic

Source: [OMB Historical Tables, Table 8.4](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/hist08z4_fy2027.xlsx) -- snapshot `sha256:abe50c2e6a6393742501cee4c3d9577652603b6beae0546667dbe57d0c59cd88`; [landing page](https://www.whitehouse.gov/omb/information-resources/budget/historical-tables/) -- snapshot `sha256:6626f5990d64703e7389138b997868445755eb0314099af4ac3c0561d7be2847`. Discretionary total, percent of GDP, outturn years 1962--2025 (n = 64; 2026-2031 rows are estimates and are excluded from outturn arithmetic).

```
outturn range:            6.0 (1999) to 13.1 (1968)
years exceeding 6.0:      63 of 64
boundary year:            1999 at exactly 6.0 -- does not exceed
recent outturn:           2023 6.3, 2024 6.3, 2025 6.2
estimates (excluded):     2026 5.9, 2027 5.9, 2028 5.7, 2029 5.5, 2030 5.3, 2031 5.0
```

The 2026 estimate of 5.9 matches the figure the 2026 annual log recorded from the committed CBO source, corroborating cross-basis closeness, and is consistent with that source's description of 2026 as the lowest discretionary share since 1962: on outturn, **no year below 6.0 has yet occurred**.

### Against the failure conditions

Degenerate by construction: with no negative-control year in existence, only failure condition 1 (`not_met` on an expected-positive) is evaluable, and it does not occur -- every elapsed year except 1999 satisfies Clause 1's arithmetic. The rubric's boundary rule behaves correctly on 1999: 6.0 does not exceed 6.0. Discrimination at the threshold is untestable for the F6 reason inverted -- history never visits the other side.

## Findings

### Finding 1 -- `threshold_outside_observed_range`, inverted polarity

F6's threshold has never been reached; F2's has essentially never **not** been reached. A falsifier whose qualifying state is the 63-of-64-year historical norm does not measure "recovery" -- it measures whether the projected decline has arrived yet. The two conditions are mirror images, and together they say something about the set's calibration the papers must carry: two of the eight thresholds sit outside the observed ranges of their own measurands, in opposite directions.

### Finding 2 -- the evaluation-year anchoring is unspecified, and it is currently verdict-determining

The adapter commits "the threshold is tested on outturn only" but never states **which** outturn an evaluation year tests. The 2026 log evaluated the 2026 current-year estimate (correctly excluded as non-outturn) and did not evaluate any elapsed year. Two readings are defensible:

1. **Evaluation-year reading** (the log's implicit practice): evaluation year t tests year t's own value, which is not outturn until t has elapsed; Clause 1 `not_met` in 2026.
2. **Latest-elapsed-outturn reading**: evaluation year t tests the most recent elapsed fiscal year; for 2026 that is FY2025 at 6.2 (cross-check basis), Clause 1 `met` -- and with Clause 2 adjudicated `met`, **F2 `triggered`**.

This is the F5-B defect class exactly -- an anchoring left unspecified, discovered when it became outcome-determining -- with the difference that here the unstated choice currently protects the **thesis** (F2 firing would mandate revision of its pillar). Under the direction-neutral prohibition, reading 2 cannot be adopted now, for the same reason F5-B-ANCHOR-2 could not be: adopting a rule while it determines a live verdict is fitting the rule to the outcome, whichever way it cuts. It is registered as a candidate rule, prospective from 2027, with its shadow value published immediately under the standing shadow-publication rule.

### Finding 3 -- the wording-versus-intent tension, recorded not resolved

"Recovers" implies a prior decline that has, on outturn, not yet occurred. The registered clause as literally worded was satisfied on outturn in the registration year itself. The pre-registration registered a falsifier whose quantitative clause was true at registration, obscured by evaluating a current-year estimate. That sentence is uncomfortable and it belongs in the papers' calibration statement verbatim.
