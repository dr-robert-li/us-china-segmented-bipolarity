# F6 dry run -- 1999-2003 favourable episode, 2009-2013 adverse episode

**Nothing in this file is a finding about the present.** This is a rubric exercise under the conventions of `README.md` in this directory, applying the F6 adapter's committed coding rules to closed historical windows. It is not a determination, it does not appear in the annual index, and it does not bind any live verdict.

**Outcome, stated at the head because it is of a different kind than F1's or F5's: the discrimination test is untestable for F6.** The committed series has never, in its entire 1990--2026 published span, been in the state the threshold describes. No expected-positive episode exists to test against. What the exercise establishes instead is a calibration finding sharper than the adapter's existing "demanding falsifier" note, an ordering validation of the measurand, and closure of an open item. The dry-run score across the set becomes: one clause failed (F5-B), one passed (F1), one untestable (F6), five untested.

---

## Design and registration, with the sequence disclosed in full

**Sequence disclosure.** The registered-exercises table in `README.md` deliberately left F6's episode pair unfixed, because fixing a pair requires series reconnaissance. That reconnaissance -- fetching and reading the committed series -- necessarily revealed the historical annual means, including the fact that no year approaches the threshold, **before** the episode pair below was registered. This exercise is therefore **not a blind discrimination test and does not claim to be one.** It is registered as a characterisation exercise: the standard failure conditions are evaluated where they are constructible, and where they are not, the reason is itself the finding. All verdict computations below were run after this head was written; the reconnaissance quantities that preceded registration are identified as such.

| Element | Committed |
|---|---|
| Falsifier | F6 -- graduate underemployment falls below 30 percent for five consecutive years, by 2035 |
| Committed series | NY Fed underemployment rate for recent college graduates (aged 22--27, bachelor's or higher, not enrolled), monthly, seasonally adjusted, three-month moving average, January 1990 -- June 2026 |
| Favourable episode | **1999--2003**, centred on the strongest recent-graduate labour market in the record: recent-graduate unemployment reached its series low in 2000 (2.77 percent annual mean) |
| Adverse episode | **2009--2013**, the post-financial-crisis trough: recent-graduate unemployment reached its post-crisis peak in 2010 (6.99 percent annual mean). The series' all-time high is the pandemic year 2020 at 7.98 percent, a spike rather than a sustained trough; the episode was selected on the underemployment measurand, where 2010--2013 is the sustained maximum |
| Failure condition 1 | The rubric qualifies no year of the favourable episode **and** a reversal of elite overproduction is independently established to have occurred there -- the F5-B pattern, an event the rubric cannot see |
| Failure condition 2 | The rubric qualifies any year of the adverse episode |
| Failure condition 3 | The rubric returns the same verdict on both episodes **and** the two episodes are established to differ on the condition the clause describes |

Conditions 1 and 3 are written conditionally because the episodes are known to differ in labour-market strength, but whether either constitutes a *reversal of elite overproduction* -- the condition F6 tests -- is exactly what cannot be assumed. That distinction turns out to carry the whole result.

### The known answers, and what they are answers about

The two episodes' relative ordering is independently established: 2000 is the strongest graduate labour market in the record -- the companion recent-graduate unemployment series' full-span annual minimum, 2.77 percent -- and 2009--2013 the weakest sustained stretch, with the post-crisis peak of 6.99 percent in 2010 (the single higher annual value, 7.98 percent in 2020, is a one-year pandemic spike, not a sustained trough), corroborated by NBER dating carried in the data file itself. **But no source known to this project claims that elite overproduction -- the structural condition -- reversed in 1999--2003.** The dot-com boom improved graduate outcomes at the margin; the structural-demographic literature the pillar rests on dates elite overproduction as intensifying continuously from the 1970s. The favourable episode is therefore an episode of *relatively good outcomes*, not a known positive instance of the condition. F6 differs from F5 in precisely this: the 1976 coup occurred and the F5-B rubric missed it; a reversal of elite overproduction has, on the available record, never occurred for any rubric to miss.

## The adverse case, written first

**Against the exercise's usefulness.** Three objections, in ascending order of force. First, the registration is reconnaissance-contaminated: the author knew no year qualified before registering the pair, so the exercise cannot surprise its author and its "outcome" was known at registration. Second, the ordering validation is weak tea -- showing that an underemployment series is lower in a boom than a bust validates almost nothing about a threshold rule. Third, and strongest: an exercise that concludes "untestable" produces no discrimination evidence at all, so F6 emerges neither validated nor invalidated, and the file could be read as elaborate throat-clearing around a single number (37.8) that reconnaissance produced in five minutes.

**Response, conceding most of it.** The first objection is true and is why the sequence is disclosed rather than laundered into a claim of blindness. The second is true. The third is conceded as to discrimination -- and resisted as to value, on one ground: the single number is not the finding. The finding is the *category*: a pre-registered falsifier whose qualifying state lies outside the entire observed range of its committed measurand is a different object from one that is merely far from threshold, because its distance cannot be interpreted as "current conditions are far from the trigger" -- there are no known conditions under which the measurand produces the trigger state. That category did not exist in this project's vocabulary before this exercise (F1 passed, F5-B failed; both were testable), and it changes what the papers must say about the eight-condition set's composition. An untestable falsifier discovered by a dry run is the dry-run programme working, not failing.

**Against the verdicts.** No serious case exists that any year 1990--2025 should qualify: the closest annual mean misses by 7.8 points on a series whose observed annual-mean range is 9.6 points wide. The vintage objection from the F1 exercise applies with less force here because the series is a single continuously-published product, but a real cousin of it does: the current file reflects the current occupational-requirements database and survey reweighting applied backward, and the 2001 trough as measured today (37.8) is not necessarily the value a 2001 evaluator would have computed. No pre-revision vintage was obtained to check.

### A precision discrepancy, recorded

The adapter's precision rule states the series "is published to one decimal place". The data file carries **three** decimals (e.g. `37.833`); the headline figures on the page are one-decimal. The discrepancy is recorded and is nowhere verdict-relevant: at any rounding, no value in the file is within six points of the boundary. The governing reading adopted for this exercise: qualification compares the calendar-year mean of the file's values against 30.0, and the strict-inequality boundary rule makes further precision moot at every observed distance.

### Partial year

2026 has six observations. Under the adapter's rules a partial year is not a calendar-year mean; 2026 is excluded from qualification and shown for context only.

### Ordering validation -- what the series can be tested on

The measurand orders the independently-known episodes correctly: the series minimum (annual 37.833, monthly 36.571) falls in 2001 at the dot-com employment peak, corroborated by the companion unemployment series' low of 2.77 percent in 2000; the maxima fall in 1992 (47.460) and 2010--2013 (45.4--46.5), the two worst sustained graduate markets in the span, corroborated by unemployment at 4.82 and 6.99 percent (the 2020 pandemic spike, unemployment 7.98, produces no comparable underemployment maximum -- 40.97 -- which is itself consistent with a shutdown shock rather than a mismatch trough). A ten-point swing on the committed measure tracks the known cycle in the right direction with sensible timing. The measurand is measuring something real; the threshold is simply outside its range.

## Findings

### Finding 1 -- F6's discrimination is untestable, which is a third outcome category

A dry run tests whether a rubric separates a known-positive from a known-negative. F6 has no known positive: the condition it tests has not occurred within the observed span of its committed series, and the trigger state has never been observed in the measurand at any point in 36 years. This is not a rubric failure -- no event was missed, which distinguishes it categorically from F5-B -- and it is not a pass. It is registered as `discrimination_untestable`, resolvable only by history supplying a qualifying state.

### Finding 2 -- the calibration finding, quantified: the threshold lies outside the observed range of its own series

The adapter already records F6 as "demanding". The exercise sharpens that to a statement with numbers: the threshold sits **7.8 points below the lowest annual mean, and 6.6 points below the lowest single month, ever recorded** on the committed series -- a series whose observed annual-mean range spans 9.6 points (monthly range 11.9). The distance from the threshold to the series' best year is nearly as large as the distance from its best year to its worst. Combined with the five-year persistence requirement and the 2031--2035 latest qualifying window, F6 triggers only if the series moves further below its all-time low than the distance from that low to its all-time high implies is ordinary, and holds there for half a decade, beginning within roughly five years. Pillar A's guard condition is, on the observed behaviour of its own measurand, close to unfalsifiable in practice. Registered against the adapter as `threshold_outside_observed_range`; joins F5-B's false-negative mode and F7's missing-series status in the papers' calibration statement. Recalibration remains prohibited; disclosure is the remedy.

### Finding 3 -- the machinery behaves as specified, trivially

Annual-mean construction, the strict boundary, partial-year exclusion, and from-scratch run recomputation were exercised on the real series. Every path returns the correct result for a series with zero qualifying years; the reset and gap branches cannot be reached on this data and remain covered only by the adapter's synthetic tests. No machinery defect surfaced, with the coverage caveat stated.

---

## Evidentiary deficiencies of this exercise

1. **Reconnaissance preceded registration**, disclosed at the head. The exercise's central finding was visible in reconnaissance; registration formalised rather than predicted it.
2. **Single publisher.** The committed series and its corroborating unemployment series come from the same NY Fed product. The ordering validation is therefore not fully independent; NBER dating is the only external element.
3. **No pre-revision vintage.** Today's file restates history under current occupational-requirements and survey weights; the 2001 trough as originally published was not checked.
4. **The unsmoothed sensitivity is unperformable** from published data, leaving one Type D obligation standing open against any future F6 verdict.
