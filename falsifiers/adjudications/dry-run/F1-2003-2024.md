# F1 dry run -- 2003 expected-positive, 2024 negative control

**Nothing in this file is a finding about the present.** This is a rubric exercise under the conventions of `README.md` in this directory: the F1 adapter's committed derivation is applied to closed historical windows whose outcomes are independently known, for the single purpose of testing whether the rubric discriminates. It is not a determination, it does not appear in the annual index, and it does not bind any live verdict.

This is the first dry run executed under the prospective requirement committed in `../../PRE-REGISTRATION.md`, Amendment 2, and the first application of the paired design to a Type A condition.

---

## Design, fixed before computation

**Sequence disclosure.** The episode pair, the roles, and the expected outcomes below were declared in the authoring session before any series was fetched or any ratio computed. The three failure conditions are those of `README.md`, committed 2026-08-19, before this exercise existed. One exercise in this file was **not** pre-declared: the source-tolerance comparison against EIA was run after the main arithmetic, prompted by what the main arithmetic showed. It is labelled as such where it appears.

| Element | Committed |
|---|---|
| Falsifier | F1 -- US generation-capacity additions converge on China's; threshold 40 percent of PRC additions, three-year rolling basis |
| Expected-positive | Evaluation year **2003**, window 2001--2003: the US gas-turbine construction boom. The US out-built China in generation capacity in this window; a convergence condition that cannot register the period in which US additions *exceeded* China's is not measuring convergence |
| Negative control | Evaluation year **2024**, window 2022--2024: the largest PRC capacity expansion on record against historically ordinary US additions. Universally documented as non-convergence |
| Failure condition 1 | The rubric returns `not_triggered` on 2003 |
| Failure condition 2 | The rubric returns `triggered` on 2024 |
| Failure condition 3 | The rubric returns the same verdict on both -- the most serious, per the README |

### The known answers, independent of the rubric

The expected-positive rests on the documented US merchant gas boom: EIA's own capacity record shows US net summer capacity rising by roughly 137 GW over 2001--2003, the fastest three-year build in the US record, while every account of the PRC grid places its acceleration after the 2002 power shortages and the 2004--2006 build-out. The negative control needs no argument: PRC additions in 2022--2024 exceeded US additions by roughly an order of magnitude in every published source. Neither answer depends on Ember, on the differencing convention, or on any choice the adapter makes.

## The adverse case, written first

The README requires the adverse case to be written against the exercise's own usefulness as well as against its verdicts.

**Against the exercise's usefulness.** F1 is Type A -- pure arithmetic on a published series. A dry run of an arithmetic rubric, the objection runs, tests nothing but the analyst's ability to divide, and the episode chosen is the least demanding available: in 2001--2003 the ratio is not near 40 percent but above 200 percent, so any construction whatever -- gross or net, sums or means, any source -- returns `triggered`. A discrimination test passed with a 5x margin on one side and a 4x margin on the other establishes only that the rubric is not absurd, not that it is sharp near its threshold, and no closed historical window exists in which the ratio sits near 0.40 -- the record jumps from 0.67 (2005) to 0.27 (2006) without visiting the threshold region.

**Response, conceded in part.** The margin objection is correct and is recorded as a limitation: this exercise cannot establish threshold-region sharpness, and no historical window can. What it can test -- and did -- is the parts of the adapter that are not division: the differencing convention against a real series containing a negative-additions year, the existence of the series the adapter names, and the source-tolerance rule. The third of these **failed in both windows**, in a way not anticipated when the exercise was designed. An exercise that surfaces an unanticipated blocking mode in a rule written for auditability has done the thing dry runs exist to do, on the second falsifier examined out of two.

**Against the verdicts.** No serious case exists that 2003 should return `not_triggered` or 2024 `triggered` on the substance; the adverse case on the verdicts is the vintage objection instead. The computation uses the 2026 vintage of Ember's series to score a 2003 window. A 2003 evaluator would have used 2003-vintage national data, and the revision distance between the two is unexamined. The verdicts are robust to it -- see the cross-basis check -- but the exercise scores the rubric against today's version of history, not the version available at the time, and that is a real difference between this dry run and a live evaluation.

## Data and derivation

### Primary source, as committed

The adapter commits Ember's harmonised yearly dataset as primary for both countries. Fetched 2026-08-20 and snapshotted under the standing rule:

- [Ember yearly full release, long format](https://storage.googleapis.com/emb-prod-bkt-publicdata/public-downloads/yearly_full_release_long_format.csv) -- snapshot `sha256:259e1095ee8ffeaf0aff37ad557916ae1823a2da13312da50ba4cec6b4574c3b`, recorded in `../../../research/snapshots/INDEX.md`. The arithmetic below was computed on the byte-identical file.

Filter applied: `Category = Capacity`, `Subcategory = Fuel`, `Area` in {China, United States of America}. Coverage: 2000--2025 for both countries.

**A series-existence finding, recorded.** The adapter's series table names `cap_total_installed`. Ember publishes no total-capacity row: totals must be derived by summing the nine fuel-level rows per country-year. The sum was checked against the Clean-plus-Fossil aggregate rows and matches within rounding for all 52 country-years in scope. The derivation choice is recorded here because the adapter's open item -- "resolve and record the Ember API endpoint and the capacity field name at first fetch" -- turns out to understate the problem: there is no field to name, and first ingest must commit a summation rule instead.

## The arithmetic, under the rule as written

Committed construction, ratio of three-year sums; sensitivity construction, mean of three annual ratios.

### Expected-positive, evaluation year 2003

```
sum A_US, 2001-2003  = 46.0 + 60.9 + 55.8 = 162.7 GW
sum A_PRC, 2001-2003 = 17.2 + 17.8 + 34.2 =  69.2 GW
R_2003  = 162.7 / 69.2 = 2.35
R'_2003 = mean(46.0/17.2, 60.9/17.8, 55.8/34.2) = 2.57

2.35 >= 0.40  => triggered
```

### Negative control, evaluation year 2024

```
sum A_US, 2022-2024  = 19.9 + 29.6 + 39.3 =  88.8 GW
sum A_PRC, 2022-2024 = 175.8 + 353.4 + 412.0 = 941.2 GW
R_2024  = 88.8 / 941.2 = 0.094
R'_2024 = 0.097

0.094 < 0.40  => not_triggered
```

### The full ratio path, for context

Committed construction by evaluation year: 2003: 2.35, 2004: 1.46, 2005: 0.67, 2006: 0.27, 2007: 0.16, then between 0.04 and 0.24 in every year through 2025 (2025: 0.083). The threshold was last exceeded in 2005. The record passes through the 0.40 region exactly once, between the 2005 and 2006 evaluations, without an evaluation year landing in it -- so the case that matters most in the adapter's own test list, the construction-straddle at the threshold (adapter test 7), never arises historically and remains exercised only synthetically.

### Cross-basis robustness -- computed after the main arithmetic

Recomputing the **numerator** from EIA total net summer capacity, differenced ([EIA Monthly Energy Review, Table 7.7a](https://www.eia.gov/totalenergy/data/browser/csv.php?tbl=T07.07A) -- snapshot `sha256:d74be9ac925b89fca58c877e744eb0f9aba3ead60946504b6a24362c86b5f2b2`):

```
2003:  sum A_US = 136.7 GW  =>  R = 136.7 / 69.2  = 1.98   triggered
2024:  sum A_US =  83.0 GW  =>  R =  83.0 / 941.2 = 0.088  not_triggered
```

Both verdicts survive a change of numerator source and basis. The margin objection in the adverse case is why: nothing about either window is close.

## Three defects surfaced anyway, none affecting the verdicts

### Defect 1 -- the source-tolerance rule blocks automatic verdicts in both windows, including the live one

The adapter's tolerance rule: Ember-versus-national divergence exceeding 5 percent of the national figure for total installed capacity in any year raises `source_disagreement` and **blocks automatic verdict emission for that year pending adjudication**. The rule does not specify the basis of the national comparison figure. Computed against the most readily available EIA total series -- net summer capacity, the basis EIA itself headlines:

```
Year   EIA net summer (GW)   Ember (GW)   Divergence
2000        811.7               745.9        -8.1%
2001        848.3               791.9        -6.6%
2002        905.3               852.8        -5.8%
2003        948.4               908.5        -4.2%
2022       1161.4              1224.9        +5.5%
2023       1187.6              1254.5        +5.6%
2024       1228.8              1293.8        +5.3%
2025       1279.2              1333.2        +4.2%
```

On this reading of "the national figure", the tolerance is breached in two of the three expected-positive window years **and in all three negative-control window years**. As written, the adapter would have refused to emit an automatic verdict for the very windows this exercise scored -- including 2022 through 2024, which are live years.

The mechanism is definitional, not error: net summer capacity is a derated measure and Ember's series is installed capacity, so the two differ systematically, by an amount that drifts with the fleet's composition -- the sign of the divergence flips as solar, whose net-summer derate is largest, enters the fleet. A tolerance rule written without fixing the comparison basis is a rule about the difference between two definitions, not about data quality.

**Directionality.** This mode cannot flip a verdict. It can only convert an automatic verdict into an adjudicated one. It is a false-alarm mode, conservative in effect -- the opposite direction from F5-B's false-negative mode -- but it means F1's advertised full automation does not survive contact with its own cross-check rule.

**What may follow, under the dry-run rules.** No rule is changed here. The defect is registered against the adapter (Amendment 1 to `../../../pipeline/adapters/F1.md`, same date), and the resolution -- committing a basis-matched cross-check series before first ingest -- is registered there as a required pre-ingest decision. F1's verdict stands roughly 30 percentage points from its threshold, so the fix window is open; that is exactly why this dry run was run now.

### Defect 2 -- negative net additions occur in the record, and the semantics are unstated

US net additions are negative in 2015 on both sources (Ember -2.2 GW; EIA -4.4 GW): retirements exceeded additions. The committed ratio-of-sums absorbs a negative year inside a positive three-year sum, and did so silently here. But the adapter nowhere states what a **negative three-year numerator sum** would mean for a threshold defined as "additions reach 40 percent" -- the ratio would be negative, which satisfies no threshold, but "US additions were negative" and "US additions were 8 percent of China's" are different findings reported identically as `not_triggered`. The sensitivity construction is worse exposed: a single negative annual ratio enters the mean undamped. No live effect; recorded as an open specification question against the adapter.

### Defect 3 -- the series the adapter names does not exist as published

`cap_total_installed` must be constructed by summation; see the derivation note above. Trivial once known, wrong silently if assumed.

## Evidentiary deficiencies of this exercise

1. **Single-vintage scoring.** Both windows are scored on the 2026 vintage of one harmonised dataset. The 2003 verdict is what today's data says about 2003, not what 2003's data would have said. The cross-basis check mitigates for the US side only.
2. **The PRC series has no independent historical cross-check here.** The NEA/NBS year-end totals were compared only at end-2024, via the figure already in the adapter. A PRC-side divergence table equivalent to the EIA one has not been constructed, and Defect 1 may bind on that side too.
3. **The Ember snapshot is one release of a twice-monthly-updated file.** The stored hash makes this exercise reproducible against its exact inputs, but a re-fetch will not match the hash once Ember next updates, which is expected behaviour of the snapshot rule rather than a defect.
4. **The tolerance comparison was not pre-declared.** It was prompted by inspection of the totals. Its result is a discovered mode, not a tested hypothesis, and is labelled accordingly.
