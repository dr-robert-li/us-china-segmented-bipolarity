# Dry run -- the 2012--13 transition, adjudicated under the F5 rubric as a negative control

> **This is a rubric exercise, not a finding about the present.** It applies the F5 adapter and the adjudication framework to a closed historical episode for the sole purpose of testing whether the rubric discriminates. It carries **no** implication about the People's Republic of China in 2026, and nothing in it is evidence for or against any live falsifier. It is filed under `dry-run/` and excluded from the annual index for that reason.

```yaml
dry_run:
  dry_run_id: DRY-2012-F5
  falsifier_id: F5
  clauses_exercised: [A, B, C]
  episode: Hu Jintao to Xi Jinping leadership transition
  nominal_evaluation_year: 2013
  window: 2012-01-01 to 2013-12-31
  role: negative control
  is_determination: false
  binds_live_verdicts: false
  authored_by: dr-robert-li
  authored_at: 2026-08-19
  result_summary:
    A: not_met
    B: not_met
    C: not_met
  rubric_verdict: correct negative on all three sub-clauses
```

---

## The role of this exercise

A rubric that fires on the archetypal positive case has demonstrated sensitivity, not discrimination. Discrimination requires a case where the clause should **not** fire and does not. This episode was chosen as the negative control before either exercise was computed, on the ground that it is the transition most often described as procedurally regular, while still containing a genuine Politburo removal, a genuine security scandal, and contemporaneous coup allegations. A negative control with none of those features would be too easy.

**The pre-committed failure condition:** the rubric fails this exercise if any sub-clause returns `met`.

None did. The rubric passes here. Its failure is on the positive case, recorded in `1976.md`.

---

## The adverse case, written first

Framework Rule 2 requires the strongest case for the opposite of the determination, published regardless of where the reasoning lands. Here that is the case that 2012--13 **should** satisfy the clause, and it is not frivolous.

**On extra-constitutional transfer.** The transition was preceded by the removal of the man widely regarded as a rival for a Standing Committee seat, in circumstances involving his police chief's flight into a foreign consulate. A process whose principal competitor is eliminated by criminal proceedings before the selection occurs is not obviously described as a constitutional transfer merely because the final vote was properly convened. Procedural regularity at the moment of the vote can be the visible end of a process whose earlier stages were not regular at all, and the clause as written looks only at the moment of transfer.

**On purge.** The removal of a sitting Politburo member was, before 2025, exceptional rather than routine. A rubric that counts it as ordinary enforcement because it arrived with a published finding is accepting the removing authority's own characterisation of its own act -- which the framework's evidence standard elsewhere forbids, treating official statements as evidence of a position and never of a fact.

**On military intervention.** Troop-movement claims in Beijing in March 2012 were widespread enough that the state arrested people and closed websites over them. A clause that dismisses an allegation because the state suppressed discussion of it is reasoning from the suppression to the falsity.

**These are answered rather than absorbed, below.** The third is answered on evidence: the allegation collapses on inspection for reasons independent of the suppression. The first two are answered on the pre-registered wording of the clause, which is a weaker answer, and that is recorded.

## Sub-clause A -- transfer of the paramount position outside the constitutional procedure

### The rule as written

CPC Constitution as amended 21 October 2007, in force at the opening of the 18th Congress, **Article 22**: "The Political Bureau, the Standing Committee of the Political Bureau and the General Secretary of the Central Committee of the Party are elected by the Central Committee in plenary session. The General Secretary of the Central Committee must be a member of the Standing Committee of the Political Bureau" ([2007 CPC Constitution](http://www.china.org.cn/english/congress/229722.htm)). Identical text was carried into the version adopted 14 November 2012 ([2012 CPC Constitution](http://www.china.org.cn/china/18th_cpc_congress/2012-11/16/content_27138030_4.htm)).

PRC Constitution as amended 14 March 2004, **Article 62(4)**: the National People's Congress has power "to elect the President and the Vice-President of the People's Republic of China" ([npc.gov.cn](http://www.npc.gov.cn/zgrdw/englishnpc/Constitution/2007-11/15/content_1372965.htm)); **Article 79**: "The President and Vice-President of the People's Republic of China are elected by the National People's Congress" ([Supreme People's Court English portal, full text after the 2004 amendment](https://english.court.gov.cn/2015-07/17/c_1170165.htm)).

### What happened

Xi Jinping was elected General Secretary at the **1st Plenary Session of the 18th Central Committee on 15 November 2012**, attended by 205 Central Committee members and 171 alternates ([China Daily, 15 November 2012](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/china/2012cpc/2012-11/15/content_15931883.htm); [Xinhua](http://www.xinhuanet.com/english/2017-10/23/c_136700371.htm)). His remarks that day confirm the plenum performed the election ([USC US-China Institute transcript](https://china.usc.edu/node/21179)).

He was elected President of the People's Republic and Chairman of the state Central Military Commission at the **4th plenary meeting of the 1st session of the 12th National People's Congress on 14 March 2013**, by 2,952 votes for, 1 against, 3 abstentions ([PRC Embassy to the United States](https://us.china-embassy.gov.cn/eng/unshown/2013lianghui/201303/t20130314_5096928.htm); [china.org.cn](http://www.china.org.cn/china/NPC_CPPCC_2013/2013-03/14/content_28239654.htm)).

**In each case the organ the constitution names performed the act the constitution assigns it.** This is the precise contrast with 1976, where the Politburo did what Article 9 assigned to a plenary session, and the plenary session ratified it nine months later.

### The scholarly characterisation, and its limits

Alice Miller of the Hoover Institution: the 18th Congress "convened according to the prescribed five-year stipulation in the party constitution", which "together with the consistent convocation of Central Committee plenums and other subordinate institutions in the run-up to the congress, accords with the fastidious adherence to formal party processes characteristic of the entire post-Mao reform era"; the leadership "delivered another orderly and deliberate transfer of power at the top" ([Alice Miller, *China Leadership Monitor* 40](https://www.hoover.org/sites/default/files/research/docs/CLM40AM.pdf)).

The only irregularity Miller records is timing rather than procedure: the Congress "was late by the standard of previous congresses in the post-Mao era, which have usually met in September or October", which she reads as suggesting "that leadership transitions of this scale provoke greater political contention and so incur delay" ([same](https://www.hoover.org/sites/default/files/research/docs/CLM40AM.pdf)).

**Recorded limitation.** Miller's article carries the whole of this sub-clause's scholarly characterisation **alone**, and *China Leadership Monitor* is analytical commentary rather than peer-reviewed scholarship. No source characterising the transition as procedurally irregular was located, which may reflect scholarly consensus or may reflect the limits of the search. **"No dissent found" is not "no dissent exists"**, and the negative control's cleanliness is to that extent less established than it appears.

A caution about the weakest tier applies here too and is worth recording, because it is a live instance of the framework's source-hierarchy doing work: Britannica states that "the party congress elected Xi Jinping" to the general secretaryship ([Britannica, Hu Jintao](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Hu-Jintao)). That is inaccurate on the procedural point this sub-clause turns on -- the Central Committee in plenary session elected him, not the Congress. The encyclopaedia is relied on here only for the Central Military Commission dates, where Miller independently corroborates the substance.

### Determination

**`not_met`**, on affirmative evidence rather than on absence of evidence: the constitutional text, the identity of the acting organ, the date, the attendance, and the recorded vote are all in evidence.

## Sub-clause B -- purge exceeding 15 percent of the Politburo

### The denominator

The 17th Central Committee's Politburo comprised **25 full members**, elected 22 October 2007 at its 1st Plenary Session ([enumerated official name list, china.org.cn](http://www.china.org.cn/english/congress/229262.htm)). The Congressional Research Service records "a 25-member Politburo" and the Congress dates of 15--21 October 2007 ([CRS Report RS22767, 5 December 2007](https://sgp.fas.org/crs/row/RS22767.pdf)). Miller independently gives 25, and refers to "the 24 members of the outgoing 17th Central Committee Politburo who remained after the removal of Bo Xilai earlier in 2012" ([*China Leadership Monitor* 40](https://www.hoover.org/sites/default/files/research/docs/CLM40AM.pdf)).

Whether the 17th Politburo had zero alternates is **not established**: the official list omits alternates and CRS records that the 16th had one, but absence of mention is not a positive statement ([china.org.cn](http://www.china.org.cn/english/congress/229262.htm); [CRS RS22767](https://sgp.fas.org/crs/row/RS22767.pdf)). Alternates do not enter the denominator under the adapter, so this does not affect the arithmetic.

```
denominator       = 25
threshold_members = ceil(0.15 * 25) = ceil(3.75) = 4
```

### The window and the event

Window for nominal evaluation year 2013, anchored backward two years: **1 January 2012 to 31 December 2013.**

| Step | Date | Action | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Suspension | 10 April 2012 | Central Committee suspended Bo's Politburo and Central Committee membership; the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection filed the case | [Xinhua via China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/china/2012-09/28/content_15791527.htm) |
| Politburo decision to expel | 28 September 2012 | Expelled from the Party and removed from public office, on a Discipline Inspection report | [Xinhua via China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/china/2012-09/28/content_15791527.htm) |
| Central Committee endorsement | 4 November 2012, at the 7th plenum of the 17th Central Committee | The plenum "endorsed an earlier decision by the Politburo to expel Bo" | [South China Morning Post, 5 November 2012](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/article/1075158/party-plenum-promotes-two-generals-and-confirms-bo-xilai-expulsion) |

The 4 November date is confirmed **only by inference** from the report's statement that the plenum "wrapped up a four-day closed-door conclave yesterday" in a piece dated 5 November; no official communiqué stating the plenum's dates was obtained. Bo's removal as a National People's Congress deputy, reported elsewhere as 26 October 2012, is **not established** from any fetched source and is not relied on.

### Was there a published disciplinary finding?

**Yes, and it was specific.** The Xinhua report of the 28 September Politburo decision published findings of abuse of power, "major responsibility in the Wang Lijun incident and the Bogu Kailai intentional homicide case", taking "huge bribes", "improper sexual relationships with a number of women", and violations of organizational and personnel discipline ([Xinhua via China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/china/2012-09/28/content_15791527.htm)).

### Did the mechanism match the constitution then in force?

**Yes, on its face.** CPC Constitution, **Article 40**: "Any decision to remove a member or alternate member of the Central Committee ... from his or her posts within the Party, to place such a person on probation within the Party or to expel such a person from the Party must be approved by a two thirds majority vote at a plenary meeting of the Party committee to which he or she belongs. In special circumstances, the decision may be taken first by the Standing Committee of the Political Bureau of the Central Committee ... pending confirmation at the plenary meeting of the Party committee." It adds: "A member or alternate member of the Central Committee who has seriously violated the criminal law shall be expelled from the Party on decision by the Political Bureau of the Central Committee." ([2007 CPC Constitution](http://www.china.org.cn/english/congress/229722.htm); identical text in the [2012 version](http://www.china.org.cn/china/18th_cpc_congress/2012-11/16/content_27138030_8.htm))

The observed sequence -- Politburo decision on 28 September, plenum endorsement on 4 November -- maps onto both the final sentence and the "pending confirmation" route.

**The contrast with 1976 is structural rather than a matter of degree.** The 1973 Constitution contained **no** provision governing the removal of a Politburo member: no quorum, no majority requirement, no ratification route ([1973 CCP Constitution](https://www.marxists.org/subject/china/documents/cpc/CONSTITUTION_CPC.htm)). In 2012 there was a rule and the sequence matched it. In 1976 there was no rule for the sequence to match or depart from. A clause about procedural irregularity behaves differently against a constitution that is silent than against one that speaks, and the rubric does not currently distinguish those two situations. That is registered as an open specification question rather than resolved here.

### The arithmetic, on two readings

```
denominator          = 25
threshold_members    = 4

Reading 1, counting the removal:
qualifying_removals  = 1    (Bo Xilai)
1 < 4                => not_met

Reading 2, applying the adapter's exclusion rule:
  published discipline finding: yes
  ordinary investigative sequence: yes
  clustered: no -- sole removal in a two-year window
  => counts toward nothing
qualifying_removals  = 0
0 < 4                => not_met
```

### Determination

**`not_met`, at a distance of three members on the more adverse reading.** The verdict is stable across both readings and across the alternative anchorings computed in `1976.md`: effective-loss-of-office anchoring gives 1 rather than 0 and changes nothing, and seats-vacated anchoring gives 1, since no member of the 17th Politburo died in office inside the window.

**That stability is the substantive result.** The negative control does not merely fail to trigger; it fails to trigger under every counting rule considered, which means the rubric's sensitivity to its own anchoring choice -- the defect that produces the 1976 failure -- does not manifest here. The rubric is fragile in one direction only. It can be made to miss a purge by choice of anchoring; it cannot be made to invent one.

## Sub-clause C -- military intervention in a succession

### The Wang Lijun incident is well sourced and is not military

The official account, from the Chengdu Intermediate People's Court verdict as reported by Xinhua: "Feeling that he was in danger, Wang entered the U.S. Consulate General in Chengdu without permission at 2:31 p.m. on February 6 and asked the U.S. side for political asylum"; he "left the U.S. diplomatic mission of his own volition at 11:35 p.m. on February 7 after being persuaded by the Chinese side"; his conduct "constituted the crime of defection and the circumstances were serious". He received a combined sentence of 15 years ([Xinhua via China Daily, 24 September 2012](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/china/2012-09/24/content_15777110.htm)).

This is the best-sourced allegation in the episode -- an official judicial finding corroborated by wire reporting -- and it is **out of scope**. Wang Lijun was Chongqing's vice-mayor and former public security chief. Nothing in the official record alleges involvement by the People's Liberation Army. The clause is about military intervention, and a police defection is not that.

**This exclusion is recorded because it cuts against the clause.** The strongest evidence available about irregularity in this episode is evidence the clause cannot use, which is a fact about the clause's scope rather than about the episode.

### The March 2012 troop-movement allegations are refuted

The allegation: "rumours had been spread that military vehicles were on the streets of Beijing" ([BBC News, 31 March 2012](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-china-17570005)); on Weibo, "chatter ... that tanks were rolling through Beijing", with claims of gunfire ([*The Diplomat*, "Coup Rumors in China", March 2012](https://thediplomat.com/2012/03/coup-rumors-in-china/)).

The adverse findings:

1. "while there is no evidence to substantiate the rumours of a coup, the party is clearly upset" ([BBC News](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-china-17570005)).
2. The photographic evidence was recycled: "images of the military that appeared in the *Epoch Times* that were purportedly taken this week were later found to have been taken from a military website, and dated back to 2010, when parade preparations were taking place" ([*The Diplomat*](https://thediplomat.com/2012/03/coup-rumors-in-china/)).
3. "the chatter on the Weibo microblog that tanks were rolling through Beijing was almost certainly nonsense" ([*The Diplomat*](https://thediplomat.com/2012/03/coup-rumors-in-china/)).
4. Minxin Pei, named: "It's very hard to stage coups in China without the direct involvement of the highest-ranking generals, who today are not closely tied with particular factions... The unit guarding the central leadership isn't controlled by the state security apparatus. It answers only to the person most trusted by the top leadership... So I wouldn't take such rumors very seriously." ([*The Diplomat*](https://thediplomat.com/2012/03/coup-rumors-in-china/))

The state response -- six arrests, sixteen websites closed, commenting disabled on Sina Weibo and Tencent Weibo from 31 March to 3 April 2012 ([BBC News](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-china-17570005)) -- is **not** treated as evidence either way, which is the answer to the adverse case's third point. The allegation fails on the misattributed photographs and the absence of any named or documentary corroboration, and it would fail on those grounds whether or not the state had suppressed discussion of it.

### The asymmetry with 1976, stated plainly

In 1976 the equivalent claim is affirmatively established: armed units under the Beijing Garrison Command were tasked to secure the Xinhua News Agency, the People's Radio Station, the *People's Daily* and the airport as part of the operation, on the record of a specialist scholarly account ([Teiwes and Sun, p. 359](https://www.bannedthought.net/China/MaoEra/GPCR/BourgeoisCommentary/China'sOctoberCoupThatEndedTheMaoistEra-Sun-Teiwes.pdf)). In 2012 the equivalent claim rests on anonymous microblog posts and a misattributed photo set.

The framework's evidence standard -- anonymous or single-outlet reporting is insufficient alone for `met` -- does the work here without any judgement about the episode's politics. **The standard was written before either exercise and applies identically to both**, which is the only thing that makes the asymmetry in outcomes reportable rather than suspect.

### Determination

**`not_met`.** The one well-sourced irregularity is out of scope; the in-scope allegation is refuted at the weakest tier of sourcing.

## Result

| Sub-clause | Verdict | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| A | `not_met` | Named organ performed the named act, twice, with dates, attendance and a recorded vote |
| B | `not_met` at three members | 1 removal on the counting reading, 0 on the exclusion reading, against a threshold of 4 |
| C | `not_met` | In-scope allegation refuted; best-sourced irregularity out of scope |

**The negative control behaves correctly.** No sub-clause fires, and Sub-clause B's `not_met` is stable across every anchoring rule considered.

Read together with `1976.md`: Sub-clauses A and C separate the two episodes. **Sub-clause B returns `not_met` on both**, which is the failure condition named in advance as the most serious. The rubric's discrimination problem is confined to one limb, and it is the limb the 2026 record identifies as nearest its threshold.

### Recorded evidentiary deficiencies

1. **Miller carries Sub-clause A's scholarly characterisation alone**, and *China Leadership Monitor* is analytical commentary rather than peer-reviewed scholarship. No contrary characterisation was located, and that absence is reported as an absence rather than as a consensus.
2. **The 4 November 2012 plenum date rests on inference** from a report's use of "yesterday". No official communiqué stating the plenum's dates was obtained. The date is not load-bearing: the Politburo decision of 28 September is inside the window on any reading.
3. **Whether the 17th Politburo had zero alternates is not established.** Alternates do not enter the denominator, so the arithmetic is unaffected.
4. **Bo Xilai's removal as a National People's Congress deputy is not established** from any fetched source and is excluded from the findings.
5. **Sub-clause C's refutation rests on journalism**, not scholarship. That is appropriate for adjudicating a contemporaneous rumour's evidentiary standing, and it is still journalism.
