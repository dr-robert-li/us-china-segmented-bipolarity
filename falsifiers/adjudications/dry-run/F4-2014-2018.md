# F4 dry run -- the baseline study as sole constructible episode

**Nothing in this file is a finding about the present.** Rubric exercise under `README.md` conventions. Not a determination; binds no live verdict.

**Outcome, stated at the head: `discrimination_untestable`, and a sharper defect beneath it.** F4's committed verdict machinery consumes a data shape -- per-country changes in the p90/p10 ratio within high-AI-exposure occupations -- that **no published source provides, including the baseline study the condition is defined against.** The exercise therefore could not construct either half of the paired design at the verdict level, and what it surfaced instead is that the adapter's six-of-ten majority rule is unevaluable as written even if a successor study appears in the baseline's own format. Score across the set: one failed (F5-B), one passed (F1), two untestable (F6, F4), four untested.

---

## Design and registration, with the sequence disclosed

**Sequence disclosure.** As with F6, series reconnaissance -- here, obtaining and reading the baseline study -- preceded registration and revealed the central finding (the absence of per-country tables) before this head was written. This is a characterisation exercise, not a blind test. All arithmetic below was computed after registration.

| Element | Committed |
|---|---|
| Falsifier | F4 -- AI raises rather than lowers within-occupation wage dispersion; p90/p10 rises in high-exposure occupations across 10 countries; deadline 2032 |
| Baseline study | Georgieff (2024), *Artificial intelligence and wage inequality*, OECD Artificial Intelligence Papers No. 13: 19 OECD countries, 36 occupations, 2014--2018, Felten-Raj-Seamans exposure as extended by Georgieff and Hyee (2021) |
| Sole constructible episode | The baseline's own window, 2014--2018, as an expected-negative: the study's known answer is **compression** (within-occupation p90/p10 fell 2.3 percent on average across occupation-country cells; the decline was larger in more-exposed occupations and statistically significant in multivariate regressions) |
| Expected-positive | **None exists.** The condition F4 tests -- the compression pattern reversing -- has not been documented on the committed measure anywhere |
| Failure conditions | The standard three, evaluable only where a verdict is constructible; where none is, the reason is recorded as the finding |

## The adverse case, written first

**Against the exercise's usefulness.** This is the weakest exercise of the four run so far: no verdict can be computed, so the "dry run" reduces to a literature check plus a population table, and its conclusion -- F4 cannot currently resolve -- was already printed in the adapter under "structural defect". **Response, conceding the overlap and stating the residue:** the adapter recorded that the *source* does not exist. The exercise establishes something the adapter did not know: that the *verdict machinery would fail even given the source*, because the committed counting rule presupposes per-country cells that the baseline itself never published, and that the committed sample-selection rule is decision-fragile at exactly the rank that matters. Both are specification defects that only surfaced by tracing the rule's data requirements against the actual publication, which is what a dry run is for.

**Against the verdicts.** No verdict is issued, so the objection attaches to the expected-negative characterisation: the baseline's compression finding is taken from the study itself rather than independently recomputed, and this project has no access to the underlying SES/CPS microdata to check it. Accepted; the study's own numbers are the only available answer key, and they are used only as a sanity check on the direction machinery, not as a verdict.

### What the baseline publishes, against what the rule consumes

The committed majority rule: `met` if p90/p10 **rises** in high-exposure occupations in **at least six of ten** countries. Its input is a per-country, exposure-conditional change in dispersion. The baseline publishes:

- occupation-level p90/p10 changes as **simple averages across countries** (Figure 2.5);
- country-level changes in **overall** inequality, Theil index, not exposure-conditional (Figure 2.3: Czechia -17%, Poland -17%, Norway +66%, Greece +34%);
- **pooled** occupation-by-country regressions of dispersion growth on exposure.

**No table or figure in the publication crosses country with exposure quartile.** The cell the majority rule counts does not exist in print. Constructing it requires the underlying microdata (EU Structure of Earnings Survey; CPS for the US), which is exactly the uncommitted fallback whose costs the adapter records.

## The arithmetic that is possible

### Direction and floor machinery, single-point sanity check

On the baseline's own pooled numbers: the average within-occupation p90/p10 change is **-2.3 percent**, the exposure gradient is negative and significant. The direction rule requires a rise; a fall returns no-rise. The 0.5 percent magnitude floor is not reached in the qualifying direction. The machinery returns the correct answer on the one data point available. This is sensitivity of the weakest kind, not discrimination, and is recorded as such.

### The committed sample rule, exercised against the baseline sample

Rule: the ten most-populous countries present in both samples, ranked by working-age population at the successor's reference date. Ranked on World Bank 15--64 population, 2018, for the baseline's 19:

```
 1 United States 216.6M    6 Poland       25.7M    11 Hungary    6.44M
 2 Germany        53.9M    7 Netherlands  11.2M    12 Sweden     6.35M
 3 France         41.8M    8 Belgium      7.34M    ...
 4 Italy          38.3M    9 Czechia      6.88M
 5 Spain          30.9M   10 Greece       6.84M   <- cutoff
```

**The rule is decision-fragile at the rank that decides membership.** Ranks 9 and 10 are separated by 0.6 percent (Czechia 6.88M, Greece 6.84M) and rank 10 from rank 11 by 6 percent (Greece 6.84M, Hungary 6.44M) -- margins inside the variation produced by defensible alternative choices the rule does not fix: the age band (15--64 versus 20--64), the population source (World Bank, UN WPP, Eurostat, national statistics), and the vintage. The committed rule names none of these. A rule committed to prevent discretionary sample choice has left three degrees of freedom that can flip the tenth seat.

## Monitoring check 1 -- recorded per the adapter's open item

The adapter requires each successor-study check to be recorded, including negative ones. First check, 2026-08-20:

**No qualifying successor exists.** Two documents evaluated:

1. **The November 2024 OECD brief** is the same study repackaged; not a successor.
2. **Jaccoud (2025)** is a genuine new study of within-occupation wage dispersion and AI exposure -- and its headline runs **toward** F4's trigger direction: "occupations more exposed to AI exhibit greater wage dispersion, especially at the top." It does not qualify as the committed successor, on three grounds each sufficient alone: its exposure measure is the Webb (2020) family, not Felten-derived, which is a methodology break under the adapter's own rule; its EU-SES sample contains no United States, and the ten-country intersection rule requires presence in both samples; and it is a pooled fixed-effects design with no per-country cells (the same shape defect as the baseline). **It is recorded and not substituted.** Substituting a differently-constructed study whose result points toward the thesis-relevant trigger is precisely the favourable-source substitution the prohibitions exist to prevent -- and the direction of its finding makes declining the substitution the binding test of that rule, recorded here as its first live application to F4.

## Findings

### Finding 1 -- `discrimination_untestable`, second instance

No expected-positive episode exists on the committed measure, and unlike F6 there is not even a continuously published series to characterise: the measurand exists only inside one study's 2014--2018 window. The paired design cannot be constructed at the verdict level in either direction.

### Finding 2 -- `verdict_input_shape_unpublished`: the majority rule cannot be evaluated from any existing publication

The six-of-ten rule counts per-country, exposure-conditional dispersion changes. The baseline study does not publish that cell, and a successor reproducing the baseline's reporting format would not either. As written, F4 resolves only if a successor publishes country-level exposure-conditional results -- a reporting choice the adapter cannot influence -- or if the uncommitted microdata fallback is adopted by prior diff. One-directional: the defect blocks resolution (permanent indeterminacy); it cannot manufacture a trigger.

### Finding 3 -- the sample rule is underspecified at the deciding rank

Population source, age band, and vintage are uncommitted, and the ninth-to-eleventh ranked candidates sit within definitional variation of each other. The fix -- committing all three before any successor evaluation exists -- is statable entirely without reference to any outcome, because no evaluation is live or in prospect. Registered against the adapter for commitment now, while provably inert.

---

## Evidentiary deficiencies of this exercise

1. Reconnaissance preceded registration, disclosed at the head.
2. The baseline's compression finding is taken on the study's own authority; no microdata recomputation.
3. Jaccoud (2025) was assessed from its published text only; its annex tables were not exhaustively checked for per-country cells before the no-per-country-cells characterisation was recorded. The three-ground non-qualification stands on the exposure-measure and sample grounds regardless.
4. The population ranking was computed on one source and one definition -- which is the point of Finding 3, and also its limit: the fragility is demonstrated on one axis of the variation, not all three.
