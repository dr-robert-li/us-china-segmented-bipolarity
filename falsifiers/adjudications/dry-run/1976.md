# Dry run -- the 1976 succession, adjudicated under the F5 rubric

> **This is a rubric exercise, not a finding about the present.** It applies the F5 adapter and the adjudication framework to a closed historical episode for the sole purpose of testing whether the rubric discriminates. It carries **no** implication about the People's Republic of China in 2026, and nothing in it is evidence for or against any live falsifier. It is filed under `dry-run/` and excluded from the annual index for that reason.

```yaml
dry_run:
  dry_run_id: DRY-1976-F5
  falsifier_id: F5
  clauses_exercised: [A, B, C]
  episode: succession following the death of Mao Zedong
  nominal_evaluation_year: 1976
  window: 1975-01-01 to 1976-12-31
  is_determination: false
  binds_live_verdicts: false
  authored_by: dr-robert-li
  authored_at: 2026-08-19
  result_summary:
    A: met
    B: not_met -- recorded as a rubric failure
    C: met
  rubric_verdict: partial -- two of three sub-clauses discriminate, one does not
```

---

## Why this exercise exists, and what would count as failing it

`falsifiers/log/2026/F5.md` records Deficiency 3: the Sub-clause B rubric had never been run against a case where the answer is independently known. Exercising a rubric only on the live case it was written for is not a test of the rubric. It is a test of nothing.

The design is a two-episode exercise with a stated expectation **written before the arithmetic was done**:

- **1976 is the expected-positive.** If any episode in the history of the People's Republic satisfies a clause about extra-constitutional succession, purge, and military involvement, this is that episode. A rubric that returns `not_met` here has a false-negative problem.
- **2012--13 is the negative control**, adjudicated separately in `2012.md`. A rubric that returns `met` there has a false-positive problem.

**The pre-committed failure conditions:** the rubric fails if it returns `not_met` on 1976, or `met` on 2012, or the same verdict on both. The third is the worst of the three, because a rubric that cannot separate the two episodes is not measuring anything.

One of those conditions has been met. It is recorded below rather than engineered away.

---

## The adverse case, written first

Framework Rule 2 requires the strongest case for the opposite of the determination, written before the reasoning and published regardless of where the reasoning lands. Because this exercise reaches three verdicts, the adverse case is written against the exercise's own usefulness.

**The strongest case that this dry run demonstrates nothing.**

Selecting 1976 as the test case is selecting the episode most likely to satisfy the clause. A rubric tuned until it fires on the single most extreme case in a seventy-year record has been tuned to one observation. If the exercise had returned `met` on all three sub-clauses, the correct conclusion would have been that the rubric passes a test it could hardly have failed, and the appearance of validation would have been worth less than the appearance suggests.

**The strongest case that 1976 should not satisfy the clause at all.** Every removal in the window was ratified by a Central Committee plenary session within nine months, the surviving leadership accepted the outcome, and the Party's institutional continuity was not broken -- the same body that had elected the Politburo in 1973 confirmed the removals in 1977 and continued to the 11th Congress. A sufficiently formalist reading holds that delayed ratification is ratification. On that reading 1976 is a severe intra-elite conflict resolved through Party organs, and a clause that fires on it will fire on any leadership conflict at all.

**The strongest case that the episode is not knowable to the standard the framework requires.** The two best available scholarly accounts conflict on the date of the arrests, on whether there was violence, and on who held which command. Four of five death dates in the window rest on a single encyclopaedia entry. The unit that carried out the arrests is not established by any fetched source. A framework whose evidence standard requires three independent sources for anything but `indeterminate` should arguably return `indeterminate` on much of this, fifty years after the fact and with archives closed.

**This last case is partly conceded below.** It is the reason the Sub-clause C determination is recorded as resting on one specialist source.

## Sub-clause A -- transfer of the paramount position outside the constitutional procedure

### The rule as written

The 1973 CCP Constitution, Article 9: "The plenary session of the Central Committee of the Party elects the Political Bureau of the Central Committee, the Standing Committee of the Political Bureau of the Central Committee and the Chairman and Vice-Chairmen of the Central Committee" ([1973 CCP Constitution](https://www.marxists.org/subject/china/documents/cpc/CONSTITUTION_CPC.htm)).

The document runs to twelve articles. It contains **no** vacancy provision, **no** acting-Chairman provision, and **no** automatic-succession provision ([1973 CCP Constitution](https://www.marxists.org/subject/china/documents/cpc/CONSTITUTION_CPC.htm)). Election by a plenary session is the only route to the Chairmanship the text describes.

### What happened

Hua Guofeng was named Chairman of the Central Committee and concurrently Chairman of the Military Commission by the **Politburo**, on 7 October 1976, the day after the arrests: "On October 7 Hua Guofeng and Wang Dongxing each delivered two reports and Ye Jianying, one, to the Politburo... The grateful Politburo named Hua chairman of the party Central Committee and concurrently chairman of the Military Commission" ([Immanuel C. Y. Hsü, *China Without Mao*, Oxford University Press](https://academic.oup.com/book/25557/chapter/192860516?searchresult=1)).

The Central Committee did not meet until the **3rd Plenary Session of the 10th Central Committee, 16--21 July 1977**, which "Adopted the Resolution to Confirm Comrade Hua Guofeng's Positions as Chairman of the Central Committee of the Communist Party of China and Chairman of the Central Military Commission" ([State Council record of the 10th Central Committee's plenary sessions](https://www.gov.cn/test/2007-08/28/content_729620.htm)).

**Interval between assumption and constitutionally specified election: approximately nine months.**

### The countervailing textual argument, and why it does not carry

Article 9 also provides that "When the Central Committee is not in plenary session, the Political Bureau of the Central Committee and its Standing Committee exercise the functions and powers of the Central Committee" ([1973 CCP Constitution](https://www.marxists.org/subject/china/documents/cpc/CONSTITUTION_CPC.htm)). On its face this authorises the Politburo to do what it did.

The argument does not carry, for a reason internal to the same article: the sentence conferring the power to elect the Chairman names the plenary session specifically, in a document that elsewhere assigns powers to the Politburo generically. Reading the general delegation to absorb the specific grant would make the specific grant redundant. This is the reading Hsü takes, and he is a source with no interest in the outcome of a 2026 measurement exercise: "Strictly speaking, the process of the selection of Hua as Mao's successor was of dubious legality. Article 9 of the party constitution stipulated that the party chairman must be elected in a plenary session of the Central Committee, and the Third Plenary Session of the Tenth Central Committee had not yet met. But the party and the country were willing to overlook the legal formality and make Hua the new leader at once" ([Hsü, OUP](https://academic.oup.com/book/25557/chapter/192860516?searchresult=1)).

### Determination

**`met`.** The paramount position transferred by an organ other than the one the constitution names, and remained so held for nine months. Recorded distance: not applicable -- Sub-clause A is qualitative.

### What this corrects in the Phase 5 record

`falsifiers/adjudications/2026/F5-A.md` records, and `falsifiers/log/2026/F5.md` repeats, a limitation: "Sub-clause A may be close to unfalsifiable", on the reasoning that the paramount position's tenure is governed by a norm rather than a procedure, so there may be no procedure for a transfer to depart from.

**That limitation is now shown to be overstated.** A historical case exists in which the clause is satisfied on the face of the constitutional text then in force, adjudicated by a source contemporary to neither this project nor its thesis. Sub-clause A is falsifiable in principle and there is at least one observation in its positive class.

The limitation is not withdrawn, because the narrower version of it survives: what was falsifiable in 1976 was a **written procedure** for the Chairmanship. The post-1982 constitutions abolished the Chairmanship, and the paramount position's tenure since has rested on norms rather than on a procedural text. The clause remains falsifiable in principle and hard to falsify under the current constitutional arrangement. That is a weaker claim than the one recorded in Phase 5, and the Phase 5 records are amended to it rather than left standing.

## Sub-clause B -- purge exceeding 15 percent of the Politburo

### The denominator

The 10th Central Committee's Politburo, elected at its 1st Plenary Session on 30 August 1973, comprised **21 full members**, 4 alternates, and a 9-member Standing Committee ([State Council record](https://www.gov.cn/test/2007-08/28/content_729620.htm)).

**The Standing Committee sits inside the 21, not additionally.** The State Council phrasing -- "9 Politburo Standing Committee members, 21 Politburo members, and 4 alternate members" -- admits an additive reading giving 34. The contemporaneous press communiqué resolves it: all nine Standing Committee names appear within the enumerated 21-name full-member list ([*China Reconstructs* supplement, November 1973](https://www.marxists.org/subject/china/china-reconstructs/1973/CR1973-11-Sup.pdf)).

This is worth recording for a reason beyond 1976. **The identical ambiguity was found in the 2026 record**, where the primary Party source states a Politburo count of 24 alongside a separate list of 7 Standing Committee members without saying whether the 7 are inside the 24. In both cases only an enumerated name list settles it. A rubric that reads an aggregate figure off an official page and computes a threshold from it can be wrong by a factor that changes the threshold, in either direction, in either year. See Amendment 1 to `../2026/F5-B.md`.

English Wikipedia describes a "25-member Politburo" for the 10th Central Committee, contradicting both the State Council record and the 1973 communiqué. The official and primary sources are preferred; the conflict is recorded rather than suppressed.

```
denominator       = 21
threshold_members = ceil(0.15 * 21) = ceil(3.15) = 4
```

### The window

Under the anchoring rule appended to `pipeline/adapters/F5.md` on 19 August 2026, the counting window runs backward from the end of the nominal evaluation year across two years. For 1976: **1 January 1975 to 31 December 1976.**

This boundary does real work and was not chosen to produce a result. Four removals commonly associated with the aftermath -- Chen Xilian, Ji Dengkui, Wu De and Wang Dongxing -- were approved at the 5th Plenary Session of the 11th Central Committee, **23--29 February 1980** ([*Beijing Review*, communiqué of the 5th Plenary Session](https://www.bjreview.com/90th/2011-04/26/content_357494_5.htm)). Chen Yonggui's relief from the Vice-Premiership is recorded in a document dated 18 August 1980 ([Deng Xiaoping, "On the Reform of the System of Party and State Leadership"](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/deng-xiaoping/1980/220.htm)). All fall outside the window and count toward nothing.

### The events, and how the anchoring rule treats them

| Member | Event | Date | In window | Counted under the anchoring rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deng Xiaoping | Dismissed from all posts inside and outside the Party, by Politburo decision, published | 7--10 April 1976 | Yes | **Yes** |
| Wang Hongwen | Arrested | 6 October 1976 | Yes | **No** |
| Zhang Chunqiao | Arrested | 6 October 1976 | Yes | **No** |
| Jiang Qing | Arrested | 6 October 1976 | Yes | **No** |

| Li Desheng | Relieved of Central Committee Vice-Chairmanship and Standing Committee membership; retained ordinary Politburo membership | 2nd Plenum, 8--10 January 1975 | Yes | **No** -- not a removal from the Politburo |
| Dong Biwu | Death in office | 2 April 1975 | Yes | **No** -- death is not a purge |

Deng's dismissal is sourced to the contemporaneous Party press: "The Political Bureau of the Central Committee of the Communist Party of China ... has unanimously agreed to dismiss Teng Hsiao-ping from all posts both inside and outside the Party" ([*Peking Review* 1976 No. 16](https://www.marxists.org/subject/china/peking-review/1976/PR1976-16a.htm); companion resolution in [*Peking Review* 1976 No. 15](https://www.marxists.org/subject/china/peking-review/1976/PR1976-15a.htm)). He was restored at the same 3rd Plenum that expelled the Gang of Four, 16--21 July 1977 ([State Council record](https://www.gov.cn/test/2007-08/28/content_729620.htm)).

The arrests are sourced to the leading specialist account: the three men were lured to a decoy Politburo Standing Committee meeting announced as a proofreading session for Volume 5 of Mao's *Selected Works*, "the arrest was organized by Wang Dongxing, and the charges against them read by Hua"; Jiang Qing and Mao Yuanxin were "arrested separately in their residences"; "The decisive action had taken no more than 35 minutes" ([Frederick C. Teiwes and Warren Sun, "China's October Coup That Ended the Maoist Era", p. 360](https://www.bannedthought.net/China/MaoEra/GPCR/BourgeoisCommentary/China'sOctoberCoupThatEndedTheMaoistEra-Sun-Teiwes.pdf)).

### The arithmetic, under the rule as written

```
denominator          = 21
threshold_members    = 4
qualifying_removals  = 1    (Deng Xiaoping)
"exceeding 15%"      => >= 4 members required
1 < 4                => not_met
```

### Determination, and the failure it records

**`not_met`, at a distance of three members.**

**This is a rubric failure and it is recorded as one.** The rubric returns `not_met` on the episode in which four members of a twenty-one-member Politburo, including the leader's widow, were seized in a single coordinated night operation lasting thirty-five minutes, and a fifth had been publicly dismissed from all posts six months earlier. The pre-committed failure condition for this exercise -- `not_met` on 1976 -- has been met.

**The mechanism of the failure is identifiable, and it is the anchoring rule.** The rule counts removals from the date of the **first official public announcement of expulsion**. In 1976 that announcement came at the 3rd Plenum in July 1977, nine months and one calendar year after the operation, and therefore outside the window that contains the operation. The rubric does not fail because the events were too small or too ambiguous. It fails because the events were resolved through an official channel more slowly than the window permits, and the anchoring rule looks only at that channel.

**The corollary is sharper than the failure.** The slower and less procedurally regular the removal, the later the official announcement, and so the more likely the rubric misses it. The anchoring rule is **anti-correlated with the thing Sub-clause B is trying to measure**. An orderly disciplinary expulsion is announced promptly and counts. An extra-constitutional seizure is announced late, or never, and does not.

### Two alternative anchorings, computed and reported

| Anchoring | Counted removals in window | Threshold | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| First official announcement of expulsion (**the rule as written**) | 1 | 4 | `not_met` |
| Effective loss of office, i.e. arrest or published dismissal | 5 | 4 | `met` |
| Seats vacated by any cause, including death | 10 | 4 | `met` |

The second row is the reading under which the rubric discriminates correctly. The third row is a different failure: **five of twenty-one seats fell vacant by death inside this window**, which is 24 percent of the body on mortality alone. A rubric counting vacated seats rather than removals would return `met` on 1976 for reasons having nothing to do with a purge, and would return false positives on any period of high elite mortality. That row is reported to establish that the exclusion of deaths is load-bearing rather than decorative.

### Why the rule is not being changed

The second anchoring is better. It is not being adopted, and the reason has to be stated precisely because it will read as stubbornness.

Adopting effective-loss-of-office anchoring would change the **live 2026 determination in the direction of the thesis**. Zhang Youxia is under investigation with no expulsion notice in the retrievable record; reporting places his detention in January 2026. Under expulsion-anchoring he counts zero. Under effective-loss anchoring he plausibly counts one, which takes the 2026 numerator from two to three against a threshold of four -- and any further such case takes it to the threshold.

The pre-registration prohibits moving a threshold as it is approached. It prohibits with equal force redefining a counting rule, after seeing that the rule under-detects, in a way that moves a live clause **toward** triggering. The asymmetry is the point: a project whose rules loosen whenever a dry run embarrasses them has no rules. That the change would be substantively defensible is exactly what makes declining it necessary, because every change made in the thesis's favour will be substantively defensible to the person making it.

**What is done instead.** The defect is recorded against Sub-clause B in the falsifier log and in `pipeline/adapters/F5.md` as a **known false-negative mode with one demonstrated instance out of one historical test**. Any future `not_met` on Sub-clause B must be read against a rubric that is known to have missed 1976. A candidate replacement anchoring is registered for the **2027 evaluation year and later**, to take effect prospectively, so that it cannot be applied to a window whose outcome is already visible. That is the only way to adopt the better rule without the adoption itself being contaminated.

## Sub-clause C -- military intervention in a succession

### The affirmative evidence

Armed units under the Beijing Garrison Command were tasked, as part of the operation, with securing the capital's media and transport nodes: "Hua told Wu the Beijing Garrison would have to secure the Xinhua News Agency, the People's Radio Station, the People's Daily, the airport and other important units, and that he (Wu) would take charge of the Garrison Command, an order effected by Chen Xilian using his authority in the MAC" ([Teiwes and Sun, p. 359](https://www.bannedthought.net/China/MaoEra/GPCR/BourgeoisCommentary/China'sOctoberCoupThatEndedTheMaoistEra-Sun-Teiwes.pdf)). Garrison Commander Wu Zhong was assessed as reliable by those planning the operation ([same, p. 359](https://www.bannedthought.net/China/MaoEra/GPCR/BourgeoisCommentary/China'sOctoberCoupThatEndedTheMaoistEra-Sun-Teiwes.pdf)).

The command constraint that made this necessary is recorded in the same source: "any unit larger than a company (*lian*) could not be moved without the permission of the MAC -- and Chen Xilian ... had authority over that body" ([same, p. 356](https://www.bannedthought.net/China/MaoEra/GPCR/BourgeoisCommentary/China'sOctoberCoupThatEndedTheMaoistEra-Sun-Teiwes.pdf)).

A separate concern about armour is recorded as having substance: "The case of the tank units near Beijing has more substance"; Hua was told on 3 or 4 October that Ye Jianying "was worried about a tank division at Changping in the suburbs of Beijing" ([same, p. 358](https://www.bannedthought.net/China/MaoEra/GPCR/BourgeoisCommentary/China'sOctoberCoupThatEndedTheMaoistEra-Sun-Teiwes.pdf)).

Hsü characterises the military's role as "key ... in the 'palace revolution'" ([Hsü, OUP](https://academic.oup.com/book/25557/chapter/192860516?searchresult=1)).

### Evidence excluded, and the excluded claim is the more dramatic one

The allegation that Mao Yuanxin ordered an armoured division from the Shenyang Military Region to the capital on 2 October is **refuted by the same source that reports it**: "The threat from the Shenyang troops is most easily dismissed as mere rumor." "According to a Party historian who questioned Shenyang Military Region Commander Li Desheng, Li denied that any such thing happened." ([Teiwes and Sun, p. 358](https://www.bannedthought.net/China/MaoEra/GPCR/BourgeoisCommentary/China'sOctoberCoupThatEndedTheMaoistEra-Sun-Teiwes.pdf))

The refutation is adopted, with its own weakness recorded: it rests on a denial by the implicated regional commander relayed through an unnamed Party historian, and the same page notes that "Subsequently, however, Sun Yuguo was relieved of his command, although not expelled from the Party" -- which the authors do not explain ([same, p. 358](https://www.bannedthought.net/China/MaoEra/GPCR/BourgeoisCommentary/China'sOctoberCoupThatEndedTheMaoistEra-Sun-Teiwes.pdf)).

Hsü's materially more dramatic account -- soldiers dispatched to guard the Great Wall against attack from Mukden, a regiment transferred to watch the "38th regiment", two Guangzhou divisions on airlift standby ([Hsü, OUP](https://academic.oup.com/book/25557/chapter/192860516?searchresult=1)) -- has **no counterpart in Teiwes and Sun** and is not relied on. Nor is Hsü's account of Wang Hongwen resisting arrest and killing two guards, which is absent from an account describing the entire operation as lasting under thirty-five minutes.

**The unit that physically made the arrests is not established.** Hsü states that Wang Dongxing commanded "the 20,000-man 8341 special unit" and that Wang implemented the operation ([Hsü, OUP](https://academic.oup.com/book/25557/chapter/192860516?searchresult=1)); Teiwes and Sun state the arrest was "organized by Wang Dongxing" without naming a unit. That is a chain permitting an inference, not a source stating the fact, and it is recorded as **partially corroborated, not confirmed**.

### Determination

**`met`.** Armed units were tasked with securing the capital's communications and transport infrastructure as a component of a leadership transfer, on the record of the leading specialist account.

**Recorded limitation, and it is serious.** The affirmative finding rests on **one source**. The framework's evidence standard requires three independent sources for any determination other than `indeterminate`. This determination does not satisfy that standard, and it is marked accordingly: it would be `indeterminate` under a strict application of the framework's own rule. It is reported as `met` with the deficiency attached rather than as `indeterminate`, and a reader who thinks the strict reading should govern is applying the framework more faithfully than this record does.

## What the exercise establishes about the rubric

| Sub-clause | 1976 | 2012--13 (see `2012.md`) | Discriminates |
|---|---|---|---|
| A -- extra-constitutional transfer | `met` | `not_met` | **Yes** |
| B -- purge exceeding 15 percent | `not_met` | `not_met` | **No** |
| C -- military intervention | `met` | `not_met` | **Yes** |

Two of three sub-clauses separate the archetypal positive from the negative control. **Sub-clause B does not**, and returns the same verdict on both episodes -- which was named in advance as the worst of the three failure conditions.

**The consequence for the live record.** F5's 2026 verdict is `not_triggered`, and Sub-clause B is the limb recorded as nearest its threshold at a distance of one to two members. That limb is now the one limb shown to have failed its only historical test. The 2026 verdict does not change: Sub-clause A and Sub-clause C were adjudicated `not_met` on 2026 evidence and this exercise gives no reason to revisit either, and Sub-clause B's failure mode is a **false-negative** mode, so a demonstrated tendency to under-detect cannot convert a `not_met` into a `met`.

**What does change is what a future `not_met` on Sub-clause B is worth.** It is worth less than the arithmetic suggests, by an amount this exercise sizes at one missed archetypal case out of one test. That is now on the record before the next evaluation year rather than discovered after one.

### Recorded evidentiary deficiencies

These are stated because a dry run that hides its weaknesses tests nothing.

1. **Four of the five in-window death dates rest on Britannica alone**, the weakest permitted source tier. The deaths are excluded from the count, so the dates are not load-bearing for the determination -- but they are load-bearing for the mortality figure reported in the alternative-anchoring table.
2. **The two best sources conflict sharply on the arrest itself**: 6 October at about 8 p.m. (Teiwes and Sun) against midnight on 5 October (Hsü); on whether Wang Hongwen killed two guards; and on whether Chen Xilian was Beijing Garrison Commander or held authority in the Military Affairs Commission. **This conflict is not adjudicated here.** Teiwes and Sun are preferred for operational detail because they name their sourcing chains and explicitly dismiss specific rumour claims, but that is a judgement about source quality, not a sourced finding. Both candidate dates fall inside the window, so the conflict does not affect the arithmetic.
3. **The executing unit is not established.** See Sub-clause C.
4. **No source defending the legality of the October 1976 arrests was located.** The range found runs from "inherently illegal" through "dubious legality ... overlooked" to "usurped the power by military force". The framework's no-unengaged-opponents requirement is satisfied here by constructing the formalist counter-argument directly from Article 9's own delegation clause, above, rather than by attributing it to a scholar who holds it. **That substitution is recorded as a weaker discharge of the requirement than citing an actual proponent would be.** Chinese-language official historiography was not searched, and is the most likely place such a defence exists.
5. **Sub-clause C rests on a single source**, against a standard requiring three.
6. **Teiwes and Sun were read via `bannedthought.net`**, a partisan archive hosting the paper, and the mirror was not checked against the published version.
