**Determination.** `met` -- no debt crisis occurred in the evaluation window, on affirmative evidence against each of the four pre-registered crisis indicators.

**Direction of the finding, stated because it matters for how the record should be read.** Clause 2 is a negative qualifier. `met` means *no crisis*, which makes F2 **easier** to trigger, not harder. This determination therefore moves against the thesis the programme is defending. It replaces a `met` recorded by default -- which carried no evidentiary weight and no adverse case -- with a `met` that has both. The determination did not change; its standing did.

The combined verdict is unaffected. Clause 1 is `not_met` on the outturn-only rule, and a quantitative `not_met` short-circuits the conjunction. F2 remains `not_triggered`.

---

## The adverse case, written first

Rule 2 requires the strongest available case that the clause is **not** met -- that is, that a debt crisis **did** occur or is underway. It follows, at full strength.

### The United States lost its last triple-A rating inside the window

Moody's downgraded the United States from Aaa to Aa1 on 16 May 2025, moving the outlook from negative to stable ([Moody's](https://www.moodys.com/web/en/us/about-us/usrating.html)). It is the only rating action of the window and it completed the loss of triple-A across all three major agencies. The stated reasoning: "Repeated administrations and Congress have not been able to reach consensus..." with debt projected at roughly 134 percent of GDP by 2035 ([Reuters, 16 May 2025](https://www.reuters.com/markets/us/moodys-downgrades-us-aa1-rating-2025-05-16/)).

### The official baseline itself describes an unsustainable trajectory

CBO projects debt held by the public rising from 101 percent of GDP to 120 percent by 2036, surpassing the 1946 record of 106 percent ([CBO, *Budget and Economic Outlook: 2026 to 2036*](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/62105); [full report PDF](https://www.cbo.gov/system/files/2026-02/61882-Outlook-2026.pdf)). Interest costs more than double from $970 billion in 2025 to $2.1 trillion by 2036, rising from a record 3.2 percent of GDP to 4.6 percent ([CRFB summary of CBO](https://www.crfb.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/CRFB_CBO_February_2026_Budget_And_Economic_Outlook_3.pdf)).

The Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget reads the same baseline as follows:

> "CBO's latest baseline shows an unsustainable fiscal outlook, with debt approaching record levels, deficits remaining elevated at more than twice a reasonable target, and interest costs exploding."

> "Later in the decade, under CBO's baseline, the average interest rate on all federal debt will exceed nominal economic growth (R>G), which could represent the start of a debt spiral."

> "In this case, a debt spiral would be far more likely and the risk of a fiscal crisis would grow."

([CRFB, February 2026](https://www.crfb.org/papers/cbos-february-2026-budget-and-economic-outlook))

### Long rates reached multi-decade highs and the term premium a twelve-year high

By 18 August 2026 the 30-year yield stood above 5 percent, its highest since 2007, the 10-year near 4.71 percent, the term premium near 80 basis points, and debt near $40 trillion ([Reuters, 18 Aug 2026](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/selling-grips-bond-markets-us-japan-inflation-fiscal-worries-take-hold-2026-08-18/)). Named practitioners read this as fiscal in origin: Jonas Goltermann of Capital Economics said the selloff "suggests investors are losing patience with fiscal profligacy" and was "entirely unsurprising: the fiscal outlook in several major economies is problematic"; Gennadiy Goldberg of TD said "the long-end has been subjected to death by a thousand cuts" (same source).

In May 2026 the 30-year had reached 5.2 percent, "highest level in 19 years", roughly 35 basis points above CBO's forecast path, against a requirement to refinance $10 trillion of debt over the following year while the average coupon on notes of 5 to 30 years -- over half of all federal debt -- stood at 3.23 percent ([Fortune, 30 May 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/05/30/national-debt-treasury-bond-rates-fiscal-crisis/)). That is a refinancing-risk argument, not merely a level argument.

### Market functioning did deteriorate, measurably, and the official record says so

In April 2025 the 10-year yield rose 47 basis points between 4 and 11 April; on-the-run 10-year depth "declined to about one-quarter of recent levels", reaching "the lowest levels since March 2023"; bid-ask spreads on long off-the-run issues roughly doubled; the 10-year swap spread moved about 50 basis points ([NY Fed, Perli, 9 May 2025](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/speeches/2025/per250509)). The Federal Reserve's own figure caption is blunt: "Treasury market depth fell significantly in April from already low levels" ([Fed FSR, April 2025](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/financial-stability-report-20250425.pdf)). The IMF recorded that "dealers reportedly reached their intermediation limits and market liquidity deteriorated" ([IMF GFSR, April 2025, Ch.1](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/gfsr/2025/april/english/ch1.pdf)). FSOC recorded "wider bid-ask spreads, a decline in market depth, and an increase in the price impact of transactions" ([FSOC 2025 Annual Report](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/261/FSOC2025AnnualReport.pdf)).

By May 2026 the Fed recorded that liquidity had "briefly declined", that the episode demonstrated "the vulnerability of market liquidity during periods of acute stress", and -- this is the adverse detail -- that "market depth for the most liquid 2-year on-the-run Treasury note stayed at historically low levels" ([Fed FSR, May 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/financial-stability-report-20260508.pdf)).

### The structural vulnerability is officially described, by the BIS, as a leveraged one

> "Given dealers' balance sheet constraints, hedge funds have increasingly stepped into the market as marginal buyers of government bonds as part of a 'basis' trade... Hedge funds use the repo market to fund the purchase of the cash government bond and to lever up returns."

([BIS Quarterly Review foreword, 8 Dec 2025](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt2512_foreword.htm))

The Fed adds that hedge fund leverage "remained stable at record-high levels" and that dealer value-at-risk "can rise rapidly when volatility increases" ([Fed FSR, May 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/financial-stability-report-20260508.pdf)).

### The fiscal process failed visibly twice

The debt limit was reinstated at $36.1 trillion on 2 January 2025 and raised by $5.0 trillion to $41.1 trillion on 4 July 2025 under P.L. 119-21, with extraordinary measures in use throughout the intervening period ([CRS IN10837](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IN10837)). A 43-day government shutdown ran from 1 October to 12 November 2025, the longest in US history ([Reuters, 12 Nov 2025](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/us-house-vote-deal-end-longest-government-shutdown-history-2025-11-12/)).

Fitch, affirming AA+ on 13 August 2026, recorded the deficit widening to 7.4 percent of GDP, "the highest among 'AA'-rated sovereigns" ([Reuters, 13 Aug 2026](https://www.reuters.com/business/fitch-keeps-united-states-rating-aa-2026-08-13/)).

### The adverse case's own honest summary

At full strength the adverse case establishes: a completed loss of triple-A, an official baseline showing record debt and R exceeding G late in the decade, the highest long-bond yields in nearly two decades, a twelve-year-high term premium, a measured liquidity deterioration acknowledged by every official body that measures it, a leveraged marginal buyer of Treasuries, and two visible failures of the fiscal process.

**What it does not establish is a crisis under the four indicators pre-registered for this clause.** Its strongest claims are about the trajectory and about the level of rates. On functioning, it establishes acute deterioration and recovery.

## Reasoning

The clause is assessed against the four indicators recorded in the 2026 baseline entry: a failed benchmark auction, emergency central-bank Treasury-market intervention, a missed federal debt payment, and documented sustained market-functioning impairment. Each is checked affirmatively rather than by absence of reporting.

### (i) No failed benchmark auction

Every benchmark auction in the window cleared. The weakest sourced result located is the 20-year bond auction of 21 May 2025: bid-to-cover 2.46 with primary-dealer takedown of 16.91 percent, characterised in reporting as tepid demand ([TreasuryDirect auction results, 21 May 2025](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/instit/annceresult/press/preanre/2025/R_20250521_2.pdf); [Reuters, 21 May 2025](https://www.reuters.com/business/tepid-demand-us-treasury-auction-shows-investor-jitters-about-tax-bill-deficit-2025-05-21/)). A bid-to-cover above 2.4 is not a failure.

Claimed record-low bid-to-cover and record-low dealer-takedown figures circulated in secondary commentary and are **not used**, because they trace to a social-media post rather than to Treasury's own record-setting-auction dataset, which was identified but not retrieved. Recorded as a gap rather than filled with the unsourced figures.

### (ii) No emergency Fed intervention

Balance-sheet runoff ended on 1 December 2025 **as scheduled under the pre-announced framework**, not as a response to market stress ([Federal Reserve policy normalisation Q&A](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/policy-normalization-qa.htm); [FOMC minutes, 29 Oct 2025](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcminutes20251029.htm)). Reserve-management bill purchases of roughly $40 billion per month began in December 2025 ([FOMC minutes, 10 Dec 2025](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcminutes20251210.htm); [NY Fed, Perli, 26 Mar 2026](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/speeches/2026/per260326)).

Standing Repo Facility usage reached $50.35 billion on 31 October 2025 and $74.6 billion on 31 December 2025 ([Reuters, 31 Dec 2025](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/banks-tap-record-liquidity-new-york-feds-standing-repo-facility-2025-12-31/)). **This is a standing facility being used as designed at quarter-end, not a rescue.** The distinction is load-bearing and a reader may reasonably test it; the reason it holds is that the facility's terms were unchanged, its usage was at pre-set rates, and no new programme was created. 2026 SRF figures are `n.a.` -- the NY Fed data hub returned a technical error -- and the absence is recorded rather than estimated.

The Reserve Bank of Australia's Deputy Governor characterises the same tool set as a source of confidence rather than of alarm: the market "weathered recent turbulence well, bolstered by confidence in the growing array of liquidity tools available from the Federal Reserve" ([RBA, 7 Mar 2026](https://www.rba.gov.au/speeches/2026/sp-dg-2026-03-07.html)).

### (iii) No missed payment

No missed federal debt payment occurred. The debt limit was binding for roughly six months of 2025 and was raised before extraordinary measures were exhausted ([CRS IN10837](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IN10837)). OFR's assessment of that period: "The Treasury market functioned well during an episode of a binding debt ceiling that ended in July" ([OFR 2025 Annual Report](https://www.financialresearch.gov/annual-reports/files/OFR-AR-2025.pdf)).

The 43-day shutdown was an appropriations failure, not a debt-service failure. A claimed 76-day partial DHS shutdown beginning 14 February 2026 rests only on a Wikipedia summary and is **flagged unverified**; it would not be a missed debt payment in any case.

### (iv) Impairment acute, but not sustained

This is the indicator that requires the most care, because the deterioration was real and officially measured.

**What did not happen.** The cash-futures basis trade did not unwind. The New York Fed's assessment is headed "No Evidence of Unwind of the Basis Trade", finding that "the basis remained relatively stable" and "it was nowhere close to what happened in March 2020" ([NY Fed, Perli, 9 May 2025](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/speeches/2025/per250509)). OFR, with proprietary repo data, records that "a material unwind of this trade did not occur in April 2025", that "hedge fund repo borrowing with Treasuries as collateral was at least $1.4 trillion with no large declines in April or May", and that repo borrowing against cheapest-to-deliver Treasuries **rose** -- the opposite of what an unwind would produce ([OFR 2025 Annual Report](https://www.financialresearch.gov/annual-reports/files/OFR-AR-2025.pdf)). What unwound was the swap-spread trade, roughly $60 billion in April and $40 billion in May, with positions back to about $305 billion by September 2025 ([Federal Reserve FEDS Notes, 22 Jun 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/decomposing-hedge-funds-u-s-treasury-exposures-20260622.html)). FSOC records that "unlike March 2020, spreads between off-the-run and on-the-run Treasuries remained steady in April, and there was limited stress in the cash-futures basis trade" ([FSOC 2025 Annual Report](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/261/FSOC2025AnnualReport.pdf)).

**Recovery, measured.** "Depth quickly recovered and by late summer 2025 was at levels similar to, if not better than, any time since the Fed's post-COVID tightening cycle started in March 2022. Depth continued trending higher through February 2026" ([Liberty Street Economics, 2 Apr 2026](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/04/treasury-market-liquidity-since-april-2025/)). The Fed's November 2025 FSR records that liquidity "increased back to or above previous levels across all maturities" ([Fed FSR, Nov 2025](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/financial-stability-report-20251107.pdf)).

**The March--April 2026 episode.** A brief Middle East-driven deterioration occurred and reversed, though 2-year on-the-run depth remained near its first quartile ([Fed FSR, May 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/financial-stability-report-20260508.pdf)). The IMF: "Financial markets have so far functioned in an orderly manner amid the war in the Middle East... market liquidity has not materially deteriorated" ([IMF GFSR, April 2026, Ch.1](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/gfsr/2026/april/english/ch1.pdf)). The Bank of England, as a non-US cross-check: "both cash and repo markets have remained resilient, and market conditions have improved since March 2026, though liquidity remains somewhat reduced relative to long-run averages" ([BoE FSR, July 2026](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/financial-stability-report/2026/july-2026)).

**August 2026.** The 30-year above 5 percent is a level and term-premium event. No source in the record identifies a functioning impairment accompanying it ([Reuters, 18 Aug 2026](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/selling-grips-bond-markets-us-japan-inflation-fiscal-worries-take-hold-2026-08-18/)).

**Corroborating official assessments of functioning:** "trading remained orderly, and markets continued to function without serious disruption" ([Fed FSR, April 2025](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/financial-stability-report-20250425.pdf)); "Treasury markets experienced heightened volatility during April 2025 but operated without material disruption" and "Repo and other money markets functioned without disruption in April 2025" ([OFR 2025 Annual Report](https://www.financialresearch.gov/annual-reports/files/OFR-AR-2025.pdf)); "During the last three months markets have remained orderly and liquid" ([TBAC, 30 Jul 2025](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0213)); and the ratings-side affirmations by S&P at AA+/stable on 26 June 2026 ([Reuters, 26 Jun 2026](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/sp-affirms-aa-credit-rating-us-cites-economic-resilience-2026-06-26/)) and Fitch at AA+ on 13 August 2026 ([Reuters, 13 Aug 2026](https://www.reuters.com/business/fitch-keeps-united-states-rating-aa-2026-08-13/)).

**A misattribution that must not be made, recorded because it would be convenient.** The BIS statement that markets showed "no clear signs of liquidity impairment or market dysfunction" appears in the foreword to a **foreign-exchange and interest-rate-derivatives** special edition and is supported by FX-specific evidence ([BIS, 8 Dec 2025](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt2512_foreword.htm)). **It is not an assessment of cash Treasury market functioning and is not used as one here.** A BIS measured assessment of cash Treasury functioning in April 2025 remains `n.a.`

### The shape of the disagreement

The crisis case and the no-crisis case barely overlap in claim. The no-crisis case concerns **market functioning** and is supported by measured liquidity, repo and positioning data held by the institutions that produce it. The crisis case concerns **the fiscal trajectory and the level of rates** and is supported by CBO's own baseline. Nothing in the no-crisis evidence contradicts the crisis case's projections; nothing in the crisis case identifies a functioning failure.

The clause as pre-registered tests the first. That is a **choice made in advance**, and it is a limitation: F2's qualifier would return `met` in a world of sharply rising debt-service costs, provided the plumbing held. A reader who thinks the qualifier should have been written about sustainability rather than functioning is disagreeing with the pre-registration, and the pre-registration is not amended in response.

Note also that "fiscal debt sustainability" appears among the most-cited shocks in both the Spring 2026 and Fall 2025 Federal Reserve market-participant surveys ([Fed FSR, May 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/financial-stability-report-20260508.pdf)). Participant concern is a position, not a functioning failure, and it is recorded at that weight.

## Contestability

`contestable: true`, on three grounds.

1. **The four indicators are a narrow operationalisation.** They test the payments and plumbing layer. A crisis defined as a sustained loss of the sovereign's fiscal room would score differently on the same evidence, and CBO's own baseline supplies the material.
2. **The standing-facility distinction.** Treating record Standing Repo Facility usage as ordinary rather than as intervention is a judgement. It is defended above on unchanged terms and pre-set rates, but a reader could hold that record usage of a liquidity backstop is what intervention looks like once the backstop is permanent.
3. **The 2-year depth finding.** The Fed's own May 2026 statement that 2-year on-the-run depth "stayed at historically low levels" is the single piece of official evidence most consistent with *sustained* impairment, and it sits against Liberty Street's finding of recovering and rising depth. Both are Federal Reserve sources. They are reconciled here as different maturities and different measures, but the reconciliation is not airtight.

## What would change this determination

Any one of: a failed benchmark auction; a new Federal Reserve programme created in response to Treasury-market conditions; a missed or delayed payment on federal debt; or measured functioning impairment persisting across multiple quarters rather than resolving within one.
