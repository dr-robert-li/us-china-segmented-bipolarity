**Determination.** `not_met`, at a distance of **one member**.

This is the nearest any F5 limb comes to its threshold, and on the reading below it is arguably the nearest condition in the entire eight-condition set. One further Politburo expulsion inside the counting window satisfies it.

---

## The adverse case, written first

Rule 2 requires the strongest available case that this clause **is** met, written before the determination and published whatever the determination turns out to be. It follows.

### The removals are real, senior, and clustered

Between October 2025 and July 2026 the 20th Politburo lost members by expulsion, not by retirement or death.

**He Weidong**, full Politburo member and Vice Chairman of the Central Military Commission, was expelled from the Party and from military service on 17 October 2025, the announcement stating he was "suspected of major duty-related crimes" ([State Council of the PRC, 17 Oct 2025](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202510/17/content_WS68f244ddc6d00ca5f9a06dec.html)). The expulsion was confirmed by the Fourth Plenum of the 20th Central Committee on 23 October 2025 ([State Council of the PRC, 23 Oct 2025](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202510/23/content_WS68fa3825c6d00ca5f9a06fbe.html)).

**Ma Xingrui**, full Politburo member, was expelled by Politburo decision of 30 June 2026, announced 14 July 2026 ([Xinhua, 14 Jul 2026](http://english.news.cn/20260714/d57f4b7648f148d98b98d1f6db135bb2/c.html)).

**Zhang Youxia**, full Politburo member and first-ranked Vice Chairman of the Central Military Commission, was placed under investigation, announced 24 January 2026 ([Ministry of National Defense of the PRC](http://eng.mod.gov.cn/2025xb/N/T/16439139.html)). At least one major outlet has since counted him as expelled, reporting the Politburo "down to 21 members following the expulsion of He Weidong and Zhang Youxia" ([The New York Times, 14 Jul 2026](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/07/14/world/asia/china-expels-politburo-official-sex-corruption.html)).

On the New York Times count, three of twenty-four are gone. On a denominator of twenty-four that is 12.5 percent; the threshold is 15 percent, or four members. On the count of members remaining that the same report gives -- twenty-one -- the removals are three against a body that has lost an eighth of itself in nine months.

### The portfolio concentration indicator is satisfied about as strongly as it could be

The adapter lists "military or security portfolio concentration" as an indicator weighing toward `met`. Both military Vice Chairmen of the Central Military Commission are gone or under investigation. The Commission itself fell from seven members in 2023 to two -- Xi Jinping and Zhang Shengmin -- by 2026 ([China Aerospace Studies Institute, Air University, 13 Jul 2026](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/Portals/10/CASI/documents/Research/Other-Topics/2026-07-13%20PLA%20Corruption.pdf?ver=3FCdY-htIjQJMdbipAVSgg==)). M. Taylor Fravel's assessment is that the Commission "has been organizationally decapitated", with roughly 52 percent of PLA leadership positions impacted and 101 senior officers dismissed or missing ([CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/assessing-xis-unprecedented-purges-chinas-military-key-developments-and-potential)).

If the clause were written about the Central Military Commission rather than the Politburo, it would have been satisfied several times over.

### Temporal clustering and proximity to a succession event are satisfied

He Weidong's expulsion was announced six days before the Fourth Plenum and confirmed at it. The adapter's "proximity to a succession event -- removals cluster around a congress, plenum, or leadership transition" indicator is met on its face.

### The scholarly case that this is succession conflict rather than discipline enforcement

The adapter's controlling question is whether a removal is "more plausibly explained by leadership-succession conflict than by enforcement of standing discipline". Serious published work argues the former.

Matthew Johnson describes the pattern as "deepening, rather than resolving, long-term regime fragility", and states the succession problem directly: "There are no durable procedures for succession. There is only the leader and the system he holds together" ([Johnson, "Late Stalinism in Beijing", China Brief 26(3), 26 Jan 2026](https://jamestown.org/late-stalinism-in-beijing/)). Brookings records the mechanism by which the two become inseparable: "The real danger for the party is if succession politicking starts to infect the policymaking process" ([Brookings, 26 Nov 2025](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/assessing-chinas-fourth-plenum-policy-continuity-personnel-turmoil/)). A *Journal of Democracy* online exclusive puts the strongest version: "By systematically purging all capable and prestigious potential heirs, Xi is engineering a monumental succession crisis" ([Journal of Democracy](https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/online-exclusive/why-xi-jinping-is-purging-chinas-top-leaders/)).

The PLA Daily's own editorial language on the Zhang Youxia investigation is unusually charged for a discipline case, holding that the system of "ultimate responsibility resting with the CMC chairman" had been "seriously trampled on and undermined" ([Ministry of National Defense of the PRC](http://eng.mod.gov.cn/2025xb/N/T/16439159.html)). That is a claim about authority over the armed forces, not about procurement fraud.

### The adverse case's own honest summary

Taken at its strongest, the adverse case establishes: three senior removals, both military Vice Chairmen among them, a decapitated Central Military Commission, plenum-proximate timing, and a body of published scholarship reading the episode as succession-driven. On the most permissive admissible member count it reaches three of twenty-four. **It falls short on arithmetic, not on argument.**

## Reasoning

### The denominator

The 20th Politburo comprises **24 full members** including the 7-member Standing Committee, elected 22--23 October 2022 ([International Department of the CPC Central Committee](https://www.idcpc.org.cn/english2023/tjzl/cpcjj/leadershipof20thCentralCommittee/)), corroborated by [CSIS's April 2023 tabulation](https://csis-website-prod.s3.amazonaws.com/s3fs-public/2023-04/230427_China_20th_Politburo_0.pdf). The Standing Committee is counted **within** the 24, not additionally, per the adapter's provisional position on Standing Committee removals.

The Party does not add full Politburo members between Congresses. The Fourth Plenum of October 2025 promoted eleven alternates to full Central Committee membership and added Zhang Shengmin to the Central Military Commission; it seated no new Politburo member ([State Council of the PRC, 23 Oct 2025](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202510/23/content_WS68fa3825c6d00ca5f9a06fbe.html)). No replacement is available to restore the denominator.

**The denominator choice does not change the threshold here, and this is worth stating because it removes a discretionary lever.** `ceil(0.15 * 24) = 4` and `ceil(0.15 * 23) = 4`. Whether the count is taken as elected or as seated after He Weidong's expulsion, the member threshold is four. The determination does not depend on resolving it.

The denominator recorded is **24**, per the window-anchoring correction now appended to `pipeline/adapters/F5.md`. That correction was made because the adapter as originally written permitted a denominator that shrank with each removal, which would have made every subsequent removal easier to count -- a ratchet that could carry the clause across its threshold on arithmetic rather than on evidence.

### The numerator, and the conflict in the record

| Member | Status | Counted |
|---|---|---|
| He Weidong | Expelled 17 Oct 2025, confirmed at Fourth Plenum 23 Oct 2025 | **Yes** |
| Ma Xingrui | Expelled by Politburo decision 30 Jun 2026, announced 14 Jul 2026 | **Yes** |
| Zhang Youxia | Under investigation, announced 24 Jan 2026; no expulsion notice located | **No** |

**The record genuinely conflicts on Zhang Youxia and this is not a search failure.** Reuters, on 4 March 2026, referred to "the 23-member Politburo", counting him as seated ([Reuters, 4 Mar 2026](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/two-senior-communist-party-officials-absent-top-chinese-political-event-2026-03-04/)). The New York Times, on 14 July 2026, counted him as expelled ([NYT, 14 Jul 2026](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/07/14/world/asia/china-expels-politburo-official-sex-corruption.html)). The Congressional Research Service, with data as of 27 April 2026, records that "the Politburo has lost one member (expelled), with two members under investigation" ([CRS IF12505](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/HTML/IF12505.html)).

**The CRS formulation is adopted**, for a reason that has to be stated because it cuts against the falsifier: no official expulsion notice for Zhang Youxia exists in the retrievable record, and the framework's evidence standard treats an official PRC statement as evidence of a position, never of a fact, but treats its **absence** as the absence of the position. An investigation announced is a position that an investigation is underway. It is not a position that a member has been removed.

Adopting the NYT count would raise the numerator to three, which is still short of four. **The conflict is therefore not outcome-determining either.** It is recorded because it will be outcome-determining in a later year.

### The purge-versus-enforcement test

Both counted removals arrived with published discipline findings, in the ordinary investigative sequence, and were confirmed through standing Party organs. Under the adapter's exclusion rule -- "a removal accompanied by a published discipline finding, arriving in an ordinary investigative sequence, and not clustered, counts toward **nothing**" -- a strict reading would count **zero**.

They are counted at **two** rather than zero, on the clustering and portfolio-concentration indicators, which are satisfied. This is the more adverse reading of the adapter and is chosen deliberately: applying the exclusion rule at full strength would let the clause be cleared by the Party's own procedural regularity, which is exactly the asymmetry Rule 1 exists to prevent.

Against that, the contrary evidence on the enforcement reading is specific rather than general. Ma Xingrui's charge sheet contains **no political accusations**, which cuts directly against the factional-conflict interpretation of his removal ([Jamestown Foundation](https://jamestown.org/no-political-charges-as-ma-xingrui-expelled-from-politburo/)). Joel Wuthnow reads the pattern as consolidation rather than fragility: "The purges suggest that Xi remains in firm control" -- while recording the limit of his own reading, that "there is also a risk in overstating Xi's authority... 'Absolute leadership' of the party over the army remains elusive even for Xi at the height of his power" ([Wuthnow, China Leadership Monitor 87, 28 Feb 2026](https://www.prcleader.org/post/why-is-xi-still-purging-his-generals)). Brookings states the same duality in one line: "Xi made them, and he could break them" ([Brookings, 26 Nov 2025](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/assessing-chinas-fourth-plenum-policy-continuity-personnel-turmoil/)).

### The arithmetic

```
denominator          = 24   (full members, 20th Politburo, as elected)
threshold_members    = ceil(0.15 * 24) = 4
qualifying_removals  = 2    (He Weidong, Ma Xingrui)
"exceeding 15%"      => >= 4 members required
2 < 4                => not_met
```

**Distance: two members on the adopted count, one member on the most permissive admissible count.** The one-member figure is the one recorded, because it is the adverse figure and the log should carry the adverse figure.

## Contestability

`contestable: true`. Three grounds are available to a reader who wants to reach `met`.

1. **The Zhang Youxia count.** Adopting the New York Times position gives three of twenty-four. Still short of four, but it halves the distance and would make a single further removal decisive on any count.
2. **The unit of analysis.** If the clause were read as testing the leadership's security apparatus rather than the Politburo as a body, the Central Military Commission's reduction from seven members to two satisfies it comfortably. The narrow reading is the pre-registered one and is not widened here, but the wider reading is not unreasonable and this record does not pretend otherwise.
3. **Unannounced status changes.** The adapter's own open items flag "members whose status is unannounced but evidently changed". Two Politburo members were absent from the March 2026 CPPCC session. Absence is not removal, and this record does not treat it as such, but a reader who weighted it differently would reach a different numerator.

## What would change this determination

One further expulsion of a full Politburo member inside the counting window, officially announced. On the adopted count that reaches three of four; on the NYT count it reaches the threshold. **F5-B should therefore be treated as the live limb of F5 and reviewed on announcement rather than annually.**

## The pre-registration test this record passes, and the one it does not

It passes the harder one: the threshold was not moved on approach. Four members remains four members at a distance of one.

It does not pass cleanly on specification compliance, because the adapter's window and denominator anchoring were ambiguous until corrected in the same commit as this record. The correction was made while the clause is `not_met` at a distance, which is the only point at which such a correction is uncontaminated by its consequences. Had it been made after a third removal, it would have been indistinguishable from fitting the rule to the outcome, and no amount of stated good faith would have repaired that.

## Amendments

### Amendment 1 -- 2026-08-19 -- citation audit corrections

A post-publication audit of this record's citations found three defects. All three are corrected here by appended amendment rather than by editing the body above, and none changes the `not_met` determination.

**1. The denominator's load-bearing clause was attributed to a source that does not state it.**

The Reasoning section asserts "24 full members **including** the 7-member Standing Committee" and cites the International Department of the CPC Central Committee. That page was re-fetched. It states that "Twenty-four members of the Political Bureau of the CPC Central Committee were also elected at the session" and separately names the seven Standing Committee members ([IDCPC](https://www.idcpc.org.cn/english2023/tjzl/cpcjj/leadershipof20thCentralCommittee/)). It does **not** state whether the twenty-four include the Standing Committee, and does not enumerate the twenty-four by name. The word "also" is, if anything, weak evidence for the additive reading -- twenty-four **in addition to** seven, for thirty-one.

This matters because the two readings give different thresholds: `ceil(0.15 * 24) = 4` against `ceil(0.15 * 31) = 5`. The inclusive reading is the **less** favourable one for this determination.

The inclusive reading is nevertheless correct, and is now supported by sources that state it explicitly rather than by inference: the Organisation for Research on China and Asia records that "24 members were appointed in the Politburo of CPC and seven out of these 24 were appointed to the Politburo Standing Committee" ([ORCA](https://orcasia.org/pages/20cc/overview)); the Geopolitics and Security Studies Centre records that "the Politburo has shrunk from 25 to 24 members" while "the size of the Standing Committee has remained unchanged and still consists of 7 people" ([GSSC](https://www.gssc.lt/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/National-Congress-of-the-CPC.pdf)); and a European policy brief tabulates the twenty-four with the seven Standing Committee members inside the table ([DWARC / China Horizons](https://chinahorizons.eu/images/docs/policy_brief/DWARC_Policy_Brief_The_20th_Congress_of_the_CCP_FINAL.pdf)). A contemporaneous press description of the body as "23 in total, including seven Standing Committee members" after He Weidong's expulsion is consistent ([Chosun, 4 Mar 2026](https://www.chosun.com/english/world-en/2026/03/04/KQGFGN4VHFDNZGHLDBISYSTPAA/)).

The standing of the two denominator sources in the table above is therefore **reversed**: the enumeration is what establishes the count, and the primary Party organ corroborates the total only. This is recorded rather than quietly restated, because a record that cites a primary source for a claim the source does not make is defective even when the claim is true, and the defect is worse here than a wrong number would be -- it would have survived a reader checking whether the source existed, and failed only against a reader checking what it said.

**2. The election date is imprecise.** The body reads "elected 22--23 October 2022". The 20th National Congress sat 16--22 October 2022 and the Politburo was elected the following day, at the first plenary session of the 20th Central Committee, on **23 October 2022** ([DWARC / China Horizons](https://chinahorizons.eu/images/docs/policy_brief/DWARC_Policy_Brief_The_20th_Congress_of_the_CCP_FINAL.pdf)). The election date is not load-bearing for any threshold.

**3. The second member under investigation was left unnamed, and is now named.** The body adopts the CRS formulation "one member expelled, with two members under investigation" at data-as-of 27 April 2026 without identifying the second. The two are **Zhang Youxia**, announced 24 January 2026, and **Ma Xingrui**, announced 3 April 2026 ([Reuters, 3 Apr 2026](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/chinese-politburo-member-ma-xingrui-under-investigation-by-anti-graft-watchdog-2026-04-03/)). Ma was subsequently expelled and is already counted in the numerator, so naming him changes nothing arithmetically; the CRS snapshot simply predates his expulsion.

**A candidate for the second name is explicitly ruled out.** Liu Zhenli, Chief of Staff of the CMC Joint Staff Department, was placed under investigation alongside Zhang Youxia in the same announcement ([Reuters, 24 Jan 2026](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/china-investigating-senior-military-officials-zhang-youxia-liu-zhenli-says-2026-01-24/)). He is **not** a full Politburo member: he does not appear in the enumeration of the twenty-four ([20th Central Committee membership list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_Central_Committee_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party)), and is described throughout as a Central Military Commission member. He therefore counts toward neither numerator nor denominator. This is checked and recorded because if he were a Politburo member the numerator picture would move, and an unchecked assumption in that position is the kind of thing that later looks like convenience.

**What the amendment does not change.** The determination remains `not_met`. Denominator 24, threshold 4, counted removals 2, distance one to two members. Under the anchoring rule adopted in the same commit as this record -- first official public announcement of expulsion -- announced investigations count zero regardless of how many there are or whom they name.

**One external framing is worth recording against this clause.** Bloomberg headlined the Ma Xingrui investigation "China Probes Politburo Member in Widest Purge Since 1976" ([Bloomberg, 3 Apr 2026](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-03/china-probes-politburo-member-in-widest-purge-since-1976)), carried also by the [Straits Times](https://www.straitstimes.com/asia/east-asia/china-probes-politburo-member-in-widest-purge-since-1976). That comparison is a journalistic judgement, not a measurement, and it is not evidence for the clause. It is recorded because the historical episode it names is the one this project uses to test whether the Sub-clause B rubric discriminates at all.
