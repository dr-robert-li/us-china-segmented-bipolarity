**Determination.** `not_met`, on affirmative evidence that the causal direction ran from civilian Party organs to the military rather than the reverse.

This is the limb where the framework's evidence standards do the most work, and where a reader is most entitled to ask whether they were applied symmetrically.

---

## The adverse case, written first

### The Central Military Commission was reduced to two members

The Commission fell from seven members in 2023 to two by 2026 -- Xi Jinping and Zhang Shengmin ([China Aerospace Studies Institute, Air University, 13 Jul 2026](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/Portals/10/CASI/documents/Research/Other-Topics/2026-07-13%20PLA%20Corruption.pdf?ver=3FCdY-htIjQJMdbipAVSgg==)). M. Taylor Fravel's assessment is that the Commission "has been organizationally decapitated", with roughly 52 percent of PLA leadership positions impacted and 101 senior officers dismissed or missing ([CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/assessing-xis-unprecedented-purges-chinas-military-key-developments-and-potential)).

### The Party's own language describes a challenge to command authority

The PLA Daily editorial accompanying the Zhang Youxia investigation held that the system of "ultimate responsibility resting with the CMC chairman" had been "seriously trampled on and undermined" ([Ministry of National Defense of the PRC](http://eng.mod.gov.cn/2025xb/N/T/16439159.html)).

Read at its strongest, that is an official statement that the chain of command over the armed forces was interfered with by senior officers. If the interference concerned who commands, and command is the instrument of any succession, then the distance between this and interference in succession is a matter of interpretation rather than of kind.

### Reporting of an operational incident and of contested control

Reporting attributed to *The Wall Street Journal* alleged a nuclear-related incident and, relayed onward, framed the Zhang Youxia investigation in terms of contested control of the armed forces ([The Guardian, 26 Jan 2026](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jan/26/china-top-ranking-general-zhang-youxia-investigation)). Dissident and diaspora outlets carried stronger versions alleging plotting against the leadership.

### Wuthnow's concession, which is the strongest single line available to this case

Joel Wuthnow's overall reading is that the purges indicate consolidation. He nonetheless records: "there is also a risk in overstating Xi's authority... 'Absolute leadership' of the party over the army remains elusive even for Xi at the height of his power" ([Wuthnow, China Leadership Monitor 87, 28 Feb 2026](https://www.prcleader.org/post/why-is-xi-still-purging-his-generals)).

An analyst arguing for `met` would put it this way: if absolute Party control over the army is elusive at the height of the leader's power, then the army is an autonomous actor in the succession question by default, whether or not it has yet acted.

## Reasoning

### The affirmative evidence, and the direction of the causal arrow

Every military removal in the window was initiated by a civilian Party organ and confirmed by another. He Weidong's expulsion was announced by the state on 17 October 2025 and confirmed by the Central Committee in plenary session on 23 October 2025 ([State Council, 17 Oct 2025](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202510/17/content_WS68f244ddc6d00ca5f9a06dec.html); [State Council, 23 Oct 2025](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202510/23/content_WS68fa3825c6d00ca5f9a06fbe.html)). The Zhang Youxia investigation was announced through the Ministry of National Defense under standing discipline procedure ([Ministry of National Defense](http://eng.mod.gov.cn/2025xb/N/T/16439139.html)). The one Politburo expulsion in 2026 with a published decision trail was decided by the Politburo itself ([Xinhua, 14 Jul 2026](http://english.news.cn/20260714/d57f4b7648f148d98b98d1f6db135bb2/c.html)).

**The arrow runs Party to military.** What the record shows is civilian organs removing officers, which is the opposite of officers determining a leadership outcome. Sub-clause C tests "deployment or threatened deployment of military or armed-police force to determine, block, or secure a leadership transition". No source in the record places military units in that role.

The adapter also settles the classification question in advance: "Personnel changes in the military command... fall under Sub-clause B if they qualify at all." The entire Central Military Commission reduction is therefore Sub-clause B material, where it is assessed and where it contributes to a `not_met` at a distance of one member. It is not double-counted here.

### Why the coup and irregular-control reporting is inadmissible for `met`

The framework's evidence standard is that anonymous or single-outlet reporting is insufficient alone for `met` on any clause, and the adapter states that this standard "binds hardest here". Applying it:

- The nuclear-incident and contested-control claims trace to a single outlet's anonymous sourcing, relayed by others ([The Guardian, 26 Jan 2026](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jan/26/china-top-ranking-general-zhang-youxia-investigation)). Relay by additional outlets does not create independence.
- Dissident and diaspora accounts are single-outlet on their own terms and are not independently corroborated.

**This is the point at which asymmetry would be easiest to introduce and hardest to detect, so the test is stated explicitly: would the same evidence be discounted if it pointed the other way?** It would. A single anonymously-sourced report that the military had *guaranteed* an orderly succession would not be admitted as affirmative evidence for `not_met` either. The standard is what forces this determination to rest on the civilian-initiation record instead, and that record is officially published and independently reported.

### The contrary evidence on the coup reading

Two independent assessments reach the negative directly. One characterises the coup interpretation as unsupported ([Chosun, 9 Feb 2026](https://www.chosun.com/english/opinion-en/2026/02/09/4242JKITMFHFFJ2JEIQEN7G64M/)). The Lowy Institute argues that reports of a coup or of the leader's decline are premature ([Lowy Institute, The Interpreter](https://www.lowyinstitute.org/the-interpreter/china-coup-rumours-xi-jinping-s-decline-are-premature)). The publication date of the Lowy piece could not be resolved -- page metadata and search indexing disagree, and internal content is consistent with mid-2025 -- so no date is asserted and its weight is reduced accordingly.

### The division in the scholarship, recorded rather than resolved

The adapter requires that where scholarly assessment is genuinely divided, the determination record the division and return `indeterminate` rather than adopt the majority view. **The division is real but it is not on this question.** Johnson and Wuthnow disagree about whether the purges indicate fragility or consolidation ([Johnson, China Brief 26(3), 26 Jan 2026](https://jamestown.org/late-stalinism-in-beijing/); [Wuthnow, China Leadership Monitor 87, 28 Feb 2026](https://www.prcleader.org/post/why-is-xi-still-purging-his-generals)). Neither asserts that military force was deployed or threatened to determine a leadership transition. A division about the meaning of an agreed set of events does not make the events indeterminate, and returning `indeterminate` here would be `indeterminate` used as a default, which Rule 3 forbids.

## Contestability

`contestable: true`, on one ground and it is a substantial one.

**The determination rests on a distinction between civilian-directed purging of the military and military intervention in succession, and that distinction is carrying more weight than the adapter anticipated.** The adapter assumed the two would be separable because military intervention would be visible as deployment. What the window actually produced was the removal of the entire senior military leadership by civilian organs -- an event the adapter routes to Sub-clause B, where it does not reach the threshold, while Sub-clause C finds it out of scope.

The consequence is worth stating plainly against interest: **F5's disjunction contains a seam through which the most consequential civil-military event in the window passes without satisfying any limb.** A reader who regards decapitation of the Central Military Commission as the substance of what F5 was written to detect has a legitimate complaint about the specification, and the correct response is to record it rather than to reinterpret a clause into covering it.

A second, weaker ground: if the anonymous reporting on contested control of the armed forces is later corroborated by independent sourcing, this determination should be revisited by amendment. It is not currently corroborated.

## What would change this determination

Independently corroborated evidence -- three independent sources, per the framework -- that military or armed-police units were deployed or threatened in order to determine, block, or secure a leadership transition. Corroboration of the currently single-sourced contested-control reporting would be the most likely route.
