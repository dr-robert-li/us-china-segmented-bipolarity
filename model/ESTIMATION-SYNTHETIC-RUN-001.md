## Validation design

Two stages, the first cheap and the second expensive, both runnable from the committed code.

**Stage 1 -- prior-moment cross-check.** 2,000 draws from the NumPyro model's prior (`Predictive`) against 2,000 draws from the independent numpy `sample_prior()`. Every scalar and vector parameter agrees in mean and SD within 0.12/0.15 standardised tolerance. This catches prior-transcription errors that parameter recovery can miss (a wrong prior can still "recover" a truth drawn from the same wrong prior). **PASS.**

**Stage 2 -- cross-implementation parameter recovery.** Truth drawn from the numpy prior; latent paths simulated by the numpy `simulate()`; observations generated by the measurement layer (tier-1 anchors on the deployment inputs at sigma 0.02; biased series on all six inputs; capability observations on `Y_throughput` and `Y_frontier`); fitted with the JAX estimator. Cross-implementation recovery is stronger than a model fitting its own simulator: a shared transcription error cannot cancel across two codebases.

Measurement design mirrors the section 4 anchor structure rather than bypassing it: the three deployment inputs are anchored (tier-1, `b = 0` by rule), the three frontier inputs are anchor-absent, so their bias terms are **fixed at the prior mean** per section 4.2 and the `anchor_absent` list `[F1, F2, F3]` is exported with every fit -- the identification rule is exercised, not asserted.

**Recovery (config A, 90 percent central intervals):** covered -- `sigma_D`, `sigma_F`, `delta`, `sigma_top0`, all six loadings `lam`, all six anchored biases `b_anchored`. Not covered -- `rho_g` (truth 0.987, deep in the Beta(6,2) prior tail: a near-unit-root growth process that T = 20 observations cannot pin), and 2 of 12 `gstar` components. A single-truth exercise cannot distinguish honest 90 percent coverage from miscalibration at this rate; a full simulation-based-calibration sweep is a published-run obligation, not a smoke-test one.

**Behaviour audit (config C, chain means against truths):** `sigma_u` 0.057 vs truth 0.059 -- sharply learned, as an anchored noise scale should be. `sigma_D` 0.71 vs 0.77 -- learned. `delta` posterior mean ~0.01 against truth 0.286 with the posterior close to its Normal(0, 0.25) prior -- **barely learned, exactly as `IDENTIFICATION.md` section 2.1 predicts** for the mechanism parameter at short T with weak AI-intensity variation; the truth remains inside the wide interval. `rho_g` 0.93 vs 0.987 and `sigma_v` 0.027 vs 0.017 -- the persistence-versus-innovation-scale trade-off (`IDENTIFICATION.md` degeneracy family), pulled toward prior mass. The estimator's weakly identified parameters are weakly identified in precisely the directions the identification file committed to in advance, which is the behaviour a synthetic validation exists to confirm.

**Convergence discipline: NOT met at smoke scale, stated plainly.** SPECIFICATION.md section 10 requires zero divergences for a published run; every configuration here diverges at a 2--8 percent rate, and minimum ESS over the latent innovation sites is far below the published-run bar. The likely geometry is a funnel in the measurement noise scales (the synthetic truth draws `sigma_z` components arbitrarily close to zero) compounded by the near-unit-root truth. **Registered work item before any real estimation run:** reparameterise or re-prior the Block M noise scales, then demonstrate a zero-divergence synthetic fit, then the SBC sweep. Config B's trapped chain is retained in this note because it carried r_hat 5.5 with only 7 divergences -- a second demonstration (after the init pathology below) that this model can fail convergence while looking clean on the divergence count alone.

## Defects found during the build, and their fixes

Recorded because the sequence is information, per the section 10 failure-disclosure commitment.

1. **CES unit-elasticity branch under JAX.** The numpy implementation branches with `if abs(sigma - 1) < 1e-6`. A naive `jnp.where` translation propagates NaN *gradients* from the unselected power branch; the committed fix is the double-where pattern (the power form is evaluated at a safe exponent wherever the Cobb-Douglas branch is selected). Caught by a dedicated gradient test at `sigma in {0.9999, 1.0, 1.0001}`.
2. **Float32 cannot evaluate the power form near the threshold.** An initial wider branch threshold (1e-3) made the two implementations disagree by 6e-4 at `sigma = 0.9995`. Matching the numpy threshold (1e-6) requires evaluating `inner**(1/r)` at `|r|` down to 1e-6, which float32 cannot do; the module enables x64 globally, which the bit-for-bit reproducibility commitment wants anyway.
3. **Default initialisation strands chains.** Under `init_to_uniform`, the initial `xbar` can land below the current level, the saturation drag `exp(phi * (log x - log xbar))` then saturates the log-path clip, and the clip's zero gradient strands the chain at its starting point -- the first fit returned r_hat 2.7e4 with ESS 1.0 and **zero divergences**, a signature worth recording: a stuck chain can look like a clean one on the divergence count alone. Fix: `init_to_sample` (initialise from the prior).

## Registered gaps -- the specification does not commit these

Per the register-don't-invent convention (F1 Amendment 1 pattern). Both need an authored decision before real estimation; the synthetic exercise defines its own and says so.

1. **Initial-state priors for the latent inputs** at the start of an estimation window. SPECIFICATION.md defines the evolution equation and a 2026 synthetic baseline but never a prior on the window's initial latents. Synthetic-scope choice: `log x0 ~ Normal(log baseline, 0.3)`.
2. **The AI-intensity evolution process.** Section 7 names the indicators but no process. Synthetic-scope choice: the deterministic exponential from `prior_predictive.py`.
